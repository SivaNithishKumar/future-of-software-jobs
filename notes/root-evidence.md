# Root evidence and open questions

Initial verification date: 19 October 2026. Preliminary research, not final conclusions.

## Exposure is an incomplete predictor of actual adoption

[BLS working paper WP-591, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/2026/ec260040.htm), [53-page full paper](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/2026/pdf/ec260040.pdf), links 14 task/occupation exposure measures from BLS occupational microdata to Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey firm adoption. One-standard-deviation higher exposure correlates with 4–11 percentage points higher adoption probability; after year/industry fixed effects, 1–8 points. Exposure explains only a modest fraction of variation and relationships differ by size/sector. This challenges career advice based on occupation labels alone; not causal proof of adoption barriers or jobs lost. Need inspect tables, sampling and definition harmonization in full methods.

## Apparently conflicting adoption headlines often measure different things

[Census May 26 2026, AI use by businesses](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html) covers biweekly data December 14 2025–May 3 2026: 17–20% of firms use AI, 19.8% in final period, 37% of firms with 250+ staff; information sector39.7%, finance/insurance33.9%, retail14%. Nov17 2025 wording changed from use in producing goods/services to use in any business function. Thus a break in the series is partly questionnaire scope; do not compare earlier5% to later20% as pure deployment growth. The source says biweekly nationally representative firms, not all workers or every task.

[Stanford 2026 AI Index](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report) reports organizational adoption88%, a different survey/source population; don't present as directly contradictory to Census or as88% of tasks automated. Trace source selection and weighting from chapter4 before use. Same headline source still celebrates SWE-bench Verified near100%, while independent and lab audits show benchmark integrity concerns: another reason to examine methods rather than borrow authoritative summaries.

## UK projections explicitly do not adjust for unknown AI effects

[Skills England annual report2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/skills-england-annual-skills-report-and-sectoral-skills-needs-assessments-2026/skills-england-annual-skills-report-2026) lines602–604 says it has not adjusted expert projections for accelerated AI adoption because evidential basis is insufficient, with heightened uncertainty and possible digital-demand overestimation. Lines372–385 explain conflicting early-career signals and post-COVID/macro confounds. Do not treat projected digital shortages as a guaranteed unfilled market.

[UK AI Labour Market Survey2025, published Jan28 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-labour-market-survey-2025-report) full PDF still needs detailed methodological pass: sampling, definitions, hard-to-fill roles, locations and attainable entry skills. Source linked PDF was found at https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6960ef384343a0da370869b7/AI_Labour_Market_Survey_2025_report.pdf .

## Broad forecasts must not be misattributed to AI

[WEF Future of Jobs2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025), [full PDF](https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf): Jan7 2025; survey of1000+ employers/14m workers/55 economies; extrapolates170m created/92m displaced/net78m through2030 across technology, demographics, green transition, geopolitics and macro. Full figure denominator is1.2bn formal jobs in study dataset, not every world worker; this is employer expectations extrapolation, not observed flows or an AI causal prediction.

## Skill premiums are not treatment effects

[IMF Jan9 2026 Staff Discussion Note](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/staff-discussion-notes/issues/2026/01/09/bridging-skill-gaps-for-the-future-new-jobs-creation-in-the-ai-age-572136), [49-page full PDF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/sdn/2026/english/sdnea2026001.pdf) and Jan14 summary identify wage premiums/new skill demand with uneven employment effects. Need inspect identification, Lightcast occupation/geography weighting, new-skill definitions, robustness and exact5year3.6% versus6.3% differences before using any number. Raw summary and PDF snippets differ, so no settled statistic yet.

## Macro evidence needing full methods

[Humlum/Vestergaard NBER33777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777) full page403 through browser; author Brookings summary in labor.md provides access and warns records only through2024. [NBER33509, Artificial Intelligence and the Labor Market](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33509) page403; search abstract says task substitution offset by productivity-driven firm labor demand, but methods and observation period need retrieval through official author/publication route before it can support conclusions. [Acemoglu simple macroeconomics2024](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487) models task exposure x cost savings; outdated capability assumptions mean it is a framework/competing scenario, not a bound on2031.

## Provisional cross-field hypotheses to attack

H1: As generating candidate code/actions/content becomes cheaper, measurement/validation and bottleneck resolution become relatively valuable. Rival: validation is also automated and absorbed into platforms; remaining work may be too scarce or low-margin to sustain many jobs.

H2: Broad ML+systems+robotics expertise is valuable at domain boundaries. Rival: selected employers demand deep specialization/credentials; shallow breadth fails technical interviews and onsite constraints block accessible roles.

H3: Physical sectors provide more durable work. Rival: capital intensity and procurement favor incumbents, economic constraints delay deployment, and greater physical need does not establish funded software demand.

H4: Workflow/data/customer access can support entrepreneurial value. Rival: integration work scales as consulting, incumbents own distribution/data, and ubiquitous AI lets customers build substitutes.

H5: Preparing around common foundations preserves options. Rival: breadth spreads learning too thin; the right evidence may favor one deep technical subfield plus a commercial domain. Need examples of skill transfer and opportunity-cost estimates, not slogans.

Each hypothesis needs disconfirming evidence and practical tests before final ranking.
