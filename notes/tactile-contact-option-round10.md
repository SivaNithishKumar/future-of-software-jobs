# Tactile sensing and contact-state estimation: bounded option audit, round 10

Draft, checked 19 October 2026 UTC. Existing robotics, neuro/assistive, interfaces catalogues and `source-index.json` were searched first; no dedicated tactile-sensor/contact-state branch was present. This pass adds that missing option, rather than repeating vision/VLA generalization. Three current papers were examined, with public source inspection and one older, explicitly licensed data fallback. No code execution, installation, model/data download, hardware action, access-gate acceptance, submission or outreach occurred.

## Decision

Tactile work offers a credible software entry through calibration records, sensor-data pipelines, trajectory-aware evaluation and runtime integration. A software-only portfolio can test force/contact prediction on recorded data. It cannot establish stable grasping, safe force control, robustness after a skin replacement, or a commercially useful robot. The attractive 2031 inference is a specialist integration route beside general robot policies; the evidence does not establish a large accessible market for independently building another tactile foundation model.

The key distinction is between an external camera and a **vision-based tactile sensor**: the latter has its own camera observing a deformable contact surface. Running an image model on tactile images is already using physical touch. A tactile-image dataset without external-camera streams cannot answer whether an external-camera-only robot is sufficient. Similarly, feeding a force transducer's values into a force-prediction baseline is an oracle comparison, not an alternative learned force sensor.

## Three current primary papers

**TacVerse.** Lan Wei and eight coauthors; [arXiv 2606.25877v1, 24 June 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25877v1); journal title *TacVerse: A Multisensor Dataset and Benchmark for Cross-Sensor Vision-Based Tactile Perception*, [DOI 10.1002/adrr.70169, first published 25 September 2026](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adrr.70169). It covers 106,800 images/seven sensor platforms, with shape, grating and force tasks. Force collection uses a 2-mm-radius spherical indenter and a six-axis reference transducer. The described protocol groups complete trials/trajectories into chronological 60/20/20 splits, with SSIM deduplication before splitting. Force RMSE rises from 0.186 within one GelSight variant to 0.348/0.976/1.277 on three other sensor platforms. This tests platform shift under a controlled stimulus, not held-out sensor serial units, arbitrary objects, wear cycles or a new force regime. Shared shape classes are not unseen-object-category evaluation. The reported few-shot protocol starts from a source-trained model and uses target validation. The actual public source differs in consequential ways described below.

**FTP-1.** Chengbo Yuan and 16 coauthors; [2606.13102v3, 13 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13102v3), first submitted 11 June. This is an action-generating policy with morphology-aware tactile tokens and sensor-specific encoders, rather than only contact classification. Unseen-sensor transfer includes training a new encoder using target-task demonstrations; it is not zero-shot hardware replacement. Evaluation uses 100 simulation episodes or 20 real trials per task. On three unseen-sensor tasks the reported average is 46.6%, versus 15% for the architecture without tactile policy pretraining. Its newer matched-trajectory VLA baseline reaches 57.0% simulation success versus FTP-1's 66.7%, supporting a benefit beyond trajectory exposure. Pretraining uses 48 H20 GPUs; downstream tuning uses eight A800s. These are author-reported lab experiments with limited denominators, not commercial uptime. Exact force calibration, unit-replacement endurance and wear-triggered recalibration were not established in the inspected report.

**SimpleTouch.** Chen Yang and nine coauthors; [2610.02784v1, 2 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02784v1). With 50 task demonstrations, it reports 77.5% across six UniVTAC tasks and 71.3% over four physical tasks, using 100/20 evaluation episodes respectively. This is contrary evidence to a universal need for additional tactile **policy** pretraining, but it retains pretrained π0.5 and T3 tactile representations. Crucially, the main insertion comparison uses 25 executed actions for SimpleTouch versus 50 for the vision baseline; matched-policy horizon analysis reports 27/12-point insertion gains from faster feedback. That makes sensing frequency an important rival explanation, not a reason to discard the study. Its deployed action pathway omits future-latent generation; future-touch prediction is training supervision. Eight H20s train the model; reported inference uses an A100 and excludes robot/simulator execution. The release statement says complete code/checkpoints are forthcoming; the project page could not be retrieved, so a runnable public reproduction was not verified.

Together these establish contact-information value on selected tasks and credible competing integration methods. They do not show that every tactile input helps: adding a poorly integrated branch can lose to the vision baseline. A calibrated wrist-force/impedance controller remains a necessary engineering comparator for a real force-control claim; none of the recorded-data experiments below can establish superiority to a tuned physical controller.

Five actual files were read, with blob identities retained in the companion records:

Deduplication could also have occurred upstream.
- [`force_regression.py`, lines 319–367](https://github.com/LannWei/Tactile_Database/blob/abe33c1e896d9529f0798cc75e36a63da9753e79/force_regression.py#L319) samples target **images**, concatenates them with all other sensors' source training rows and uses source validation. [`torch_common.py`, lines 1298–1308](https://github.com/LannWei/Tactile_Database/blob/abe33c1e896d9529f0798cc75e36a63da9753e79/torch_common.py#L1298) creates a new model/optimizer for that experiment. This is not an explicit resume-and-fine-tune of the fixed source model with target-validation selection described by the paper. Do not present the default command as an exact published-protocol replication.
- Counterevidence to a generic leakage accusation: regression target normalization is fitted on training rows, then inverted for scoring; MAE pretraining collection explicitly requests training-only paths. The latter inherits the same unresolved sample-ID grouping. The helper returns MAE/RMSE/Pearson, not the paper's complete MSE/R² table. [Normalizer/model training, lines 1231–1354](https://github.com/LannWei/Tactile_Database/blob/abe33c1e896d9529f0798cc75e36a63da9753e79/torch_common.py#L1231), [pretraining paths, lines 658–735](https://github.com/LannWei/Tactile_Database/blob/abe33c1e896d9529f0798cc75e36a63da9753e79/torch_common.py#L658), [metric helper, lines 95–129](https://github.com/LannWei/Tactile_Database/blob/abe33c1e896d9529f0798cc75e36a63da9753e79/common.py#L95).
- Runtime is also a gate: the release supports CUDA and Apple MPS but explicitly disables CPU training. Its training routine pre-caches selected train, validation and test image bytes in RAM; a 24.7-GB data listing is not a reliable estimate of a small laptop run. Train/test image caching itself does not train on test labels, so it should not be mislabeled leakage. [Device resolver, lines 590–617](https://github.com/LannWei/Tactile_Database/blob/abe33c1e896d9529f0798cc75e36a63da9753e79/torch_common.py#L590), [prefetch, lines 1246–1256](https://github.com/LannWei/Tactile_Database/blob/abe33c1e896d9529f0798cc75e36a63da9753e79/torch_common.py#L1246).

The [public batch evaluator](https://github.com/michaelyuancb/ftp1-policy/blob/89fa681d6c014cce28300946b7526db808e0b1c1/UniVTAC/scripts/shell/eval_ftp1_batch.sh#L18) defaults to 100 episodes, one worker and a 20-action execution prefix, with six task-specific checkpoints and author-local checkpoint paths. It logs exit codes and fails the overall batch if a task process fails. Its [metadata summarizer](https://github.com/michaelyuancb/ftp1-policy/blob/89fa681d6c014cce28300946b7526db808e0b1c1/UniVTAC/scripts/eval_result_utils.py#L59) includes `success`, `failed` and `error` in episode counts; unknown result categories are reported separately. Resume handling can rerun abnormal episodes, so an extension must preserve every attempt rather than count only the eventual episode record. Those are source contracts, not evidence that the published experiment used a particular retry pattern or that a separate `until_success` utility produced the headline result.

A [public reproduction issue](https://github.com/michaelyuancb/ftp1-policy/issues/1) reports a training gap, but its author used multi-task co-training. The maintainer explicitly says released checkpoints are **per-task** tuned using the first 50 demonstrations and warns about action-slot alignment. This supplies a concrete rival explanation; it does not establish an independently confirmed failed reproduction. No outreach or issue creation occurred.

## A more accessible, older public-data fallback

The archived Sparsh source is pinned to [`fee6a05a97330eca014b59c71ac41596fe3974a7`](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sparsh/tree/fee6a05a97330eca014b59c71ac41596fe3974a7), last code commit 27 February 2025; GitHub marks it archived on 1 April 2026. Its source [license is CC BY-NC 4.0](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sparsh/blob/fee6a05a97330eca014b59c71ac41596fe3974a7/LICENSE.md), not a permissive commercial code license. This is an artifact fallback; a fourth paper survey was not performed.

[DIGIT force data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/digit-force-estimation) and [GelSight force data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/gelsight-force-estimation) advertise CC BY-NC 4.0. They contain three indenter geometries, reference force and trajectory-associated slip labels; published normal-load limits differ, 5 N versus 3 N. Slip labels are inferred from a friction-cone method, rather than independently observed object-drop labels. Displayed full sizes are 12.4 GB and 52.8 GB. The GelSight data revision is [`136db55485b6501605b1d2648ce0fe44740e302e`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/gelsight-force-estimation/commit/136db55485b6501605b1d2648ce0fe44740e302e), 22 October 2024. Its public LFS metadata lists `flat/batch_1/dataset_gelsight_00.pkl` as 65,405,631 bytes and `dataset_slip_forces.pkl` as 1,197,081 bytes, making a bounded first-fixture selection possible without fetching the entire release. These are declared metadata, not verified downloads.

The actual [force/slip loader, lines 64–160](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sparsh/blob/fee6a05a97330eca014b59c71ac41596fe3974a7/tactile_ssl/data/vision_based_forces_slip_probes.py#L64), exposes trajectory membership, scales/clips forces, and marks a target positive when slip occurs anywhere in a forward label window. Features and target must be distinguished: a future-window target is forecasting supervision, not proof of detection lead time or recovery. A saturated normalized target also cannot distinguish forces beyond its clipping bound. Keep original reference units/values for evaluation. A force-derived slip rule cannot serve as an independent physical-success oracle when evaluating a predictor that uses the same force values.

## Concrete 30/90-day entry experiment

**One project: a contact-state estimator with an honest calibration and shift contract.** Budget 40 hours for 30 days and 120 total for 90 days. No new sensor is required for recorded-data work. The current pass only establishes a candidate; launch is conditional on rights, approved data/runtime access and one actual feature/label fixture.

First eight hours: select the noncommercial research fallback or resolve TacVerse's contact-sharing and code-rights gates without inferring permission. Freeze revisions and file hashes, manifest the minimum image/label shards, calculate compressed/unpacked/RAM requirements, and inspect one real timestamp/image/force/trajectory association. Do not count a fictional schema example as that fixture. If no viable fixture or reusable trial identifiers exist after eight hours, narrow to a source/protocol audit and stop promising a benchmark result. No contact-sharing gate was accepted during research.

For the remaining first month, choose one sensor and force estimation as the primary endpoint. Compare a training-mean baseline, background-difference contact area/centroid plus ridge regression, and one small CNN or approved frozen encoder/probe. Keep physical force units, per-axis RMSE/MAE, residuals by force bin and a prediction-interval calibration curve. Treat sensor-specific startup-background adaptation as a separate condition from source-only calibration. Reserve complete trajectories/batches before fitting; with three indenter geometries, reserve one geometry and report that distribution shift separately. Every test trajectory must appear exactly once; all exclusions and clipping counts must be explicit. Report CPU/accelerator latency and memory for the actual selected path, without asserting a control-loop rate.

Acceptance is a valid benchmark contract, not a mandatory neural-model win: zero split-crossing trajectory IDs, zero training-fitted statistics from held-out rows, no missing example/label associations, no unreported saturated targets and uncertainty evaluated on untouched trajectories. A learned-model improvement is accepted only if its paired, whole-trajectory bootstrap interval beats the selected simple baseline; otherwise preserve the negative result. Choose any force-error requirement before test inspection, using the intended application tolerance. There is no defensible universal newton threshold for an unspecified robot/object.

In 90 days, extend to the second sensor family only after its first fixture/rights/resource gates pass. Match overlapping observed force support, hold out a complete indenter/batch, repeat at least three probe-training seeds and calibrate abstention on validation data. Publish or retain a transparent split/coverage/compute report according to rights and user authorization. Test synthetic blur, lighting or gain perturbations as synthetic faults; do not call them physical gel wear. If serial units, acquisition sessions, gel replacements or cycle counts are absent, explicitly stop at **sensor-family transfer**, not device-replacement or service-life robustness. Contact/slip analysis may be secondary, but preserve causal feature prefixes and distinguish friction-cone proxy targets from independent slip/drop measurements.

What actually needs a lab: gel/sensor replacement and wear experiments; synchronized external camera, wrist-force and tactile baselines; reference-transducer calibration; friction/force sweeps on fragile or unfamiliar objects; and controlled robot trials with a tuned impedance/force controller. A simulator can test interface logic and modeled contact, but cannot validate unmodeled elastomer wear, contamination or physical damage. Physical execution would require separate authorized equipment, supervision and task-specific limits; no such work is included here.
