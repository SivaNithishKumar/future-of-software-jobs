## Bounded observed evidence — P40-P

V1 reports a 256-A100 Oracle Phoenix cluster, with SRP/EPRI and other partners. Two May 2025 utility-shaped events met a reported 25% reduction versus average baseline for three hours, with 15-minute ramps and return below baseline. CAISO was a historical emergency reenactment, not contemporaneous California dispatch; its displayed power used five-minute averages. Modifiable workloads were batch training/fine-tuning/inference; strict Flex0 serving was untouched. Flex1/2/3 tolerated 10/25/50% average-throughput reduction across 3–6 hours. Power came from NVIDIA-SMI; throughput from custom scripts. The simulator used advance job profiles; Amperon supplied forecast and historical grid signals. Policies combined DVFS, checkpoint/pause and GPU reallocation. Fair DVFS had the best reported average throughput; mixed DVFS/pause balanced throughput and jobs affected. Authors acknowledge single-cluster scope and further market testing. Their checkpoint-overhead argument depends on infrequent events and long jobs. [Primary methods, sections 2–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00909v1).

This is meaningful evidence against the assertion that workload flexibility is only a simulator idea. It does not supply the following stronger claims. Those are unresolved questions, not accusations of defects or misconduct:

- **Delivery boundary:** a GPU-derived series is insufficient by itself to certify facility-meter MW, UPS/cooling/network overhead, a system-wide capacity contribution, or the same response at a different site. Absolute curtailed MW is unverified here; multiplying GPU nameplate watts by device count would not repair that boundary.
- **Service contract:** an average-throughput allowance cannot substitute for per-request intended-arrival deadlines, TTFT/TPOT, semantic correctness, cancellation/error accounting or strict interactive availability. A report can pass its declared batch contract while failing an unrelated interactive contract. Preserving protected work is a useful result; changing that protected work's power is a separate experiment.
- **Information time:** forecast target time, first publication, revision, controller receipt and actual command time must join. Historical values can legitimately verify a completed event; they must not silently become the forecast available to an earlier decision. A pre-announced event window is a different experiment from surprise activation. No archived decision-time receipt chain was verified in this pass, and no future-information misuse is alleged.
- **Counterfactual and recovery:** a percentage against a chosen baseline is not avoided energy or carbon. Define the baseline under the same offered work, quality and hardware, then account for deferred work, retries, failures and the drain beyond the event. A short recovery window can miss later catch-up or slower completion. Financial settlement, realized payment, independently accepted utility delivery and customer net benefit remain unverified.
- **Reliability and generalization:** selected successful events do not identify availability under arbitrary event timing, changing workload mixes, an unavailable controller, network loss, stale telemetry or long sequences of events. A trial result is not an interconnection permission or a national headroom estimate.
