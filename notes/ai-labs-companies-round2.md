# AI, ML and compute: company, institution and contribution map — round 2

Checked 19 October 2026. Bounded research pass for the ongoing 24-hour programme; not an exhaustive institution census or final career ranking. The earlier software productivity scan is intentionally not repeated. This pass inspected public team, programme, job, publication, model-card and repository pages. It did not contact anyone, apply for anything, install software, download model weights, enter private systems or execute the proposed projects.

“Observed” below means the public source or artefact was inspected, not that its performance or a company's commercial claims were independently verified. Current postings can expire. Neither a job ad nor a grant announcement establishes the number of people who will be hired in 2031.

## How to use this directory

Choose a technical branch first, then an institution or contribution route. A lab's reputation is not a substitute for matching its actual work and entry requirements. For the reader, the most adjacent branches appear to be applied ML systems, data/evaluation and production inference; foundation-model research and embodied learning require additional proof of depth. Current mastery and eligibility remain unassessed.

The named researchers are public professional discovery pointers from official lab pages or papers, not recommendations to approach them privately. Official team, careers, community and contact pages are provided for research. No outreach is authorized or performed.

## Technical branch key

- **A — Applied model systems:** useful agents, integrations, voice/document workflows and customer deployment.
- **B — Inference and hardware/software systems:** serving, scheduling, memory, kernels, compilers and performance validation.
- **C — Evaluation, data and reliability:** benchmark science, multilingual evaluation, data curation, observability and model behaviour.
- **D — Learning algorithms and open training:** pre/post-training, optimization, data mixtures, architectures and experimental research.
- **E — Embodied-model intersection:** multimodal representation, action learning, dataset/tooling and simulated/physical evaluation. Field deployment economics belongs to the robotics track.

Project identifiers P1–P6 below link the directory to concrete 30/90-day work. They are proposals, not completed work or promises of hiring eligibility.

## Institutional access: important routes and limits

**IndiaAI compute.** The [official portal](https://www.compute.indiaai.gov.in/login) exposes researchers/academia, student, fellowship, startup/MSME, early-stage and government categories with verification documents. Published researcher thresholds include h-index/publication/citation alternatives; other categories have different criteria. Registration approval precedes compute requests and subsidy proposals. Its FAQ says requests under 5,000 GPU hours are automatically approved **after the required access process**; that is not automatic eligibility, no-cost unrestricted compute or confirmed allocation for this user. Supplier capacity, wait times and actual delivered performance were not tested. Do not upload personal or employer documents without a later explicit request.

**UK public compute.** The [UK Compute Roadmap, updated 23 April 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-compute-roadmap/uk-compute-roadmap), commits future investment and allocation reform. The [AIRR portal](https://portal-airr.isambard.ac.uk/) is an official starting point; exact current research-allocation criteria were not fully audited. A national expansion target is not equivalent to a personal GPU allocation. University affiliation, supported project, security, export controls and programme-specific conditions can matter; which apply here remains unknown.

**MSR programmes are not interchangeable.** The India Research Fellows employment-style 1–2-year programme above explicitly excludes PhD students/graduates. In contrast, the [2026 Microsoft Research Fellowship](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/2026-microsoft-research-fellowship/) requires active PhD enrollment for students, includes India and UK/Europe among eligible regions, pays through institutions rather than directly to individuals, and its 2026 disbursement period has already passed. Neither is a generic grant for an unaffiliated developer.

**Degree decisions.** Research-scientist ads with first-author publication and pretraining requirements give real evidence for a research-depth route. Engineering career pages that accept varied backgrounds give real evidence for an engineering route. These should not be collapsed into either “you need a PhD for AI” or “a portfolio always replaces a degree.” For a working developer, a targeted research-engineering collaboration can test appetite and fit before a multi-year degree decision. Programme admissions, cost, supervisors and geographic eligibility require separate evaluation.

## Six concrete contribution projects

All projects begin with a written protocol, existing approved resources and a budget. New package/model downloads, paid compute, physical hardware, submissions, applications and messages are **not** authorized by these research notes. Tests should reveal when a simple baseline wins.

### P1 — Indic speech/document workflow with trustworthy evaluation

**Fits:** AI4Bharat, Vaani/IISc, Sarvam, BharatGen, Cohere community and applied enterprise teams. Foundations: speech/NLP metrics, data splitting, Python/SQL, error analysis and domain understanding.

**30 days:** define one narrow task, such as extracting correctly grounded fields from noisy multilingual voice notes. Build a strong conventional/model baseline and a 200–500 example evaluation plan stratified by language, speaker/district, noise and domain. Start with public permitted data; record what is transcribed versus raw. Measure field/task correctness and abstention, not WER alone.

**90 days:** reproduce the evaluation, address one systematic failure and test on held-out speakers/domain data. Show accuracy/coverage, subgroup denominators, human correction time, latency and cost. Have a competent language/domain reviewer inspect ambiguous cases if later available and authorized. The proof is a reproducible benchmark plus an integration that changes an outcome, not the number of supported languages on a banner.

### P2 — Inference performance at matched correctness and latency

**Fits:** Sarvam inference/kernels, AMD/Arm/NVIDIA ecosystems, vLLM/SGLang, Baseten and Fireworks. Foundations: C++/Python, accelerator/CPU architecture, profiling, cache/memory and distributed systems.

**30 days:** choose one already-available supported model/device and fixed trace. Sweep context length, concurrency and request mix. Measure TTFT, completion/TPOT, p95/p99 latency, memory, utilization and accepted-task quality. Explain a bottleneck before changing code. Use a small CPU/kernel slice if GPU access is absent.

**90 days:** one source-level fix, portability test or scheduler/cache optimization with regression coverage, a matched-budget baseline and a losing regime. Demonstrate repeatability and full cost. This can support an upstream-ready proposal; it does not establish the multi-node 100B-model/on-call experience demanded by a senior Sarvam ad.

### P3 — Independent evaluation with a tested scorer

**Fits:** AISI/Inspect, EleutherAI, BharatGen evals, Fireworks eval platform and frontier-lab behaviour teams. Foundations: statistics, software testing, task/oracle design and interpretable traces.

**30 days:** implement or specify25–50 high-quality cases in one meaningful domain. Keep actor-visible and held-out evidence separate. Include correct-but-unusual outputs, incomplete results, unauthorized actions, tool failures and judge failures. Pin versions; test the scorer against deliberately wrong and correct outputs.

**90 days:** expand representative coverage; run repeated trials at matched budgets, report uncertainty and maintain a failure taxonomy. Replicate an upstream task result and explain discrepancies. A draft contribution can be prepared locally; public submission is a separate action. Useful output includes a negative result showing why a proposed scorer is invalid.

### P4 — Model specialization or routing with an honest business denominator

**Fits:** Sarvam, Mistral, Baseten, Fireworks, Ai2/Qwen artefact users. Foundations: train/validation separation, distillation/adaptation, calibration, system costs and production requirements.

**30 days:** compare a simple rule or small model, a larger model and a transparent router on one constrained workflow. Include fallback, retries and review in the cost. Do not tune on the final evaluation or assume synthetic training labels are ground truth.

**90 days:** demonstrate a Pareto improvement that survives held-out users/time and distribution shift. Quantify net value per eligible case and quality at each escalation coverage. If routing saves tokens while increasing correction cost, report that failure. An employer can assess this work without believing a universal “small models win” thesis.

### P5 — Embodied-data and model tooling before a hardware purchase

**Fits:** Hugging Face/LeRobot, NVIDIA GEAR, Ai2 embodied research, BOLD and robotics-lab collaborations. Foundations: coordinate frames, timestamps, control/ML interfaces, imitation learning and evaluation.

**30 days:** inspect a permitted existing episode dataset; audit synchronization, units, missing actions and train/test leakage across scenes or demonstrations. Reproduce a small offline or simulated policy evaluation if resources exist; label it precisely.

**90 days:** a reproducible dataset validator, simulator adapter, inference-timing improvement or narrow generalization experiment. Physical claims require actual robot trials with randomized starts/objects and failure accounting, approved separately. A 50-episode fine-tuning suggestion in a tutorial is not evidence of operational reliability.

### P6 — Small, falsifiable learning-algorithm research

**Fits:** BOLD/SOFAIR, MSR, DeepMind research engineering, Ai2 and open-science groups. Foundations: probability/optimization, strong implementation, experimental design, relevant literature and ability to explain negative results.

**30 days:** pick a tractable claim from a paper and one small mechanism, such as an evaluation-guided search rule, data-mixture intervention or learning stability change. Specify baselines, compute budget, seeds, measurable outcomes and a falsifier before results.

**90 days:** reproduce the baseline, run ablations and test a held-out setting. Provide code/configs/results with uncertainty and failure cases. A narrowly novel, reproducible result is more persuasive than claiming to reproduce an entire proprietary system. A research degree may be appropriate if this type of work is rewarding and required by the target role; the experiment is a low-commitment way to test that preference.

## Comparison by incentives: where not to fool ourselves

**Commercial frontier labs** can offer scale, users and compute but can change priorities quickly and keep key artefacts private. Research publication alone does not make a method independently reproducible. **Inference startups** live or die on workload economics and customer operations; fundraising/run-rate numbers do not establish margin or durable defensibility. **Hardware vendors** benefit when tools increase use of their platforms, so benchmarks must expose device-specific assumptions. **University/public labs** can pursue open methods and longer questions but have grant windows, fixed-term posts, student/fee rules and procurement constraints. **Open communities** provide inspectable contribution routes, but unpaid participation is not a guaranteed job, compute grant or career ladder.

Native-platform competition is a concrete counterargument to a standalone infrastructure startup: the energy track identified [NVIDIA Mission Control power-aware scheduling](https://docs.nvidia.com/mission-control/docs/systems-administration-guide/latest/prs/introduction.html). A thin generic scheduler or evaluation wrapper may be absorbed by a platform. Differentiation should survive the question: what deployment constraint, evidence or integration does the native vendor still fail to solve, and who pays to fix it?
