# Education, tutoring, assessment and adult technical learning — round 5

Checked 19 October 2026. This pass goes beyond the Turkish-mathematics PNAS example in `sectors.md` and the coding-skills RCT already audited in the software/labor notes. Those studies are cross-references, not new independent evidence here. No learners were enrolled, contacted or assessed; no private educational records, dataset downloads, installations or paid model calls were used. Public primary-paper PDFs were read where needed.

## Working conclusion

“AI does the work” and “AI helps a person acquire a skill” are different products. The checked evidence supports real benefits in particular designs, including some immediate unaided tests, support for human tutors and teacher preparation. It also shows why higher task completion, pleasant conversations and cheaper inference cannot establish durable independent expertise or a profitable education business.

The strongest actionable direction for the reader is to combine conventional software/data skills with **credible assessment, curriculum-aware feedback and an actual educator workflow**, while using those same measurement principles for his own learning. This is a hypothesis to test, not a field ranking. Established retrieval practice, worked examples, formative assessment, adaptive problem banks and human teaching remain serious alternatives.

## Evidence map: what changed, what was measured, and what remains open

### EL01 — Harvard structured physics tutor: a positive active-learning comparison, narrow duration

Kestin et al., *Scientific Reports*, 3 June 2025, study two lessons in a Harvard undergraduate physics course. Of 233 enrolled students, 194 meet consent/participation/complete-assessment eligibility. The crossover keeps regular peer groups together and compares a specifically scaffolded, self-paced tutor at home with in-class active learning. Outcomes are pre/post lesson quizzes and experience reports. This is stronger than comparing an AI tool with no instruction, but the intervention bundles individual pacing, feedback, software sequencing and learning environment. It does not establish semester-long replacement, delayed retention or engineering-job transfer. [Primary paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6), [institutional publication page](https://serl.fas.harvard.edu/publication/ai-tutoring-outperforms-class-active-learning-rct-introducing-novel-research-based).

The paper describes a platform that controls multipart problem order because prompting alone did not reliably provide that structure. This matters for developers: instructional state and content design are part of the treatment. Direct publisher PDF retrieval failed; PMC required a browser check. Detailed primary indexed text and the official article/data pointer were inspected, but a full supplement reanalysis was not performed. Its linked Stata-data repository has no visible licence in the retrieved tree; public visibility must not be treated as permission for arbitrary reuse. [Data pointer](https://github.com/HarvardAItutor/Study-Data-v4).

### EL02 — Tutor CoPilot: helping human tutors, not removing them

Wang et al.'s latest version is **v3, 10 October 2026**. Its main text describes >700 tutors and ~1,000 students, whereas older abstracts/versions advertise 900/1,800; use version-specific denominators. Tutors are randomised, students can encounter tutors from both arms, and the principal outcome is session-level exit-ticket mastery: about +4 percentage points overall and larger benefits among lower-rated tutors. The protocol says tutors cannot help during exit tickets, so this is immediate independent assessment—not merely assisted practice. It is not a directly identified long-run MAP-test effect. Appendix B extrapolates from an earlier observational exit-ticket/MAP association. [Latest primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03017v3), [version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017).

Appendix N's approximately **$20/tutor/year** comes from $1,419.66 of API usage across 429 treatment tutors over two months, annualised. It does not price tutors' labour, curriculum, software, training, school procurement or operations. The implementation reports a >30-second early response problem traced to data retrieval, illustrating a conventional performance bottleneck. Provider ratings identify tutor heterogeneity, not an immutable student trait. Raw school conversations are not presumed public or available for the reader's project.

### EL03 — Nigeria: independent paper tests and real support costs, not “two years retained in six weeks”

World Bank Working Paper 11125 evaluates a teacher-guided after-school Copilot programme in nine schools with computer labs, June–July 2024. Interested students were randomised: 657 treatment/671 control; 422/337 completed the final assessment. The primary English effect is approximately 0.238 SD; the often-quoted 0.31 SD combines English, AI knowledge and digital skills. A separately administered school-term English exam also improves (0.206 SD). The assessment is monitored pencil-and-paper. [Full primary paper](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/2f996675-b769-49e5-82a8-b15cb79b6b20/download), [official reproducibility record, updated 16 October 2026](https://reproducibility.worldbank.org/index.php/catalog/419).

Important qualifications: assessment completion is ~64% versus ~50%; the paper provides Lee-bound/IPW sensitivity analyses with assumptions, and positive English/total lower bounds should be acknowledged. There is no equal-time non-AI after-school arm to isolate the chatbot from additional time/teacher support. Stronger effects for initially better-prepared students contrast with simplistic claims that every system most benefits the weakest learner.

The pilot budget is about **$48 per assigned participant**, excluding electricity and school infrastructure; hardware is prorated. Table 13 includes content, training, stipends, equipment, management and data/support. “1.5–2 years of schooling” is an effect-size conversion against business-as-usual learning, not measured two-year retention. Longer-program gains and lifetime wage/benefit ratios are extrapolations, not outcomes observed in this six-week trial. The full PDF was extracted; its costs and attrition sections were read. No student data was accessed.

### EL04 — StudentBench: actual equivalence testing, immediate adult GRE outcomes

Northcutt et al., **v2, 30 September 2026**, report 2,383 paid adult participants and 2,469 sessions. They compare one hour of AI tutoring, live human tutoring and an unrelated-video control, with counterbalanced new GRE-style tests and no post-test access for tutors. The combined AI–human difference is −0.58 percentage points, 90% CI [−2.18, +1.03], inside a ±0.25-SD margin; the pooled TOST p=.015 is an equivalence test, not merely failure to detect a difference. Equivalence is not established for Verbal separately. [Primary methods and appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28470v2).

Limits materially change the headline: no solo-GRE-practice arm; immediate outcomes only; screened English-speaking adults with platform access; human sessions cluster heavily (1.59 effective degrees of freedom in the pooled comparison), with sensitivity checks and model dependence. The “best model in five domains” display selects different maxima; individual equivalence tests are not corrected across tutors. Expert-rated pedagogical quality is not the same endpoint as learning.

The **918× cheaper** ratio uses AI inference versus a $75/hour reference human rate. It is not a full product delivery-cost comparison. Teacher wages, curriculum/assessment design, recruitment, support, devices, governance and retention measurement are absent from that price ratio. Code is MIT; the released dataset card says CC BY 4.0, identifies privacy masking and explains which historical/private processes cannot be rerun. No dataset was downloaded. [Code](https://github.com/handshake-ai-research/studentbench), [data/licence/limits](https://huggingface.co/datasets/handshake-ai-research/studentbench).

### EL05 — A positive adult retention counterexample becomes less precise in the latest version

Contractor/Reyes, *Experimental Evidence on the Learning Impact of Generative AI*, is a proctored in-person undergraduate experiment on unfamiliar topics and analytical writing; both assessments prohibit resources. This is a genuine counterexample to “AI learning gains always vanish when removed.” However, use **v2, 4 October 2026**. Visually checked PDF Table 4 reports immediate ITT **0.285 SD (SE .123)** and one-week **0.213 SD (SE .116)**; the delayed fraction-correct effect is 3.9 points, p=.068. The positive delayed estimate is uncertain at 5%, rather than proof of no effect. [Latest primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849v2), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08849v2).

Earlier v1 has different numbers and stronger delayed significance. Its HTML, and parts of v2 HTML, corrupt numeric macros (including impossible percentages), so this pass used the latest downloaded PDF and viewed Table 4 on page 39. “Augmentation” versus “automation” use is self-selected: its heterogeneous associations do not randomise prompting strategy. One-week unfamiliar-topic recall/essays do not establish multi-year technical expertise. The paper also reports rule violations in the first assessment and sensitivity analyses; proctored does not mean perfectly enforced.

### EL06 — Seven weeks of practice: structure helps, but adaptivity's incremental value is unresolved

Akgun/Toker's June 2026 preprint analyses 89 applied-statistics undergraduates after exclusions. Everyone first receives the same AI pretesting/instruction; only subsequent practice differs: adaptive retrieval, fixed retrieval or learner-directed AI. The adaptive group outperforms learner-directed study on the final near-transfer test, but not fixed retrieval (adjusted p=.830); fixed versus learner-directed post-test difference does not reach its adjusted 5% threshold (p=.071). [Primary study](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22328v1).

It therefore does not isolate AI pretesting's benefit, prove adaptation beats a fixed schedule, or compare against non-AI retrieval. Seven weeks includes continued scheduled practice, not seven weeks with support withdrawn. Allocation/exclusion detail, an explicit model/version, public data/code and assessment-access controls are insufficiently specified in the short text for a full replication. Treat it as limited supportive evidence for practice structure, not a settled superiority claim.

### EL07 — Adult voice tutoring: engagement, mastery and exam transfer differ

Yang/Van Alstyne/Dellarocas's September 2026 MBA preprint randomises 86 students to a structured tutor or holdout where consumer AI remains available. The preregistered complete-case analysis includes 46 students after completion/quality screens. A different instructor's final has all 86 records and supports the same direction, but it is a take-home final and not a long-term locked-down retention test. The authors say the study tutor is absent from outcomes; absence of that tutor is not proof other resources were unavailable. Short-answer scoring uses model panels with a human-expert pass still pending. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23958v1).

Voice increases interaction and costs more without a corresponding demonstrated mastery advantage over text. Its within-student modality sequence later depends partly on completion history; the authors report alternative parity coding. This is informative for a product decision, but a preference for voice is not proof voice teaches better. The higher-order exam signal rests on two items and fails the stated multiplicity rule. Keep it provisional rather than ignore the positive direction.

## Strong non-LLM alternatives and what they actually test

**Retrieval practice:** Roediger/Karpicke's 2006 experiments compare testing with restudy, including 2-day/1-week retention. Restudy wins immediately, retrieval later; confidence can point in the wrong direction. Karpicke/Blunt's 2011 study extends the benefit to conceptual/inference questions compared with elaborative concept mapping. Neither study establishes that every technical skill should be learned unaided, or that retrieval replaces instruction. They justify an inexpensive serious baseline. [2006 primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/), [2011 primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/), [author-hosted 2011 paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf).

**ASSISTments:** the 2016 cluster RCT involves 43 Maine schools/2,850 seventh-graders and combines feedback/hints, teacher reports and professional development. Its year-end standardized-test effect is 0.18 SD, with larger benefits among lower prior achievers. The software alone is not the isolated treatment; students already have take-home laptops and teachers receive training. This is a mature formative-assessment/workflow alternative, not a generic chatbot. [Primary paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2332858416673968), [funder's design record](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/efficacy-study-online-mathematics-homework-support-evaluation-assistments-formative-assessment-and).

**Mindspark:** the 2019 AER publication's lottery-based Delhi programme reports 0.37-SD maths/0.23-SD Hindi gains over 4.5 months. Earlier working-paper numbers differ slightly; preserve versions. It bundles adaptive computer work with after-school provision, so it is not an isolated algorithm effect. A separate nine-month Rajasthan personalization experiment compares full adaptation with grade-level software: no meaningful average difference, but benefit for the lowest initial quartile. Adaptivity is not automatically valuable for everyone. [AER record/replication pointer](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20171112), [research-partner record of the separate component experiment](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impacts-computer-based-individualized-instruction-math-learning-india-0?lang=en). The latter's primary papers still need full methods retrieval; do not treat a partner summary as a fresh independent reanalysis.

## Educator workflow and actual budgets: a current 2026 test

EEF/NFER's Aila report, released **6 October 2026**, is especially useful. Schools are randomised; 464 teachers enter, 416 from 106 schools contribute to the primary analysis. The diary-time ratio is 0.76 (95% CI .64–.89), not a measured 24% improvement in student learning. Both groups may use **other AI**, and new Oak-resource use is restricted. The quality analysis reviews 42 lesson-resource sets with a noninferiority margin of −2.5 on a 25-point scale; it does not establish exact quality equality or pupil outcomes. [Full primary report, methods/Table 17/Table 18](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/projects/aila-lesson-planning_teacher-choices_evaluation-report.pdf), [official results](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/ai-tool-reduced-primary-teachers-lesson-planning-time-by-a-quarter-but-views-on-the-tool-were-mixed).

Usage declines across the trial. Experienced teachers often need to **adapt existing plans**, while Aila primarily creates new ones; curriculum alignment/free access do not ensure repeated use. Earlier 2024 ChatGPT/guide research uses a different comparator, excluding GenAI, so the two percentages are not the same estimand. [2024 evaluator report page](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/chatgpt-in-lesson-preparation-a-teacher-choices-trial/).

Budget evidence must be labelled: Aila is free to teachers; EEF lists a £433,589 evaluation grant and announces a £2.5m AI-learning research fund. Those are research/evaluation budgets, **not revenue available to a new lesson-planning SaaS**. Nigeria's pilot costs show real support/content expenses; Tutor CoPilot/StudentBench's API ratios omit them. None establishes what a school would pay the reader or how long procurement takes. A developer needs a named workflow owner and funding decision, not an “education TAM” slide.

## Specific technical branches and preparation paths

| Branch | Real work | Proof that matters | Main barrier / falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assessment and evaluation engineering | Parallel forms; validated keys/rubrics; item difficulty; leakage; delayed/transfer tests; scorer audits | An independent learner outcome changes a decision; rubric agrees with competent review | LLM judge confidence substitutes for validity; question bank contaminated; no expert access |
| Tutor / feedback software | Curriculum state, attempt-first interaction, hints, content grounding, recovery and accessibility | Fewer misleading hints and answer leaks at matched task learning/cost | Fixed worked examples or native product already perform as well |
| Teacher workflow / integrations | Adapt existing resources, LMS/roster/assessment interfaces, permissions and usable reports | Total preparation/review time improves with acceptable resources and repeat use | Export/revision burden offsets generation speed; free incumbent tool covers the need |
| Learning data / research engineering | Event contracts, anonymisation, experimental assignment, longitudinal follow-up and reproducibility | Correct denominators and an independently reproduced analysis | Data permission or sampling makes the claim indefensible |
| Adult / workplace technical learning | Diagnose prerequisites; practice/retrieval; independent debugging and transfer | Delayed ability on unseen relevant tasks, alongside useful delivered work | More completed tutorials but no retained competence or workplace outcome |

Education research/psychometrics leadership needs statistical/measurement depth and domain collaboration. Conventional product/data/platform engineering can be an entry layer without claiming to be a learning scientist; exact employer requirements still govern eligibility. A strong contribution is a reproducible correction or assessment method others trust, not declaring a new learning theory from a chatbot demo.

## A concrete 30 / 90 day offline/self-learning experiment

### Outcome and budget assumption

Proposed outcome: determine whether a chosen study method helps **the reader independently solve and explain unfamiliar technical tasks after a delay**, while producing a reusable assessment/reporting artifact. This is a personal diagnostic and engineering portfolio, not a powered trial of efficacy in other people. A planning allowance is **30–45 focused hours in the first month**, then another **40–70 hours through day 90**; actual feasibility depends on the selected unfamiliar topic and current ability. No new software, paid service or participant access is required for the initial design.

Use one bounded topic with an executable oracle, for example unfamiliar SQL window/NULL/transaction behaviour. Do not assume SQL is presently a gap; a short unaided diagnostic chooses the specific unfamiliar constructs. Existing Python `sqlite3`/SQLite can support a local synthetic exercise bank; record actual runtime/engine versions before later execution. SQLite's official docs describe the semantics and its copyright page identifies the public-domain source; no proprietary school/student data is needed. [Semantics](https://www.sqlite.org/windowfunctions.html), [copyright](https://www.sqlite.org/copyright.html).

### Days 1–10: freeze the learning and assessment contract

1. Select 3–4 small concept families at comparable baseline difficulty; keep easier, already-mastered material out of the efficacy comparison. Predefine permitted tools, equal total study time and outcomes.
2. Prepare separate training, immediate, 7-day and 21-day forms. A near-transfer item changes surface/schema; a harder transfer item requires choosing/explaining the principle in a new problem. Merely changing row values is not sufficient evidence of broad transfer.
3. Freeze keys/fixtures before studying and keep held-out solutions out of tutoring context. Use synthetic inputs, hand-check tiny cases and validate expected query results/state transitions. Accept alternative correct queries through result/invariant checks, not exact string matching. Audit the scorer before trusting any learning claim.
4. Compare a serious mature baseline—official explanations, worked examples, retrieval and a fixed review schedule—with an attempt-first, hint-based tutor condition using an already-approved assistant or recorded/local feedback. If no approved model is available, first build/test the assessment/retrieval baseline; do not buy model access to preserve the comparison.

### Days 11–30: run a self-study diagnostic, not a fake RCT

Counterbalance methods across concept families/order where feasible. Record prior knowledge, time, requests, hints, incorrect feedback, revisions and all incomplete tasks. A single learner cannot eliminate topic difficulty, carryover, expectations or sequence effects; do not claim statistical superiority from a few exercises.

At immediate and 7-day checks, answer without AI/notes, predict the outcome, explain the rule and repair one injected misconception; schedule the 21-day check into the following phase where needed. Separately record assisted task completion. Measures: correct results/invariants, explanation rubric, novel-task success, time, confidence calibration and resources used. Repeatedly testing the exact training questions measures familiarity, not the intended transfer.

Proposed day-30 acceptance: a reproducible task/answer manifest; at least one known scorer mistake caught by a deliberate adversarial fixture; an honest result showing both retained and failed concepts; and enough evidence to change the next month's study policy. No arbitrary “95% mastery” claim or universal learning effect is needed. A lower-scoring but more efficient method may still be useful depending on the actual work goal.

### Days 31–90: deepen one contribution

- **Assessment-engineering lane:** add equivalent-form checks, scorer disagreement, a small item-error taxonomy and a delayed-test report. Attempt to reproduce one public study's aggregate result using its existing published summary/code only after licensing/setup approval; do not claim historical data collection has been replicated.
- **Learning lane:** extend only the promising method to an adjacent concept; check 30/60-day retention and independent failure diagnosis. Keep scheduled practice separate from retention **after practice withdrawal**. Revisit the same goal on a structurally new task rather than accumulate tutorials.
- **Tutor-quality lane:** inspect a small explicitly licensed MathDial/public synthetic set for faulty hints and premature answer disclosure, compare fixed feedback with model-assisted feedback and report error/utility trade-offs. A simulated student's success is an engineering test, not evidence of human learning.

Package one small case study: learning objective, data/source/rights, baseline, task definitions, deterministic oracle plus explanation rubric, assistance log, delayed failures, time/cost and changed decision. The next reality gate is an educator/domain review or an authorised adult-user study with a power/consent/privacy plan; none is enrolled or contacted under this research request. Do not pilot on children or use employer/student data by assumption.

**Kill/narrow criteria:** no actual prerequisite gap; test/answer contamination; unreliable oracle; gains only while hints are visible; delayed performance worsens; explanations remain copied; added generation/review cost exceeds a fixed problem bank; no educator repeats the workflow; or the product is a wrapper around an existing free service without a measured advantage. Keep the learning/measurement skills even if an education product is rejected.

## What this changes in the 2031 career tree

Branch education recursively into assessment validity; tutor/feedback systems; teacher resources/integrations; learning data/experimentation; accessibility/modality; and adult/workplace skill acquisition. Within each, attach the actual outcome and buyer. Keep content generation, engagement, immediate unaided tests, delayed retention and workplace transfer as different leaves.

A plausible advancement path is to own a narrow learning/teacher outcome, demonstrate a reproducible improvement against mature alternatives, gain domain trust, maintain the system across curriculum/user changes, and then lead product/research decisions with qualified collaborators. Neither a model leaderboard nor the ability to teach a chatbot proves this. Stable backend/data/security skill and experimental judgment preserve options if education procurement, distribution or model bundling makes a particular product unattractive.
