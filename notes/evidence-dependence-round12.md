Checked 20 October 2026, preserving the 19 October publication cutoff. This is a synthesis of the completed methods audits, keyed to all ten IDs in `claim-register.json`. It is not another set of empirical studies, a meta-analysis or a causal re-estimation. Original reports and claims remain unchanged; the [structured map](<evidence-dependence-round12.json>) preserves source versions, dependency types, unknown overlap and claim-specific limits.

**The strongest triangulation is narrower than the career story.** Separate US outcome systems support a descriptive early-career hiring concern; separate randomized coding studies establish that assistance can help or hinder under different conditions. Neither convergence identifies a universal AI employment effect or a personal 2031 return. Many apparently additional findings share exposure labels, vendor pipelines, cohorts or the same underlying paper.

## Independence has several dimensions

An independent outcome collection can share a treatment definition and the same omitted macro shock. A different questionnaire can be linked to the same administrative register. Two independent randomized assignments can study different tools, tasks and outcome units. A new report can simply republish an earlier study. Consequently the map distinguishes **outcome provenance, treatment/label provenance, cohort overlap, analysis/publication reuse and counterfactual assumptions**. It does not assign one “independence score,” count significant studies or treat unknown linkage as zero overlap.

### Concrete shared measurement roots

The [Stanford August paper](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf), §1.2/Appendix A.3, uses Eloundou GPT-4 beta task exposure and Anthropic task-usage classifications. The [Yale methods page](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market) uses a PCA summary with Eloundou sensitivity. Its [exposure-methods appendix](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/labor-market-ai-exposure-what-do-we-know) combines Eloundou, Felten, Eisfeldt and Copilot applicability measures after crosswalking; Webb is excluded from its final PCA. [BLS WP591 Table 1](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/2026/pdf/ec260040.pdf) similarly includes multiple variants from a few source families. Fourteen measures are not fourteen independent theories or outcome datasets.

O*NET sits behind several of these approaches, including patent/task overlap and complementarity. Thus agreement may partly reflect a shared description of occupations. That does not make the scores useless or prove correlated errors of a particular sign. It does mean that new labels or a PCA cannot by themselves supply independent ground truth about Indian tasks or actual adoption. Anthropic conversation-based usage adds a different behavioral input, while still inheriting task mapping, model classification and selected-user coverage.

The South Asia and IMF audits also distinguish **AI exposure**, **AI-skill ad demand**, **reported tool use**, **self-attributed hiring effects** and **actual employment**. One cannot validate the entire chain by finding a correlation at each link in different selected populations. The India survey provides genuinely different adoption measurement; the regional industry's exposure weights still import US occupational structure. Same vendor plus overlapping dates establishes a dependence risk, not a verified count of shared advertisements.

Model labels require the same care. South Asia uses ChatGPT-4o plus manual review for a business-services occupation list. IMF uses 4.1-nano for new-skill categories and 4o-mini for degree-field inference. The startup paper uses embeddings/random forest for an AI score and GPT-4.1 for product classification. These are different targets and pipelines. A shared model vendor is not proof of shared labels, shared records or measured error correlation. Exact prompts/snapshots, validation sets and frozen input corpora remain distinct gates.

## Apparent contradictions usually need a matched estimand first

**ADP versus QWI versus Yale:** occupation/industry, ages 22–25 versus 22–24 versus broad groups, monthly versus boundary-quarter stocks, and endpoints through June 2026 versus April 2025 versus survey tracker windows differ. ADP and QWI offer stronger descriptive triangulation than a second summary of ADP. Common exposure assignments and pre-ChatGPT trends still limit AI attribution. A broad CPS null is neither a powered rejection of every narrow-cohort effect nor evidence to discard the hiring signal.

**Danish null versus divergence:** worker use/employer encouragement through December 2024 is different from newly reporting enterprise AI use, detrended FTE through September 2025 and size-specific contrasts. Both use BFL, so outcome collection is not wholly independent. Their treatment surveys are distinct, and harmonizing them within the same register could be especially informative. Precise average nulls and small-firm trend-relative declines can coexist; neither establishes aggregate offsetting job creation.

**Cui versus METR:** induced Copilot adoption and weekly PR throughput are different from permission to use AI and active completion time on familiar mature repositories. Windows, task mix, experience, compliance and quality checks differ. These experiments establish context dependence without a common pooled multiplier. The 2026 METR follow-up changes participant/task selection and includes returning developers; it is not an independent, clean overturning of the original trial.

**Higher AI advertised pay versus fewer non-AI ads:** composition can raise the average offered salary as junior or other positions disappear. IMF, South Asia, Indeed and the older Indian platform use different pay-reporting selection, skill definitions and controls. They do not jointly identify a worker's raise from training. Similarly, a leaner startup or higher engineering share can coexist with fewer engineers per firm; market entry, survival, hours and demand must bridge to aggregate employment.

The claim table's “not independently audited here” labels are scope boundaries, not findings of no domain evidence. Clinical/robotics/security reports retain their own independent technical evidence and reviews. This worker does not create dependence edges between domain studies it has not inspected.

## How to use the map in the final research tree

Keep one research-family node for a paper's editions, summaries and our reviews. Attach its outcome collections and label/treatment inputs separately. Mark a reused cohort explicitly and leave undocumented record linkage null. When combining studies, first state the common narrow conclusion and then the mismatched units/windows that prevent numerical pooling. Multiple reviewers can improve interpretation without multiplying underlying empirical evidence.

BLS/UK projections, WEF employer expectations, exposure models and conditional 2031 scenarios remain separate from observed hiring. Current occupation levels used to calibrate a forecast do not make its future numbers observations. Institutional syntheses whose underlying sources are not traced remain unclassified, rather than receiving an extra independent-evidence count.

For preparation, the independently supported action is to **test a reachable work requirement with an independent oracle and observe retained transfer, acceptance and access separately**. That is a proposed decision process assembled from limited evidence. No source or source count demonstrates its personalized return, guarantees leadership, or supplies an optimal five-year timetable.
