## What the three artifacts establish

**BatteryML provides useful competent baselines, not universal battery health truth.** Its v5 Appendix A distinguishes same-distribution charging-policy tests, unseen-policy tests, and random cell splits. Its CRUSH task predicts a 90% endpoint from 20 cycles, unlike a generic 80%-life task. For SOH it explicitly substitutes observed discharge capacity when standardized reference-performance-test capacity is unavailable. That proxy can change with workload as well as degradation. Its paper finds strong ordinary tree/linear baselines and no universal winner. Early-life features require their declared complete cycles; they cannot be used earlier simply because a file contains them. These are benchmark/task definitions, not evidence of calibrated fleet-wide safety or warranty decisions. The archived repo also means a contribution plan must not assume active upstream issue/PR review.

**BatteryLife improves diversity while exposing important target and selection limits.** Section 4.1 uses an 80% threshold generally, but 90% and first-cycle reference capacity for CALB. It excludes tests ending more than 2.5 percentage points above the chosen threshold and extrapolates nearby unfinished tests. That is not an observed failure time for every cell. It reports random 6:2:2 splits, validation-selected hyperparameters and three-run mean/SD; those SDs do not establish population or shifted-domain predictive coverage. Its seen/unseen-condition and cross-domain evaluations are useful counterevidence to universal transfer. Repeated early prefixes are dependent observations. Paper and README sample counts differ; use exact versioned manifests rather than treating headline rows as independent batteries.

The current pinned [split recorder](https://github.com/Ruifeng-Tan/BatteryLife/blob/3b17bb53202e10711deae0448c91f84144bb3dd9/data_provider/data_split_recorder.py) supplied a bounded static check: HUST train/validation/test counts 47/15/15, MATR 102/34/33, SNL 30/10/10; each sampled group has zero filename intersections. MATR batches 1–4 occur in every split. This supports cell-file separation in these lists, **not batch holdout**. It is not an audit of all aliases/protocols or actual dataset contents. The pinned [loader](https://github.com/Ruifeng-Tan/BatteryLife/blob/3b17bb53202e10711deae0448c91f84144bb3dd9/data_provider/data_loader.py), lines 433–528, uses full early-cycle arrays and prefix masks and filters cells whose EOL is no greater than the early-cycle threshold. Thus a short-prefix score can describe a population selected to survive longer. Do not label this future-feature leakage without a concrete counterexample; test whether changing post-cutoff values changes a prediction, and state the eligibility population.

**BEEP is the native alternative a custom tool must beat.** The paper describes cycler-file/metadata organization, researcher-defined validation, structuring, features and regularized model examples. Its first-100-cycle capacity/temperature features and endpoint labels are offline contracts. It describes 95% confidence bounds, including test-data-derived bounds; that alone is not demonstrated prediction-interval coverage under new temperature/protocols. The current [README](https://github.com/TRI-AMDD/beep) supports several cycler formats. Protocol generation is also part of the historical framework; this option uses read-only exports and excludes protocol/control generation.

## Evaluation contract before model choice

Separate three questions: current capacity under a specified reference test; total cycle life predicted after S completed cycles; and RUL at a live decision time. Record nominal versus initial measured capacity, test/operating temperature, charge/discharge protocol, rest periods, physical cell/batch identities, firmware/export version, event/publication time and observation cutoff. Calendar age, test-cycle index and equivalent full cycles are not interchangeable.

For a new-cell question, keep every prefix/export of a physical cell in one split. For new manufacturing batches, hold out batches. For new conditions, hold out declared protocol/temperature combinations and report interactions; an unseen chemistry is a still stronger shift. Fit transformations and choose models only on allowed training/validation units. Preserve still-running cells as censored/lower bounds in the new project; do not silently set EOL to test end or mix measured and extrapolated labels. This is a proposed evaluation policy, not a claim that every reviewed benchmark uses it.

Report cell/batch-level errors, complete failed attempts and setup/review burden. Bootstrap or intervals need the appropriate independent unit and enough groups; six synthetic cases cannot establish coverage. Separate seed variation from uncertainty over new cells. An apparent gain that is indistinguishable from noise is indeterminate, not proof that the opportunity or learner is weak.

## No-new-battery project

**Question:** can a small read-only telemetry/label audit expose an information-time, identity or endpoint error that a configured native parser/schema misses?

The attached `battery-health-round13-tiny-fixture.json` contains six original synthetic cases: measured crossing, right censoring, 90%-initial versus 80%-nominal endpoints, recovery after a threshold crossing, future-feature/early-failure eligibility, and a physical-cell export alias. Integer mAh values make threshold arithmetic inspectable. Expected answers precede any candidate implementation, but the same author made them; this is practice material, not an independent learner assessment or electrochemical validation.

**30-day option: 24–36 focused hours assumed.** Spend 4–6 defining one task/reference and resource gate; 6–10 on a typed CSV/JSON contract and as-of/cell identity checks; 6–8 on the tiny oracle and censor handling; 4–6 comparing a simple deterministic query/validator and a training-label mean/median baseline where labels are observed; 4–6 on a fresh source/configuration fault and handoff. Existing Python/SQL or paper arithmetic suffices; no dependency install is presumed. The outcome is a contract/error report, not a trained safety model.

**90-day option: 80–120 total hours assumed, including that first stage.** Allocate the additional 56–84 hours as 8–12 for one cleared small data/version gate, 16–24 for one adapter/native-baseline comparison, 16–24 for one cell/batch/condition comparison, 12–18 for independent checks and losing regimes, and 4–6 for a domain-review/handoff plan. Within the first block, cap unresolved setup/rights work at 4–6 hours and retain synthetic/manifest scope if it fails. At five hours/week, the total is 16–24 weeks, not a calendar promise.

A possible later data gate is [BatteryLife Zenodo v11](https://zenodo.org/records/19688272): metadata lists UL_PUR.zip at 6.0 MB, CALB.zip at 12.7 MB and labels at 12.6 kB, while the collection is 30.9 GB. None was downloaded. The rendered rights section exposes no license value and the API retrieval failed, so data rights remain unresolved; code MIT and an “Open” badge do not clear all originating data. A chosen archive must fit a separately approved ≤50 MB cap and have checked source terms/checksum. CALB's 90% target cannot be pooled as an 80% target. A tiny single-condition set cannot prove temperature transfer.

If an approved numerical stack is later available, compare training-only mean/median and simple linear/regularized or tree baselines before a deep model. Initial success criteria concern correct lineage/censoring and lower analyst rework at matched error coverage. No monetary warranty/safety payoff is assumed; real review costs, decision thresholds and acceptable errors require a responsible workflow owner.

**Kill or narrow:** stop execution if rights/reference/identity cannot be established within cap; reject a comparison with cell overlap or future information; keep uncertainty unresolved where groups are too few; remove custom components already covered by configured BEEP/BatteryML; stop a novelty/venture thesis when the native route meets the same need at lower complete cost. Lack of lab access stops physical validation, not the software-learning option. No buyer or independent reviewer has been secured.
