# Round 4: nuclear and fusion software paths an AI/software engineer can actually enter

Owner: energy_infrastructure. Evidence cutoff/check date: 19 October 2026. Distinct scope: actual software tasks, numerical/data verification, occupational access and bounded public-software proofs. The taxonomy worker owns the broader institution map. Earlier research files remain unchanged. No installation, benchmark execution, private access, application, outreach, physical experiment or operational control was performed.

## Finding

There are software jobs in fusion **before fusion electricity exists**. Several current postings describe familiar Python/backend, cloud, scientific workflow and agent-platform work. The nearest path for an existing AI engineer is often joining a team that serves physicists and engineers, rather than becoming the physicist responsible for the model or a qualified operator.

The best concrete example is Proxima Fusion's [Software Engineer – Simulation Platform](https://www.proximafusion.com/careers/open-position/software-engineer-simulation-platform-0186dc7c): Python is required, FastAPI/React/TypeScript/Pydantic/JAX/cloud are desirable, and the advert explicitly says no fusion background is needed. It is Munich onsite; salary, relocation and work-authorisation eligibility were not established. That is occupational evidence, not a prediction of plentiful vacancies or commercial fusion by 2031.

For scientific-software roles, trustworthy numerical results are part of the product. Versioned input data, compiler/library behaviour, random seeds, optimisation convergence and physical-model assumptions matter alongside clean APIs. Passing unit tests, agreeing with another code, fitting historical data and being suitable for a regulated engineering decision are different claims.

## 2. Where money and software work already meet

**A specific awarded platform contract:** UK Fusion Energy's [official procurement notice](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/search/show/search_view.aspx?ID=APR608189), published 15 April 2026, records a Dassault Systèmes 3DEXPERIENCE licence award concluded 24 March 2026, **£16,926,920 excluding VAT**. Its short description says three years plus an optional year, although its formal options field says no; do not treat an extension as certain. The annual report separately describes its engineering digital thread on that platform and a strategic support contract. This establishes digital-engineering spend; it also shows the strength of an incumbent platform and framework procurement route. The whole value is not an available budget for a new developer or SaaS startup.

**The software-industry evaluation workflow:** UKAEA's [19 March 2025 market-engagement notice](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/010178-2025/PDF) describes defining fusion simulation challenges, demonstrating/validating vendor capability, identifying gaps and collaborating on development. That notice is planning evidence, not an award. Third-party aggregators surfaced later awards and inconsistent values; those amounts are excluded here rather than silently promoted to verified spend.

**Funded programmes can buy ordinary enterprise software too.** The licence/platform example illustrates configuration, identities, information models, migrations and integrations around scientific engineering. Such work can be valuable without being novel plasma AI. It can also be delivered by an established platform/integrator, limiting a standalone startup's route to market.

Commercial inference: the plausible buyer is a scientific-computing/data/platform lead who can name a recurring bottleneck—failed/repeated runs, difficult discovery of signals, untraceable derived data, inaccessible HPC, weak test coverage or tools scientists cannot reliably use. A general nuclear chatbot or another dashboard has no demonstrated business case from these sources.

## 4. Public code/data that make an entry proof possible

### A. FAIR-MAST: the most direct backend/data route

[UKAEA's current service page](https://www.ukaea.org/service/fair-mast/) identifies an **active** historical MAST M05–M09 diagnostic archive: JSON metadata API plus public S3/Zarr signal data, FastAPI/Postgres, xarray/pandas. It explicitly welcomes non-fusion data scientists and schema/data-quality/downstream-tool collaboration. Software is MIT; catalogue/data/site are **CC-BY-SA-4.0 except where noted**. [Code](https://github.com/ukaea/fair-mast/).

The separate [old MAST data-request portal](https://opendata.ukaea.uk/mast-data/?ccfe-request=1&ccfe-ta=518&ccfe-tb=12304&ccfe-tc=916595) states noncommercial share-alike terms and incomplete validation. Keep permissions and quality attached to the exact archive/resource; “MAST data” is not one blanket licence. The paper and README describe design/usage; this research did not independently test endpoint availability or download signals.

**Primary papers:** Jackson et al., [*FAIR-MAST: A fusion device data management system*, SoftwareX, 2024](https://scientific-publications.ukaea.uk/wp-content/uploads/1-S2-0-S2352711024002395-MAIN.PDF), and [*An Open Data Service for Supporting Research in Machine Learning on Tokamak Data*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPS.2025.3583419). The work is concretely about search, metadata, object storage and reproducible access, not operating a tokamak. Paper-reported access improvements should be reproduced for the selected workload rather than assumed universal.

### B. FDS: useful contribution surface, explicitly pre-production

[UKAEA Fusion Data Service](https://github.com/ukaea/fds/) is Apache-2.0 and covers FastAPI, metadata/provenance, cloud storage and OIDC/access control. Its README explicitly says **no deployments yet**, pre-production and provisional API/data model until 0.1.0. It is not evidence of access to live fusion data. The local development catalogue starts empty and examples are fixtures. Public metadata tests, migrations, access boundaries and API semantics are potential contribution tasks; an existing public FAIR-MAST archive is a separate route.

The maintainers request an issue before substantial work and a private channel for vulnerabilities. These are future collaboration requirements, not authorisation for outreach during this research. No issue, account or local stack was created.

### C. PROCESS: scientific Python, optimisation and numerical reproducibility

[PROCESS](https://github.com/ukaea/PROCESS) is a UKAEA systems-code project under MIT. The current Python implementation and major restructuring mean that a historical paper or floating main branch cannot be treated as the same executable model. Pin an official release and its fixtures/documentation.

The [installation documentation, updated 15 October 2026](https://ukaea.github.io/PROCESS/installation/installation/) says testing is on latest Ubuntu/Python 3.11 and describes Mac/non-Ubuntu rounding differences that can accumulate into different optimiser convergence or nonconvergence. This is a real numerical issue, not evidence that a Mac user has necessarily introduced a bug. [Testing documentation](https://ukaea.github.io/PROCESS/development/testing/) describes unit/integration/regression suites and review of changed reference results. A changed result needs an explanation rather than either blindly widening tolerance or demanding universal byte equality.

Potential software contribution: parsers, result comparison, provenance, test diagnostics, profiling and well-scoped numerical regression improvements with domain review. This is not a recommendation to optimise a physical plant design as a first project.

### D. OpenMC: deeper scientific/HPC software, with explicit dependencies

[OpenMC](https://github.com/openmc-dev/openmc) is an open Monte Carlo transport code with Python/C++ interfaces; the [current licence](https://docs.openmc.org/en/stable/license.html) is permissive MIT-style. Romano et al.'s [2015 foundational paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030645491400379X) is useful history, but its original Fortran architecture must not be described as the present C++ implementation.

Its [current test documentation](https://docs.openmc.org/en/stable/devguide/tests.html) requires a built executable/Python module and specified cross-section/ENDF data; some tests additionally need NJOY. It explicitly discusses strict floating-point builds and thread/library effects on regression results. Tests execute real code paths, not only isolated wrappers. Nuclear-data files have provenance/processing/version requirements separate from the code licence. Full suites or public benchmark input availability must not be assumed from a GitHub README.

Contribution routes include scientific API quality, file conversion/validation, reproducibility, profiling and test coverage. Runtime/data builds require approval for installs/downloads and measured resources; none were performed here. A small teaching/test case is a more realistic entry than reproducing a large full-plant calculation on a laptop.

## 5. What a meaningful proof must verify

Scientific software has several independent failure sources: implementation, discretisation/solver choice, input/geometry conventions, measurement calibration and physical-model/data uncertainty. Increased throughput is valuable only at comparable numerical error and problem definition.

- **Code agreement is limited evidence.** Two codes can share data or assumptions and agree while differing from measurements. Comparison with an analytic solution or mathematical invariant helps verification; comparison with a traceable measured experiment and its uncertainty supports physical validation. Neither alone establishes regulatory suitability.
- **Monte Carlo noise is not a tolerance to hide behind.** Record particle/work counts and independent seeds; compare repeated-run distributions and expected uncertainty/convergence behaviour. Separate a systematic bias from sampling variation. A single run within a wide error bar is weak evidence.
- **Optimisation success flags are not sufficient.** Report residuals, constraints, termination reason, initial conditions, solver/library versions and sensitivity. A faster result from relaxed constraints or a different solved point is not an equivalent speedup.
- **Data transformations can alter scientific meaning.** Preserve signal units, dimensions, time bases, diagnostic names, quality/calibration information and missingness. Resampling, cropping and normalisation need reference checks. Store event/shot time, publication/revision time and retrieval time separately when available; do not invent missing historical availability.
- **ML leakage has a physical structure.** Windows from one experimental shot/campaign or the same simulation family should not be randomly distributed across train/test. Shared calibration/setup can create apparent transfer. Keep whole-shot/campaign or out-of-regime holdouts and test downstream uncertainty, not only held-out rows.

Two recent primary preprints illustrate the boundary between ML and operational research. [Marshall et al., 26 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26216), describes integration/testing of neural emulators in a real-time C++ plasma-control environment and extensive pre-experiment validation. [Amorisco et al., 28 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28468), reports experimental MAST-U feasibility with an existing control architecture. Both are preprints in the inspected record. They establish specialised integration/validation work, not a claim that a generic agent can control a plant or that a hobby reproduction is operationally qualified.

## 6. Two software-only 30/90-day entry experiments

These are proposals. No software was installed, data copied in bulk or code run. Pick one route; separate subsequent approval is needed for installations, container images, datasets, cloud costs or external contributions. Neither project involves a physical device, private facility or live controls.

### Route 1 — Nearer fit: scientific data quality and reproducible access

**30 days:** Use a small, declared sample from the public FAIR-MAST catalogue and only resources with explicit compatible terms. Build a read-only Python client or quality/evidence adapter, rather than a replacement archive. A record should identify shot/campaign, diagnostic, URI/version/hash where available, units, dimensions/time base, quality flags, processing/calibration provenance and resource licence. Preserve publication/revision/retrieval times separately and mark unavailable fields as unknown.

Compare with the project's documented direct-access/xarray baseline. Validate transformations using synthetic constant/sinusoidal signals with known results, round-trip array/dimension checks and independently computed summaries. Test missing signals, incompatible units, irregular clocks, truncation and changed metadata. For actual archived signals, numerical agreement with a reference transform can verify the pipeline without claiming physical interpretation. Measure bytes read, peak memory, retrieval latency and correctness; a faster request that silently drops samples fails.

**90 days:** Identify one narrow maintainable improvement with a scientific user/maintainer: a dataset adapter, metadata-validation rule, failure explanation or reproducibility notebook. Extend the fixed test set to whole held-out campaigns/diagnostics. If adding an ML classifier, use whole-shot/campaign splits and calibration; do not train on random overlapping windows. An agent interface, if useful, should call the same read-only typed tools and emit source-backed results. It should be compared against plain API/notebook use, not judged solely for fluency.

**Equipment/knowledge:** CPU laptop and network for a small sample. No tokamak, detector, GPU or physics degree is necessary to begin this data-engineering proof. Physics interpretation and quality criteria require a domain reviewer; local full-stack work may require approved containers/Postgres/dependencies. Share-alike/data exceptions must remain in the manifest.

**Go/kill:** Continue if the adapter catches consequential metadata/data errors or reliably saves researchers time without changing signal meaning. Kill the standalone-product claim if the archive already solves the task, no user needs the extra layer, terms prevent the intended reuse, or correctness depends on unavailable private calibration. A useful upstream contribution/portfolio can remain even when no scalable business follows.

### Route 2 — Deeper fit: numerical regression and reproducibility tooling

**30 days:** Choose a maintainer-documented educational/test case from one **released** OpenMC or PROCESS version and the corresponding reference files. First inventory build/data requirements and licence/provenance. After approved setup, reproduce the reference path unmodified. Create a run manifest with commit/release, exact inputs, data hashes, compiler/runtime/libraries, thread settings, seeds, solver termination and resource use. Avoid generating new engineering designs or adjusting physical operating parameters.

For deterministic components, add a mathematically independent check or documented invariant and predeclared tolerances. For stochastic components, use independent repeated seeds and work-count sweeps to distinguish bias, convergence and uncertainty coverage. Retain failed/nonconverged runs in the report. When possible, compare serial and parallel execution at the same numerical contract; bit-level changes and material scientific changes should be explained separately. Do not call cross-code agreement experimental validation.

**90 days:** Deliver one focused software improvement: a failing parser/regression reproduction with a minimal fix, an error-reporting improvement, a verified performance optimisation or a robust comparison tool. Keep the official reference result, numerical-error budget and test path visible to an independent reviewer. For PROCESS on a Mac, verify sensitive conclusions on its documented supported environment; do not spend weeks assuming every platform-dependent difference is a source defect.

**Equipment/knowledge:** Start with CPU tests and small cases. Larger suites need cross-section data, build tools and possibly institutional HPC. Learn numerical linear algebra, conditioning, floating point, statistics/Monte Carlo, optimisation and scientific-file conventions. High-fidelity model development adds transport/plasma/thermal domain knowledge. No personal nuclear equipment or operational credentials are required for public software verification; private/higher-assurance deployments have separate requirements.

**Go/kill:** Stop an optimisation claim if it changes the solved problem, loosens tolerances, excludes hard cases or produces unexplained reference drift. Stop a contribution if maintainers do not need the proposed abstraction. Do not promote a toy/research benchmark to a validated physical design or safety claim. This route can still establish RSE/HPC competence transferable to other scientific industries.

## 8. What this implies for a five-year developer path

Career inference, not a labour forecast: build the ability to make scientific/engineering teams faster **without weakening evidence**. A strong backend/AI engineer can start with data/API/cloud/agent-platform problems and progressively learn numerical/domain concepts. A deeper RSE route requires sustained mathematics and scientific-software practice, but it need not begin with becoming a reactor physicist. Core physics research, instrumentation/control authority and safety-significant engineering remain separate specialisations.

The opportunity is employer- and programme-concentrated, often site-based and dependent on funding, work permission and institutional access. This argues for transferable data/distributed/numerical-verification skills rather than a portfolio whose value depends on one fusion timetable. Actual role requirements and the PLM contract make this more actionable than “nuclear is the next big thing.”
