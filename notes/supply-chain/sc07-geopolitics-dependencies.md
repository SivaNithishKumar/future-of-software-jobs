# SC07 — geopolitics, physical dependencies and developer access

Selected evidence checked 20 October 2026. This is a dated supply-chain analysis with an incomplete current-law read, not an export decision or a country ranking.

## A. What the official legal sources establish

[May 2026 BIS guidance](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/bis-guidance-may-31-2026.pdf) distinguishes the announced non-enforcement of new AI Diffusion compliance requirements from older restrictions on an entity’s headquarters or ultimate parent. Those older controls can matter even when the entity is physically elsewhere. This read did not recover the formal rescission/replacement instrument; old worldwide Diffusion tiers must not be treated as active by assumption, nor all controls as removed.

The [published final rule effective 15 January 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-15/pdf/2026-00789.pdf) creates a narrow case-by-case review path for specified exports from the US to China/Macau, with TPP strictly below 21,000, defined total DRAM bandwidth strictly below 6,500 GB/s and certifications. It does not issue a licence or promise approval. Its binding supply comparison uses aggregate TPP, including a specified 50% condition—not a simple count of chips or world installed capacity. Independent US testing and recipient/security/IaaS conditions have their own scope. Including HBM in the DRAM definition does not settle every separate HBM control.

The full EAR Parts 742 and 744 returned tool-size failures. Complete current HBM, equipment/software, U.S.-person, FDP, entity, exception and later-amendment coverage remains **unverified**. No retry, alias or workaround was used. Nationality, item, employer and service eligibility for the reader are not established.

## B. Where the physical dependencies sit

The OECD’s [2025 value-chain study](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/mapping-the-semiconductor-value-chain_5ba52971/4154cdbf-en.pdf) supports complementary stages, rather than national self-sufficiency:

| Geography | Selected source-bound stage examples |
|---|---|
| US | Fabless design and EDA: NVIDIA/AMD; Cadence/Synopsys |
| Taiwan (OECD: Chinese Taipei) | Foundries and packaging: TSMC/UMC; ASE |
| Korea | Memory: Samsung/SK hynix |
| Japan | Wafers, materials and tools: Shin-Etsu/SUMCO; Tokyo Electron |
| Netherlands | EUV and selected deposition tools: ASML; ASM |
| Other Europe/UK | German wafers, Belgian R&D, UK IP: Siltronic; imec; Arm |
| China | Fabrication/design/packaging; JCET/Tongfu packaging examples |

Firm headquarters, parent ownership, customer location and production sites are different attributes. This study mixes older trade/input-output data with 2023 company measures; it cannot certify October 2026 supply independence or qualified output.

The [December 2025 capacity study](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/the-chip-landscape_27ef5d87/02dbd028-en.pdf) places 87% of its September 2025 in-production wafer capacity in China, Taiwan, Korea, Japan and the US. Its unit is maximum wafer starts in eight-inch equivalents, independent of yield and die size. “In production” includes first silicon; pilot/R&D lines are included. Mixed fabs can appear fully in multiple chip-category panels. Country charts use fabrication location. These are not additive counts of usable AI accelerators, and announced fabs are not qualified volume output.

The geopolitical inference is conditional: policy can change access and investment, while replacement requires stage-specific tools, process compatibility and qualification. The selected evidence does not measure substitution speed, inevitable national winners or future job counts. It also does not close the unread legal layers above.
