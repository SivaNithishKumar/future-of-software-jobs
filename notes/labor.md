# Labor market evidence for developer futures, checked 2026-10-19

## Main interpretation

The defensible forecast is a changing, more uneven software profession, with pressure on repeatable implementation and entry-level hiring and continued demand for people who own production outcomes. Current evidence does not establish economy-wide AI job destruction. Nor does it establish that experienced developers are safe indefinitely. A 2031 career strategy should be robust to both faster and slower AI adoption: domain judgment, strong engineering foundations, AI integration, evaluation/security, production operation, and customer/business ownership.

## Verified institutional evidence

1. **BLS, updated Aug 27 2026, 2025–2035 US projections.** Current software developer/QA/tester group forecast is **10% growth**, from **1,905,400 to 2,090,800**, **185,400 net jobs**, with **106,100 annual openings** including replacement. Developers alone: **1,717,800 to 1,892,600**, rounded **10%**, **174,700** added; May 2025 median wage **$135,980**. QA/testers: **6%** growth. Older cached searches still return **15% / 129,200 openings / 2024–34**, so do not quote those as current. These are US structural projections, not global, near-term hiring, entry-level probabilities, or precise 2031 totals. [BLS software developers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm)

2. **BLS programmers, same projection vintage.** The narrower occupation is projected **−7%**, **110,800 to 102,700**, with **4,400 annual replacement openings**. BLS specifically attributes pressure to automating repetitive programming and shifting higher-skilled tasks to developers. This supports distinguishing coding tasks from whole software ownership; occupational classification changes and overlap also matter. [BLS computer programmers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/computer-programmers.htm)

3. **ILO–NASK, May 20 2025, global task-exposure model.** Nearly **30,000 occupational tasks**, expert review, AI-assisted scoring and harmonized labor data: **25% of global employment** in potentially exposed occupations, **34% in high-income countries**. Clerical work is highest exposure, with increased exposure in software/media/finance. This is capability exposure, explicitly **not actual job losses**. Whole occupations retain tasks requiring human involvement; adoption depends on infrastructure, cost, institutions and skills. [ILO release](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/one-four-jobs-risk-being-transformed-genai-new-ilo%E2%80%93nask-global-index-shows)

4. **WEF Future of Jobs, published Jan 7 2025; employer expectations for 2025–2030.** More than **1,000 leading employers**, representing **14m+ workers**, **22 industries**, **55 economies**. Survey-based extrapolation estimates **170m jobs created, 92m displaced, net +78m** across structural trends, not AI alone. Software/app developers, AI/ML, big data and fintech are among fastest-growing percentage categories. **39%** of existing skill sets expected to change or become outdated. Survey employers plan both AI hiring and reductions: about two-thirds plan hiring specific AI skills, **40% anticipate workforce reduction where AI automates tasks**. Treat as reported expectations, not measured outcomes or reliable net-creation arithmetic for any individual country. [WEF report](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025)

5. **OECD Skills in the AI Age, July 8 2026, institutional synthesis.** Firm AI uptake rose from about **7% to 20% across 2021–2025** in OECD countries; adoption remains uneven, especially for SMEs facing cost, infrastructure and skill constraints. Advanced AI skills remain uncommon, around **1% of workforce**. Critical thinking, collaboration, creativity and learning complement technical skills. Interpretation: a large integration/adoption opportunity remains; do not confuse 20% firm adoption with 20% of tasks automated. [OECD executive summary](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/skills-in-the-ai-age_972bd15e-en/full-report/component-4.html)

## Observed employment, caveats and disconfirmation

6. **Stanford/ADP Canaries, revised Aug 12 2026, payroll data through June 2026.** Millions of US workers: no widespread economy-wide displacement; however employment of **22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below a counterfactual tracking less-exposed peers**. This is a relative gap, not 19% of all young workers losing jobs. The channel is reduced hiring more than increased separations. Experienced workers show no comparable gap; base compensation shows little adjustment. Results persist excluding technology firms/computer occupations and controlling interest-rate/remote-work exposure. But they attenuate with education controls, some differential pretrends exist, and ADP signals exceed national survey benchmarks. Authors explicitly call these **descriptive indicators, not causal estimates**. [Stanford paper landing page](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/)

7. **Yale Budget Lab tracker, updated Oct 14 2026.** Occupational composition is not changing in ways clearly aligned with AI introduction; usage measures show no connection to employment/unemployment changes; synthetic differences-in-differences shows no clear AI footprint. This tempers mass-displacement claims. It does not refute narrow entry-level damage: CPS is stronger for broad groups than small graduate cohorts, exposure mixes substitution/complementarity, and occupation labels can miss task-level change. [Current tracker](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/tracking-impact-ai-labor-market). The methodological analysis is dated May 7 2026: [Yale methods and caveats](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market).

8. **Indeed Hiring Lab, Sept 17 2026, US salaried postings 2021–2026.** More-exposed occupations show higher advertised-pay growth. Post-ChatGPT differential is **5.7%** with occupation controls and **4.7%** comparing same job titles; **2.4%**, not significant at 5%, when occupation-by-seniority mix is held constant. Entry-level share of the most-exposed *annual-salary-advertising* postings fell **29%→10%**, senior **22%→47%**. This sample skews senior and excludes jobs without annual salaries; these are ads, not hires or incumbent wages. Seniority regression splits mostly lack significance. Positive pay trends are compatible with fewer openings and hiring selection. Do not claim AI caused either. [Indeed analysis](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/09/17/ai-exposure-isnt-squeezing-advertised-pay-in-the-us-its-boosting-it/)

## 2031 implications (inference, not source forecasts)

- Strongest adjacent path for an applied AI engineer: own a domain workflow from discovery through production and measured economics, with security/evaluation/operations built in. This maps closely to existing applied/forward-deployed AI and agent-platform roles.
- Do not specialize only in one framework, prompt syntax or generating code. Tool competence is necessary but production reliability, data/context, authorization, evaluation and business accountability travel across model generations.
- Entry-level apprenticeship may be the sharpest bottleneck. Seek real system ownership, debugging, incident analysis and expert feedback; build evidence of judgment instead of project count. Do not infer juniors are unnecessary: removing the training pipeline creates later senior-supply problems, and current data do not settle how employers repair it.
- India/global-remote planning needs an eligibility filter. UK/US growth and salary observations cannot be directly transferred to India; remote roles often restrict residence and payroll jurisdictions.
- Watch hiring rates and employment by tenure/age, not just layoffs. Reduced hiring can damage opportunity before aggregate headcount falls.

## Methods audit added after user emphasis on deep reliability

### Stanford revision and sample audit (full paper)

[Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf), pp. 2, 5–6, 40–43, 139–140: Main panel includes **3.5–5m worker–firm matches per month**, full-time, positive earnings, under 70, firms continuously present Jan2021–Jun2026. This conditions on firm survival, and excludes much new-firm formation. About 30% lack titles; history-based occupation imputation then remaining unmatched records excluded. Panel growth **+6.3%** versus BLS nonfarm **+3.1%**; unbalanced ADP **−3.7%**, illustrating sample-selection importance. Headline **19%** is a new descriptive measure, not directly comparable to older **13% regression**. On same descriptive metric gap was 15% at Jul2025; top two quintiles −11% vs bottom three +10% by Jun2026. Revision improves occupation crosswalk/imputation and relaxes prior firm filters; within-firm results are pipeline-sensitive. Headline therefore signals differential exposure/age trends, not identified treatment effect of employers adopting AI.

### Yale design audit

[May 7 2026 methods](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market): CPS, top and bottom unweighted occupation terciles, equal occupation information rather than worker weighting; synthetic comparison chosen to reproduce pre-2022 paths, 2020 omitted. Main employment outcome is mean occupation employment share of civilian population; pay outcome averages occupation medians because of topcoding. Assumptions: exposure distinguishes affected jobs; untreated weighted occupations reproduce counterfactual path; concurrent shocks do not differentially coincide. Mixing positive/negative effects can wash out. Restricting to ages22–27 leaves few occupations reaching 50 observations/quarter, explaining weak narrow-subgroup power. This is methodologically more disciplined than simple exposed/unexposed plots, but null estimates still do not prove zero effects everywhere or predict future models.

### Actual-adoption counterpoint, and why wages are tricky

[Humlum/Vestergaard's own Brookings summary, Oct6 2026](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/still-waters-rapid-currents-early-labor-market-transformation-under-generative-ai/): surveys of **25k Danish workers**, **11 exposed occupations**, linked monthly admin records **through Dec2024**, precise nulls for earnings/hours and workplace outcomes, ruling out average >2%. Firms adopting chatbots did not drive exposed early-career declines. Crucially adopters already earned **4–9% more before ChatGPT**; levels cannot identify tool returns. Self-reported task reorganization is substantial despite stable economic outcomes. This paper's older observation window, Denmark institutions, actual adoption measures, and incumbents/workplaces differ from Stanford's US exposure-by-age analysis through2026. They answer different estimands, not a clean replication dispute. Neither establishes2031.

### What would falsify or strengthen the career thesis?

The thesis is that integration, verification and operational/domain ownership remain valuable while routine implementation loses scarcity. Strengthen it with adoption-linked hiring and wage effects within comparable firms, repeated demand for production/evaluation/security skills, real deployed business margins and failure-cost reductions. Weaken it if autonomous systems reliably own requirements, integration, incident response and external accountability at lower total cost, or if these skills' postings/pay contract across sectors after macro controls. Benchmark gains, selected vacancies, employer intentions and productivity demos alone cannot settle that question. Treat2031 as scenarios and monitor observable mechanisms rather than presenting a point prediction.

## Actionable paths for the branched 2031 mind map

These are proposed learning and evidence-building paths, **not forecasts that these exact titles will dominate in 2031**. No claim is made about the reader's current seniority, years of experience, mathematical level, education, interview readiness, compensation, work authorization or access to hardware. Being an AI engineer and having built projects does not establish those particulars. The user's interest in broad expertise is a reason for a broad technical foundation; it is not evidence that equal-depth mastery of all fields is feasible or required.

### Common trunk: demonstrate independent engineering judgment

Before choosing a deep branch, produce a small evidence inventory from existing permitted work. For each of Python/software design, TypeScript/UI, SQL/data modelling, Linux/networking, distributed systems, statistics/experimental design, ML training/evaluation, security and domain discovery, distinguish **observed**, **practised with help**, **operated independently**, and **unknown**. Use artifacts and a runnable exercise rather than self-rated years. Private employer code is not a public portfolio; use sanitized descriptions or a separate synthetic project where permission is absent.

Suggested diagnostic exercises are a failure-prone integration service with idempotency and recovery; a model comparison with an untouched holdout set; an unfamiliar service incident with logs and a postmortem; and a short customer interview translated into an acceptance test. Record where AI assistance was used, what it got wrong, and what the developer could verify independently. These exercises diagnose gaps; passing them does not establish expertise.

The common foundation to develop is programming and debugging, data structures and algorithms sufficient for the target work, SQL and data quality, HTTP/auth/networking, tests and observability, probability and statistics, security boundaries, written design trade-offs, and customer/domain reasoning. The branch choice should follow which problems the user enjoys and can access, plus validated hiring/customer demand. A reasonable portfolio structure is **one deeply practised specialty, one adjacent capability, and literacy elsewhere**, subject to the user's available time and aims.

### Branch A — Applied AI engineer / technical product owner / forward-deployed engineer

**Why this branch:** a close adjacency to the known AI-engineering context, supported by the concrete Anthropic and GitLab requirements above. It combines reliable software, models, customer workflows and product delivery; it does not require inventing a foundation model.

**Learn:** API/service fundamentals, queues and durable workflows, identity/permissions, structured extraction and retrieval, tool calling and state transitions, evaluation design, failure recovery, cost/latency accounting, and discovery of the customer's real acceptance criteria. Learn to justify a deterministic component, an existing model, fine-tuning, or no AI using evidence.

**Build:** a synthetic operations workflow such as receiving a service request, gathering evidence, proposing actions, obtaining an approval and updating a simulated downstream system. Include incomplete documents, conflicting state, stale data, duplicate requests and provider outages. Keep external actions simulated until separately authorized. Reuse mature libraries selected for the project; the research task itself installs nothing.

**Test:** compare human/manual, deterministic and model-assisted baselines under the same task distribution. Predefine task success, consequential error severity, manual intervention, completion latency, cost and rollback/recovery. Hold out cases from prompt or agent tuning; measure repeated runs where stochasticity matters. A slick demonstration with no denominator is insufficient. Show an authorized operator can inspect evidence and recover a failed job.

**Entry:** an internal scoped workflow, a contribution to an existing agent/backend project, an intermediate backend role with AI responsibilities, or a customer-facing applied role where prior experience fits. Do not imply a senior FDE salary is an accessible baseline. A useful proof package is a runnable service, evaluation report, design decision and one authorized real-user outcome.

**Leadership evidence by 2031:** multiple consequential systems operated over time; clear decisions about automation limits; measured adoption and economics; incident learning; reusable patterns adopted by others; mentoring and cross-team delivery. This is a destination to work toward, not a title guaranteed by completing a course.

### Branch B — AI evaluation, verification and security engineering

**Why this branch:** real employer demand exists for testing agent behavior, prompt-injection defenses, production guardrails and audit controls. The compelling work is making evidence reliable enough to support a deployment decision, not accumulating benchmark scores.

**Learn:** probability, confidence intervals and paired experiments; sampling and leakage; threat modelling; authorization and isolation; human rubric design; grader calibration; adversarial testing; regression analysis; and communicating what an evaluation cannot establish. Inspect's primary documentation distinguishes scorers, model grading, metrics and transcript review, and provides an existing framework to study before inventing one. [Inspect scoring documentation](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/scoring.html)

**Build:** an evaluation suite for Branch A's workflow containing legitimate hard cases and controlled attacks: misleading retrieved text, forged approvals, unauthorized tool arguments, cross-user data access, missing evidence and recovery after partial completion. Maintain explicit expected outcomes and failure categories. A synthetic suite is a learning artifact, not evidence of complete protection against real adversaries.

**Test:** use independent human review on a subset; compare human agreement with automated graders; separate infrastructure errors from model failures; report severity-weighted failures and subgroup coverage; repeat critical cases with varied seeds, models and contexts. For access-control tests, directly assert forbidden effects never occurred in the tested runs. Zero observed failures gives a bound under the sampling assumptions, not a universal safety guarantee. Inspect supports sandboxed tool execution, which should be studied along with the limits of the sandbox. [Inspect sandboxing](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/sandboxing.html)

**Entry:** extend CI/evaluations in an existing product; contribute a reproducible, well-scoped evaluation or defense to a maintained project; target software-quality, application-security or ML-platform roles with an evaluation component. The senior Atlassian posting is a reference for later depth, not a requirement that every entrant already has seven years of ML work.

**Leadership evidence by 2031:** evaluation standards influence releases across teams; critical failures are caught before deployment; uncertainty is communicated accurately; independent reviewers can reproduce results; attacks produce durable fixes; and developers can use the system without the specialist becoming a permanent manual bottleneck.

### Branch C — Platform, data and reliability engineering for AI systems

**Why this branch:** agent and ML roles still need APIs, correct data, performance, incident handling and distributed-system guarantees. This is a transferable foundation if a particular model or framework loses popularity.

**Learn:** database transactions and migrations, data contracts and provenance, indexing/retrieval, queues/backpressure, retries/idempotency, caching and invalidation, service-level objectives, profiling, load tests, observability, deployment rollback and cost models. Add GPU/inference internals only when the target problem needs that depth; do not prescribe CUDA as universal developer preparation.

**Build:** a multi-tenant synthetic ingestion and retrieval service feeding a model-assisted application. Support incremental updates, deletion, schema changes, duplicate events, authorization, provenance and reproducible dataset versions. Add a model gateway only if it solves a measurable routing, reliability or cost problem.

**Test:** replay interrupted ingest; prove deletion and tenant isolation; inject provider timeouts and duplicates; measure data freshness and result correctness; identify the saturation point under load; compare performance against a simpler baseline; show a safe migration and rollback. Choose objectives around user needs rather than an arbitrary number of nines. Google's SRE material is a primary practical starting point for SLOs, error budgets and reliability prioritization. [Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/)

**Entry:** backend/data/platform/SRE responsibilities at an existing software company, an open-source reliability contribution, or operating the platform behind Branch A. More infrastructure should be justified by measured failure or load, not included to make a portfolio look enterprise-sized.

**Leadership evidence by 2031:** other teams ship faster with fewer incidents; operating costs and service risk are understood; migrations and outages are handled competently; architecture stays simpler than the constraints allow; and the engineer teaches others to make reliable trade-offs.

### Branch D — Robotics software and deployment, with perception or controls as deeper options

**Why this branch:** physical-world integration is a distinct set of skills with visible employer demand, including in India. It is not automatically protected from automation, and its market size, relocation requirements and hardware-access costs differ from web software.

**Learn first:** C++/Python, Linux and debugging, linear algebra and rigid transforms, kinematics, feedback/control basics, ROS2 communication and transforms, time synchronization, sensor calibration, logging/replay, simulation and failure diagnosis. For the **perception** subbranch add estimation, uncertainty, Kalman/Bayesian filtering, geometry, tracking, sensor fusion and real-time optimization. For **controls** add dynamics, stability, estimation and constraints; this should be assessed separately from perception and general ROS integration.

**Build:** begin with a simulated mobile robot that localizes and reaches goals, with logged observations and repeatable test scenarios. Add sensor dropouts, timing skew, moving obstacles and map errors. Progress to a supervised physical platform only after simulation competence and appropriate access/approval. The research does not authorize hardware purchases, installation or unsupervised physical operation.

**Test:** measure collision/intervention rates, goal completion, localization error and runtime/latency against simple baselines under varied environments. Prevent train/test scene leakage. Demonstrate a reproducible failure, identify its cause and repair it. Simulation success is not physical reliability; later work must report the sim-to-real gap, calibrations and operating envelope explicitly.

**Entry:** a concrete ROS2 integration/deployment contribution or project can support the integration lane. The Aeva perception role above shows that research-heavy perception can require additional mathematical depth, production experience and graduate education; a ROS course is not an equivalent qualification. A role-fit review should distinguish hard education filters from preferences before recommending formal study. The official ROS tutorial URL was identified but automated retrieval was blocked by Anubis during this pass, so its current lesson contents were not verified: [ROS2 Jazzy tutorials](https://docs.ros.org/en/jazzy/Tutorials.html).

**Leadership evidence by 2031:** a fleet or deployed system works within a documented envelope; failures are traceable from field logs; hardware/software teams coordinate; field maintainability and operator workflows improve; and releases meet independent engineering and safety review requirements. Pure research leadership is a different route involving novel contributions and peer scrutiny; it should not be conflated with fleet deployment leadership.

### Progression gates shared by all branches

Use observable gates rather than fixed claims such as “master ML in six months.” **Foundation gate:** explain and debug the core components with documented assistance. **Independent-build gate:** implement and test an unfamiliar bounded task. **Production/reality gate:** support authorized users or real hardware over time and diagnose failures. **Specialist gate:** improve a difficult system against a credible baseline, with review from someone experienced. **Leadership gate:** other people adopt the engineer's decisions, systems or practices, producing sustained outcomes without requiring the engineer to perform every task personally. These gates may be reached at different times in different skills; years elapsed and job titles are imperfect proxies.

## Geographic portability and missing personal inputs

- **US statistics:** BLS demand and wage numbers describe the US occupation and labor institutions. They provide directional context, not a conversion to rupees, remote salaries, or a promise of local openings.
- **India:** distinguish product companies/global capability centers, services/outsourcing, domestic startups, industrial/robotics employers and independent contracting. They have different budgets, skill filters and client exposure. The present packet's selected postings cannot quantify these segments.
- **UK and global remote:** a role's stated locations and employer policy govern eligibility. A globally distributed team is not global hiring permission. Separate remote-in-India, remote from a fixed set of countries, relocation/sponsorship, contracting and employer-of-record arrangements when collecting opportunities; verify actual eligibility before career conclusions. No personal immigration eligibility is inferred here.
- **Robotics and embedded systems:** colocated labs, vehicle access, travel and field work can constrain portability even where some code work is remote. Simulated portfolio work helps learning but does not demonstrate field experience.
- **Personal inputs still unknown:** current skill evidence, seniority/responsibility, education, weekly time, appetite for customer work versus research, target geography, relocation preference, financial runway, compensation constraints, hardware access and desired founder-versus-employed balance. Their absence should change recommendation confidence, not be filled with invented history.

## Research gaps for the extended investigation

1. **Representative India demand:** build a transparent sampling frame across employer types and locations, collect official requirements, remove duplicate/reposted requisitions, distinguish seniority and location eligibility, and retain collection dates. Measure shares within that frame, never call them shares of the entire job market. Missing salary and publication dates remain missing. A larger purposive sample remains nonrepresentative.
2. **Vacancies versus hiring:** track filled/withdrawn roles where employers disclose them, time-to-fill, job starts and age/experience profiles. Open ads can be stale, evergreen or duplicate. An inaccessible or removed URL cannot establish that a person was hired.
3. **Actual adoption and causal timing:** prioritize studies linking firm/worker adoption to administrative outcomes, with pretrends, placebo dates, within-firm comparisons, adoption-intensity measures and robustness to interest rates, remote work and education. Report standard errors, confidence intervals and the null's detection limit. No comparison design eliminates every confounder.
4. **Task change within stable employment:** seek longitudinal evidence on autonomy, hours, workload intensity, supervision and training, not just headcount. AI may increase output expectations without changing pay or occupational labels.
5. **Entry-level pipeline:** investigate apprenticeships, internships, promotion rates, replacement hiring and whether AI-assisted novices acquire durable debugging/design skill. Do not assume either universal deskilling or automatic accelerated expertise.
6. **Skill premiums:** distinguish advertised pay, offered pay, realized earnings and total compensation; control occupation, seniority, geography and firm. Raw AI premiums may reflect selection, especially where adoption predates the measured pay gap.
7. **Robotics opportunity quality:** distinguish demos, paid trials, repeat deployments, unit economics, operator/intervention burden and actual hiring. Count software integration/deployment roles separately from research-scientist roles and hardware/manufacturing roles.
8. **Founder route:** test access to paying customers, distribution, workflow switching cost, support burden, liability and gross margin. Code generation lowers build cost for competitors too; no source here establishes that a one-person startup is likely to succeed.
9. **Credentials and access:** inspect hard degree filters, equivalent-experience wording, research-publication expectations and public contribution pathways by role. Recommend a degree only where it addresses an actual role filter or learning objective and its costs fit the user's constraints.
10. **Durability of the recommendation:** record what would change the chosen branch—sustained adoption-linked demand decline, autonomous capability that covers previously human ownership, better local opportunities, changed user interests or unacceptable entry costs. Revisit on new evidence; a five-year plan should have explicit revision conditions.

### Branch-ready fields for the final mind map

Every career leaf should carry: **problem owned → observed demand/source date → transferable fundamentals → specialized learning → concrete build → test/oracle → entry channel → portfolio evidence → geographic/credential constraints → leadership evidence → uncertainty/revision trigger**. Do not let an attractive role label substitute for these fields. Separate a recommendation from an observed labor statistic, and mark unverified personal fit explicitly.
