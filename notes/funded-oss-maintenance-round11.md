## Finding

Paid maintenance exists through several different transactions: a public body commissions scoped work, a programme buys specialist services for a project, a grant finances agreed future milestones, a foundation pays approved project expenses or contractors, and sponsors make discretionary contributions. These mechanisms are not interchangeable with a salaried role or a recurring income entitlement.

The entry gate is usually **a project and a credible receiving community**, not possession of a new certificate. Some routes allow individuals without university affiliation; others require a particular project affiliation, country, public-benefit purpose or recognised maintainer involvement. Authority to merge code demonstrates community trust, but does not imply a paid position. Conversely, funding can pay for useful documentation, test infrastructure, compatibility work or coordination without conferring that authority.

## 1. Sovereign Tech Fund: commissioned infrastructure work

The agency invests globally in foundational technologies rather than user-facing prototypes. Current requirements say the proposed work must **exceed €50,000**, prohibit duplicate public financing of the same activities, and require freely reusable/modifiable/redistributable code and documentation. Code may use OSI-approved or FSF Free/Libre licences; documentation cannot use NC/ND restrictions. Applicants need ecosystem expertise or community endorsement. The described selection-to-contract path is approximately **six months**; approved invoices with reports may then take up to **30 days**. No degree gate is stated in the inspected requirements, but this is not a simple individual microgrant. [Fund criteria and process](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/fund), [agency payment/global-scope FAQ](https://www.sovereign.tech/faq)

**Access implication:** an India- or UK-based contributor is not excluded merely because the fund is German, but contracting/legal review, scope, rights and actual community backing still apply. A newcomer should not stretch a tiny test improvement to the minimum budget. A credible route may be contributing a validated subtask to an already coordinated infrastructure effort; that requires a later authorised agreement, not an assumed entitlement to part of its funding.

**Historical funded example:** the agency lists **curl, €195,000 in 2022–2023, status Past**, for bug cleanup and HTTP-related work. Its page describes a 122-known-bug starting backlog and intended classification/fixes. This is evidence of a funded scope, **not an audit that all 122 bugs were fixed**. [Agency curl record](https://www.sovereign.tech/tech/curl)

## 2. Sovereign Tech Resilience: support for projects, paid delivery through partners

The programme commissions specialist services and delivers them to selected foundational projects. Current partners cover technical debt, memory safety, PQC preparation, audits and supply-chain improvements. Engagements are scoped with maintainers; supported artifacts must circulate under open terms. This is not an unrestricted grant for any contributor who finds a bug. The page's **September 2026 status pauses new bounty reports and new bounty programmes** while a backlog is processed; do not assume a predicted autumn resumption has occurred. [Programme/service/payment distinctions](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/resilience), [eligibility and licensing](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/resilience/criteria)

Its public account reports over 10,000 lines of tests added to systemd and 221 CVEs triaged for Yocto, plus audit outcomes. These are reported service outputs, not independently reproduced security-effect estimates. Named implementation partners, rather than arbitrary applicants, deliver the contracted services. [Relaunch and partner model](https://www.sovereign.tech/news/resilience-relaunch)

**Access implication:** a developer can prepare a relevant test, reproducible-build or migration artifact, but project participation, supplier procurement and paid employment/subcontracting are separate routes. A programme's paid work is not an invitation to probe an estate or submit unsolicited security findings. No bounty platform or supplier recruitment process was accessed here.

## 3. NLnet CodeSupply: a currently open, narrow milestone route

The first call closes **3 November 2026 at 12:00 CET**. It seeks **€5,000–€50,000** contributions to software metadata: origin/licensing, vulnerabilities, quality, data access and related infrastructure. Results must use recognised free/open-source terms. The programme FAQ says there are **no CodeSupply grantees yet**; examples from another fund cannot be presented as its track record. [CodeSupply scope and call](https://nlnet.nl/codesupply/)

University or company affiliation is not required; individuals can be paid directly. Payment follows completed agreed milestones, and selection can take **three–five months from the call deadline**. Earlier work cannot simply be reimbursed. The FAQ requires a European dimension; it gives a team/funding-share route and a possible exceptional-contribution route outside the relevant countries. This is not unconditional worldwide eligibility. [Eligibility](https://nlnet.nl/codesupply/eligibility/), [payment, geography and past-work rules](https://nlnet.nl/codesupply/faq/)

**AI condition:** the same FAQ asks applicants to write their own proposal; if generative AI writes any part, the text must disclose this and explain why. Do not repurpose this research note as an undisclosed application. This condition is about proposal authorship; it does not settle every project's AI-code contribution policy.

**Historical sibling-fund example:** NGI Zero Commons Fund's final call closed **1 June 2026**. Its FiberQ grant, starting June, funds schema migration, topology/attribute validation, tests/CI and interchange work in a GPL-3.0-or-later QGIS plugin. The grant page distinguishes the open plugin from a separate commercial companion. It establishes a funded scope, not completion of every promised feature. [Closed Commons Fund](https://nlnet.nl/commonsfund/index.html), [FiberQ grant](https://nlnet.nl/project/FiberQ/), [public code](https://github.com/vukovicvl/fiberq)

## 5. NumFOCUS small grants: a project-controlled microcontract route

Only fiscally sponsored/affiliated projects submit; each selects its own proposal. The page allows work worldwide subject to applicable restrictions, requires achievable work within a year, and lists up to **$10,000 per proposal**. Project accounts/expense procedures matter: contractors billing over **$600** must sign an independent-contractor agreement before billing. An interested contributor is not automatically the grantee or the person controlling a project's account. The public call dates are explicitly **2025**, so a 2026 round is **unverified**. [Programme, eligibility, payment and reporting rules](https://numfocus.org/programs/small-development-grants)

A listed **2024 $10,000 PyTables award** targets HDF5 direct-chunk support. The public source project is BSD-3-Clause, with dependency/component terms to check separately. The award list does not itself prove the feature shipped or identify an unfilled paid assignment. [PyTables code/contributor route](https://github.com/PyTables/PyTables)

**Access implication:** concrete tests, compatibility fixes, packaging and documentation can be legitimate paid deliverables. An eligible project must first want the work and control its submission. No degree gate or programme-wide AI rule was located in the inspected guidance; these are not permissions to ignore the receiving project's rules.

## 7. GitHub Sponsors: discretionary support rather than an award or job

GitHub permits one-time/monthly sponsorship and recognises many kinds of open-source contribution, not only code. **India and the UK are supported recipient regions**. Personal sponsorship carries no platform fee; organisation sponsorship can carry up to 6%. Recipient approval, payment setup and additional terms remain separate. The old matching-fund eligibility ended in **January 2020**; do not advertise it as current. [Official platform rules and regions](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/getting-started-with-github-sponsors/about-github-sponsors)

This audit did not trace a verified Sponsors transfer to a specific completed maintenance artifact. A sponsor button or public tier is not proof of revenue. As a separate example of sponsorship economics, curl says its sponsors support engineer time/infrastructure and explicitly promise no goods or services in return for donations. GitHub's CI sponsorship of curl is not automatically a GitHub Sponsors payment. [curl sponsor terms](https://curl.se/sponsors.html), [donation use](https://curl.se/donation.html)

**Access implication:** recurring support can reduce maintenance costs but can change or disappear. If a recipient promises support/service benefits, their separate commitments need assessment. Project donations held through a fiscal host do not automatically become a maintainer's personal salary. No platform-wide AI exemption overrides project or sponsor terms.

### First window: validate a maintenance claim before writing a funding story

Spend about 6–10 h selecting one precise question from permitted prior work and checking the mature baseline, current roadmap/issue state and contribution rules. For example, the already-researched **PyVRP #1040 quick-benchmark question** remains a carry-forward candidate, not a grant-funded assignment or confirmed NumFOCUS-eligible project. Its prior MIT/code and benchmark-oracle findings are in `contribution-novelty-round9.md`; do not assume current maintainer interest or missing functionality without a launch-time check. [Public issue reference carried forward](https://github.com/PyVRP/PyVRP/issues/1040)

Allocate 12–20 h to a small independent reproducer/oracle, an A/A or negative control, and a comparison with the native tool. Another 6–10 h can establish exact code/data/configuration pins, rights, resource bounds and failure records; use the remaining 6–10 h for an evidence packet and explicit open questions. Fit within the overall cap. New software installs or upstream execution need later authorisation; if not available, the result is a source/fixture design, not a reproduced defect.

The packet should contain:

- An observed problem or clearly labelled untested hypothesis, receiving project and affected users; distinguish an issue request from a maintainer commitment.
- Current native capability and prior art; what remains if the baseline already solves the problem.
- A small failure case with an independently specified expected result, actual result if run, exact versions and limitations.
- Future deliverables with separate acceptance gates: regression test, compatibility change, documentation and handoff; no promise that a reviewer will merge them.
- Rights/provenance and project AI policy; distinguish code/document/data terms, not just repository visibility.
- Hours/resources for **future unfunded work**, review/coordination/reporting overhead and a named-but-unconfirmed receiving authority.
- A prospective milestone/cashflow ledger and fallback if funding or upstream acceptance fails. Prepared work remains prepared work, not a retrospective bill.

### Conditional second window: demonstrate maintainability, not proposal volume

Use 25–40 h for a bounded improvement and independent holdout, 40–65 h for matched validation/negative cases and maintainability work, and 25–45 h for handoff, reporting and a properly scoped future plan, within 90–150 h. This does not authorise external submissions or recruit reviewers. If no independent receiving review is available, state that limitation rather than claiming adoption or mentoring.

Prefer outcomes such as a reproducible regression signal, fewer invalid build artifacts at matched functionality, clearer failure diagnosis, an independently usable test fixture or a smaller release-maintenance burden. Record actual active hours, rejected approaches, native-tool costs and reviewer/coordination effort. Count outputs that fail acceptance. A merged PR, grant award, successful release and paid invoice are four different events.

**AI/native-tool comparison:** automation may lower implementation, documentation and diagnosis costs, while also creating review load. Use it where the project permits and retain provenance/verification. If a mature native tool or AI-assisted workflow removes the residual problem, reduce or abandon the scope; do not inflate work to a fund's minimum. Fixed outcomes and hourly invoices create different obligations: report actual work honestly rather than treating generated volume as earned billable effort.

**AI application boundary:** NLnet's disclosed-authorship rule means the person must write/own any later application and disclose/explain AI assistance as instructed. The present packet is research/preparation, not application text. Other programmes' absent/unstated AI rules do not establish that every use is allowed; project contribution, confidentiality, copyright and fund-specific terms remain separate checks.

## Economics, uncertainty and stop conditions

An advertised award ceiling divided by coding hours is not take-home income. Funds can cover multiple contributors, services, infrastructure and reporting. Selection delay, non-reimbursable preparation, milestone acceptance, invoicing, work outside scope and post-grant maintenance change the economics. Donations are especially different from enforceable purchases. Personal tax/employment/contract treatment was not assessed.

No individual acceptance probability is calculated. Historical recipient counts, funded examples or programme budgets mix different cohorts, project maturity, criteria and applicant self-selection. Even a published historical selection rate would not identify the user's probability or future-cycle funding.

Stop route-specific preparation if the geography/project-affiliation gate fails; rights or employer ownership are unresolved; the work is already funded; upstream does not want the direction; the supposed defect disappears under the native baseline; the only “oracle” is generated by the implementation under test; or funding is needed before even the first validated evidence can be produced. The response can be a smaller, useful artifact or a different route, not another speculative application.

The stronger 2031 path is sustained, attributable responsibility for software others use: diagnosis, compatibility, release quality, documentation and handoff. Funding can buy time for that work, but public-interest value, community authority, grant eligibility, paid delivery and durable income remain separate claims.
