# Delayed independent competence after AI assistance — round 16

Checked 20 October 2026, retaining the 19 October publication cutoff. Existing education-learning, skill-return, human-method reviews and the source index were read first. This pass examines **three primary evidence families**: a delayed software-design pilot, a two-week programming thesis and a professional customer-support outage analysis. The last is an explicitly different learning/withdrawal outcome from a previously cited productivity study. Immediate Trio quizzes, credential visibility and broad education findings are not counted again.

**Direct evidence for durable independent competence in employed software engineers remains sparse in this bounded search.** The studies support measuring assisted output separately from later independent work. They do not establish that AI necessarily erodes skill, that scaffolding guarantees retention, or that a particular learning policy produces five-year career gains. Published outcomes are often shorter, narrower and less decisive than their “long-term” labels suggest.

## Three primary methods audits

| Evidence | Actual delay and independence | Main inference boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Mahmoud et al., *Fast and Fleeting*, 2026 | Similar new OOP design task **2–7 days later**, median 7; no assistive tools; **19 of 21** completed | Direct delayed near-transfer measurement, but tiny student sample and nonsignificant delayed between-group difference |
| Alam, MSc thesis, May 2026; repository 8 June | **Same linked-list problem two weeks later**, supervised no AI; **13 complete cases** | Repeated-task reconstruction, not novel transfer; assisted-to-unassisted decline does not itself identify reduced learning |
| Brynjolfsson/Li/Raymond, published QJE 2025 | AI absent for **minutes/hours during outages**, after continuing months of exposure/practice | Positive professional-learning signal, not months after training/support withdrawal or a software-engineer skill test |

### 1. Software-design near transfer: useful measurement, uncertain harm

[Mahmoud et al.'s author-hosted manuscript](https://sanadlab.org/assets/pdf/MahmoudCHASE2026.pdf) is 11 pages; its [official CHASE 2026 programme](https://conf.researchr.org/details/chase-2026/chase-2026-papers/8/Fast-and-Fleeting-Evaluating-ChatGPT-s-Impact-on-Students-Computational-Thinking-Sk) confirms the conference contribution. The inspected author PDF retains template conference/DOI placeholders, so it is not silently presented as byte-identical to a final publisher version. Participants are university learners, mostly early undergraduates, rather than employed engineers.

The researchers pair on programming experience, self-rated Python proficiency and introductory-course grade, then randomize within nine pairs and separately assign three unmatched participants. There are **10 AI/11 control** initially; one in each arm misses the delayed test, leaving **9/10**. Both groups receive the same brief teaching/videos and model-answer feedback. During learning, AI participants use **GPT-4o** in a cleared researcher account; controls have ordinary web search. Immediate testing and the delayed event-management design task prohibit assistance. The latter is similar to the initial university-information-system task, providing near transfer across domains, not broad software maintenance competence.

The delay is not uniformly one week: participants choose a slot with a minimum two-day gap, median seven. One reports intervening preparation; others report none. Self-report does not establish complete absence of exposure. Two author-graders use a predefined answer key/rubric and report high agreement; assessor blinding is not established in the inspected methods. Abstract-class/method selection and implementation correctness remain proxies for abstraction/decomposition, not a validated comprehensive measure of computational thinking.

The decisive qualification is statistical: AI-assisted learning-task correctness improves, but delayed correctness—reported medians **32.67% AI versus 51.83% control**—is **not significantly different**. All observed completion-time differences are also nonsignificant. These results are compatible with a meaningful disadvantage, a smaller effect or sampling variation; they are not an equivalence test. Within-person category drops compare assisted initial work with harder independent work and cannot alone identify forgetting. The paper's roughly 9%/7% time language concerns fractions of allocated task time; it should not be advertised as a precise professional productivity multiplier.

Full primary methods/results were inspected, including reported attrition and practice. Pages 6–7 were checked as text; page 6 was rendered visually. A Figshare replication link is supplied, but its files/licence were not downloaded or executed. No student logs were accessed.

### 2. Two-week programming repeat: the counterfactual changes the story

[Farhana Alam's University of Saskatchewan thesis](https://harvest.usask.ca/items/89132c8e-a553-44d1-b73e-0d63ef204244), [full primary PDF](https://harvest.usask.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/c8fa5e8b-a161-486c-94f1-654f0951351d/content), has a May 2026 cover and June repository date. This is an exploratory master's thesis, not a large peer-reviewed employee experiment. Relevant methods, score table and limitations were inspected in the 113-page PDF.

Approximately **26–27** students expressed interest; sign-up, pilot, attendance and incomplete-record exclusions leave **13 complete cases**, AI **6**, control **7**. Assignment is described as random, but an allocation/attrition-by-arm flow and pre-assignment programming pretest are absent. All analyzed cases have both sessions; that is not zero attrition from recruitment. Habitual AI use differs markedly between arms. The AI tool is a free ChatGPT web service during September–November 2025 with dynamic GPT-5-class/mini fallback; other AI tools may also have been used. This is not a frozen model intervention.

Participants first solve one linked-list problem in 30 minutes using their chosen language. AI is available only to the assigned arm. Both then debug deliberately faulty code without AI. Two weeks later, they repeat **the identical coding problem**, supervised without AI. The 0–10 rubric combines correctness, efficiency, readability, edge cases and structure. Blind scoring/inter-rater checks and quantified intervening practice are not established for the coding outcome; qualitative coding is by one researcher. Familiarity with a common interview problem and remembering a previous answer can matter. The Watson–Glaser practice quiz is a separate immediate endpoint and is not treated as delayed engineering competence.

Table 5.11, visually inspected, permits a useful arithmetic check. AI mean scores move **9.50 → 6.67**; control **6.00 → 6.00**. Four of six AI participants decline, but the AI arm's delayed mean is still slightly **above** control; medians are **5.5 versus 6.0**. No new inferential test or causal estimate is calculated here. The drop demonstrates that an assisted artifact can exceed later independent reconstruction; it does **not** establish worse learning than a no-AI counterfactual.

Similarly, calling the initial coding-score difference a “baseline competence” confound is misleading: that score is measured **after assignment with AI permitted**, not before treatment. Genuine pre-existing imbalances remain possible, but that post-assignment difference cannot prove them. Prompt styles are self-selected, with very few cases; an explanatory prompt or successful participant does not identify a superior teaching strategy. Same-task retention also does not establish unseen debugging/design transfer.

### 3. Professional outage evidence: positive, but the two clocks differ

[Generative AI at Work](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044), published 4 February 2025 in QJE 140(2), uses technical-support work for a business-software firm. This pass audits **§VI.B/Figure VII and online Appendix E/Table A.VIII**, not its familiar overall productivity headline. A [typeset author copy](https://danielle.li/assets/docs/GenerativeAIatWork.pdf) was retained; the publisher's full 52-page online appendix was readable as primary text. Direct appendix-byte retrieval returned 403, so no local appendix hash or visual-table inspection is invented.

The parent study contains **5,172 agents/approximately three million chats**, September 2019–June 2021; most workers are outside the US, mainly the Philippines. Their assistance is a proprietary GPT-3-based system trained on historical conversations, suggesting responses/documentation while humans remain responsible. The rollout is manager/scheduling/budget constrained, not a randomized permanent-withdrawal experiment. Normal product learning, weekly coaching and work continue. Following recommendations is chosen, not randomized.

Significant outages last minutes to hours and affect some workers depending on server/login conditions. The study compares chat duration around AI onboarding, retaining common pretreatment observations and selecting post-onboarding chats occurring during outages. After more months of prior exposure, workers still handle chats faster while recommendations are unavailable. This is evidence consistent with acquired work routines and a counterexample to inevitable dependency.

However, **months of exposure before an outage are not months without support**. At chat level the authors lack the resolution/quality endpoint needed to confirm that faster outage chats remain equally successful. Table A.VIII's large observation counts include pretreatment chats and are not counts of outage-only learners or independent outages. Exact affected-agent/event counts and independently documented no-suggestion compliance are not fully pinned here. Figure VII and the table show nonflat preperiod patterns; no new joint pretrend test or causal re-estimation is performed. Models control agent, year-month and tenure with agent-clustered errors, which does not automatically resolve outage-specific case mix, staffing, worker survival or selective adherence. The authors themselves call the outage estimates noisy and note possible chat-composition differences.

This is valuable professional longitudinal evidence for possible learning through use. It is not a delayed unfamiliar-code test, clean training-versus-tool withdrawal effect, skill retention after a long break, or an earnings/career return. The outage analysis is another outcome of the **same firm/study**, not an additional independent workplace trial.

## Does scaffolding establish later independence?

Two targeted primary screens prevent overclaiming. [Sankaranarayanan's v2 “Epistemic Debt” preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20206v2) tests an explanation gate, but its AI-blackout repair phase is **immediately** after construction. Its own future-work section says persistence over months is unknown. It is not delayed-transfer evidence, irrespective of stronger “long-term” wording elsewhere. No EEG claim is used as a skill oracle.

[Xiao et al.'s prompting-instruction RCT](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16033v1) has a genuine six/seven-week follow-up, but its primary retained skill is **writing a pedagogical prompt**. The analysis keeps **431/979** complete cases; the delayed codebook section separately says 440 responses. The study reports no significant randomized between-group final-exam difference, while prompting/exam associations are observational. Continued prompting/practice occurs, and a locked-down resource protocol for the final is not established in the inspected text. This screen therefore does not close the employee technical-independence bridge. It supports testing the training architecture rather than assuming “better prompting” is equivalent to better independent engineering.

The already-audited Contractor/Reyes one-week result remains a positive but uncertain retention counterexample; it is not re-counted as new evidence. These mixed findings justify neither “all AI learning gains vanish” nor “scaffolds solve deskilling.” No credible multi-year professional-software competence estimate was established by this bounded search; that is a search/evidence gap, not proof no such study exists.

## Tentative diagnostic within the existing hours

Use the existing **30–45 focused hours in month one, plus 40–70 through day 90** from the education plan. This is one optional diagnostic, not extra hours stacked onto every role project, a learner assessment already performed, or proven career advice. Actual ability, time and resource availability remain unknown.

| Month-one allocation | Proposed task | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| 4–6 h | Diagnose one genuinely unfamiliar prerequisite and freeze the scoring/assistance contract | A target worth studying; no assumed SQL/C++ deficit |
| 8–12 h | Learn using serious worked-example/retrieval baselines and a bounded attempt-first/hint/explanation AI policy | A documented practice process, not an isolated causal mechanism |
| 6–8 h | Controlled practice and independent debugging on the same concept family | Exposure and failure history, rather than tutorial completion alone |
| 4–6 h | Fresh independent tests, scoring and assistance/exposure ledger | Local retained and near-transfer performance |
| 8–13 h | Analyze errors and produce a reproducible case study/handoff | Inspectable engineering evidence with limits |

Choose one existing project: the retained-skill diagnostic, unfamiliar SQL semantics, a data-recovery contract or a narrow compiler/timing prerequisite. Do not start all of them. Require an independent expected result and novel failure case before interpreting a score. Keep separate: repeated-task reconstruction, a fresh same-concept problem, a structurally different transfer problem, docs-allowed debugging without generated solutions, and safe assisted operation. A fluent explanation and a passing visible test are incomplete oracles.

A workable calendar is learning on days 4–7, an immediate check on day 7, then fresh checks around days 14 and 28. **Seven and 21 days are planning choices, not proven optimal intervals.** Record every intervening practice, production encounter, generated answer, tutorial and item/concept exposure. If practice continues, call the result independence under continued practice, not pure retention after withdrawal. Changing a row value or variable name is not automatically new conceptual transfer.

The earlier independent-review warning is essential: if the reader writes/hand-checks the held-out solutions, he has been exposed to them. Use independently prepared, appropriately licensed forms without showing answer keys in advance where actually available. Otherwise label the exercise an **unblinded personal diagnostic**, not an independent efficacy comparison. Do not recruit participants or contact reviewers under this research authorization. A separately authorized reviewer can help later; absence of one remains explicit.

For an AI learning condition, attempt first, ask for a bounded hint, predict a result, explain the causal mechanism and then verify. Record actual model/version, fallback, time and feedback. Compare against a credible documentation/worked-example/retrieval approach at similar time, rather than against no teaching. With one person, even counterbalancing cannot eliminate topic difficulty, order and carryover; report raw cases and failures rather than a population treatment effect.

Use the later 40–70 hours to deepen the **same** artifact, add maintenance/changed-condition tests and repeat an independently assessed slice around days 60/90 while logging continued exposure. If assisted output improves but fresh independent diagnosis does not, change the practice or prerequisite; do not interpret that as lack of personal ability. If foundations improve but the role's degree, experience, language or geography gates remain, the artifact has not removed those barriers. No study here turns this protocol into a salary, job or leadership forecast.

The matrix records source/version pins, the three main outcome designs, excluded/screened endpoints, gaps and the protocol. No learner was assessed; no participant records, model calls, new packages, outreach or shared artifacts were used or changed. Only arithmetic on a published score table was independently executed.
