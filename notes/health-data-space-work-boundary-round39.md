## Different actors and access decisions

Primary use concerns care and related services for the person. Secondary use concerns other ChapterIV purposes. The holder, applicant/user, access body, manufacturer and national contact point are different roles. An EHR-as-a-service offered to a Union-established customer and a non-Union manufacturer placing a product on the Union market raise different questions from a developer's home country. No applicability determination is made about the reader or their employer.

ChapterIV permits certain health research, algorithm training/testing and education uses under its conditions; it does not make any AI project eligible. Some public-interest/policy/statistics purposes are reserved to specified public bodies/mandates. Access assessment includes intended-purpose expertise and suitable technical/organisational safeguards. This is neither a universalPhD requirement nor a waiver of research/clinical qualifications. Marketing, discriminatory decisions and other prohibited uses are excluded. Default holder exemptions for natural persons/microenterprises can be changed by national law; they are not access permissions. Sensitive-data safeguards, opt-out rules and IP/trade-secret protections also matter. [Articles50–54,66–71](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng)

Anonymised and pseudonymised data are not interchangeable. The law prefers an anonymised form when sufficient; justified pseudonymised access retains additional controls and separated reversal information. Removing a name does not prove anonymisation. Permit access occurs through controlled processing with named identities, restricted data, logs, audit and reviewed non-personal outputs. No actual SPE, output-disclosure control or anonymisation test was inspected or run. [Articles61,66,68,73](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng)

Third-country **applicant eligibility** under Article91 is distinct from **authorised infrastructure participation** under75(5). The former includes a Commission reciprocity implementing-act route; the latter is deferred2035. Therefore neither “automatic global access by2029” nor “no possible applicant access before2035” follows. No relevant UK/India act, approved applicant, workright or storage exception was verified. Article87's Union storage/processing rule has an adequacy exception; it is not a verified country-specific permission here. [Articles75,87,91,105](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng)

## Concrete work, and what is still unspecified here

The law names work that a systems/data engineer can understand: versioned exchange specifications and two harmonised EHR components; provenance/access logs; technical documentation and test evidence; dataset catalogues and data-quality descriptions; application/permit lifecycle systems; identity-scoped processing and controlled outputs. These are engineering requirements and interfaces, not evidence that a particular organisation must create new roles.

Three boundaries matter for choosing work:

1. **Standards are dependencies.** Articles15/36/73/77/78 provide for technical instruments, while delegated powers can amend characteristics/label elements. A statutory deadline or empowerment is not an observed adopted specification, draft status or working deployment. No implementing/delegated-act register or current detailed technical release was inspected in this capped pass. Do not invent a mandatory FHIR release, implementation guide, catalogue profile or SPE package.
2. **Native infrastructure is serious counterevidence.** Article40 calls for Commission testing software to be open-source; this is not proof of a released repository, licence or complete test suite. Existing FHIR validators, native profile constraints, SQL, logging/identity services and managed processing platforms may already satisfy substantial parts of an actual contract. The residual opportunity can be integration, support and independently checked meaning, rather than a new general platform.
3. **Duties do not quantify demand.** Articles55/56 require resources;85 connects procurement/funding to specifications. No budget amount, award, tender or headcount was verified. The overview's expected savings/sector growth are expectations, not observed outcomes or employment estimates. Existing bodies and suppliers may absorb or automate work. [Regulation, Articles36–40,55–57,73,77–79,85](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng), [Commission overview](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/european-health-data-space-regulation-ehds_en)

The official Commission overview is a public reference/discovery route. National authorities/access bodies/contact points are mandated classes, not a newly verified directory of employers. The failed programme route leaves its team/funding/deliverables unknown. Existing health12 routes—HAPI/Smile,OHDSI,NRCeS/C-DAC and Oxford/TVS-SDE—remain saved contribution context only. They are not newly verified EHDS implementers, grants or vacancies. No named new public lab or current funded role was established.
