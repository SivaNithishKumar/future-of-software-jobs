# Adoption measurement and economic interpretation

Checked 19 October 2026. Root-owned questions, distinct from labor_market's Census/QWI methods audit.

## Firm exposure evidence has an important population restriction

[BLS WP591 full paper](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/2026/pdf/ec260040.pdf), sections3.3–3.4. Main adoption data use the November2025–February2026 supplement/revised question. Occupational shares come from2018–2020 OEWS. Linking requires surviving, same-EIN firms and restricts to single-location firms to align establishment exposure with firm adoption. Samples are approximately15,500 revised-question and57,000 earlier-question firm-year observations. Propensity weights balance observed industry/size characteristics; they cannot restore excluded multi-unit firms or eliminate unobserved selection. Average firm employment is approximately13 in the firm-weighted sample.

The exposure/adoption relationship is therefore evidence for this linked population, not a validation of job-replacement predictions at large distributed technology employers. The paper itself compares question vintages. Questionnaire scope, occupation-share age, survivor linkage and aggregation should remain visible when citing a generic exposure score. A modest association is neither no information nor an estimate of jobs lost.

## Comparative advantage is a useful framework, not a personal2031 oracle

[Author-hosted working paper,22May2026 version](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/citation_file_upload/LORV2026.pdf); [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35271). Uses2024 German DiWaBe linked data. Task user costs are inferred by fitting observed occupational adoption shares, with worker productivity/pay and AI exposure entering a structural model. Section4.4 evaluates fitted shares and penalized nested-model selection; the headline roughly60% refers to occupation-level variation, not60% of individual decisions or future jobs. The individual-level appendix uses a different pseudo-R². User-cost heterogeneity produces most of the incremental fit; competitive wage-setting, task mappings, distributional assumptions and normalization matter.

This is evidence against equating technical feasibility with realized adoption. It does not establish that changing one inferred cost causally changes employment, or that the fitted German ranking transports to India/UK or stronger2031agents. Held-out future/geographic prediction has not been established in this root pass. Keep the May22 version distinct from later repository metadata; do not claim the latest full NBER revision was inspected.

## Questions these findings create

For an opportunity branch, identify the actual decision, available alternatives, total deployment/review/failure cost, who pays and what changes behavior. A capability benchmark alone cannot fill those inputs. Conversely, a friction today may disappear through native tooling or organizational learning; it is not a permanent human job moat.

For personal preparation, test useful capability and the ability to diagnose unfamiliar failures independently. A good project can falsify its original AI premise by showing a simpler system works better. A career path needs an additional bridge to attainable roles or customers. These are proposed decision principles, not measured returns to a particular course or portfolio.
