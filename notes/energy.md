# Energy, compute infrastructure and climate: developer work through 2031

Evidence cutoff: 19 October 2026. Findings below separate observations, projections and career inferences. This is a sector research module, not a quantified forecast of software vacancies.

## Thesis

The defensible career opportunity is software that makes expensive physical assets more usable, reliable and accountable. The bottleneck is often data access, integration, operational trust and commercial permission rather than a missing ML model. AI infrastructure expands this opportunity, but electricity, building cooling, transport and industry would create much of it even without the largest AI demand forecasts materialising.

## What the primary evidence actually establishes

1. **Electricity growth is broader than AI.** IEA's *Electricity 2026* forecasts global electricity demand growth of 3.6% annually in 2026–2030. Its observed/preliminary 2025 figures show renewables approximately matching coal generation and nuclear output at a record. Renewables plus nuclear reaching half of generation by 2030 is a projection. Nameplate GW, electricity actually generated, peak dependable output, and connection applications must not be conflated. [IEA, February 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/executive-summary).

2. **The current AI energy projection is 950 TWh, not the much-repeated 945 TWh.** IEA's 16 April 2026 update estimates data centres consumed 485 TWh in 2025 and projects around 950 TWh in 2030, approximately 3% of global electricity; AI-focused centres triple. Energy per task has fallen very rapidly, while reasoning/video/agentic task complexity rises. It identifies digital skills, fragmented data and protection/security concerns as adoption barriers, and says only 10% of global electricity consumption is covered by open electricity-data policies. These are scenarios with financing, chip and grid constraints, not inevitable demand. [IEA, 16 April 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary).

3. **There is already a physical asset base to operate.** California ISO says more than 16,000 MW of battery storage has been added since 2020. Its 2026 summer assessment separately distinguishes resources already onboarded from resources expected by June. A battery's MW is instantaneous power; its usable MWh, state of charge and duration determine what it can supply during an actual shortage. Storage software opportunities concern dispatch, degradation, forecasting and settlement, not merely increasing battery sales. [CAISO, 2026 summer assessment](https://www.caiso.com/generation-transmission/resource-adequacy/2026-summer-loads-and-resources-assessment).

4. **Grid underuse can coexist with enormous queues.** IEA's September report identifies 1,700 GW of advanced renewable projects and 600 GW of utility batteries awaiting connection in 2025. It estimates selected grid technologies could connect up to 330 GW without reinforcement; this is modelled potential, not delivered capacity. Crucially, 70% of surveyed operators use AI widely in maintenance but 23% in real-time operations. Reported barriers include skills/readiness (64%), data (60%) and trust (60%). [IEA, 21 September 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/modernising-grids-in-the-age-of-electricity/executive-summary).

5. **Demand flexibility is a real market, with rules.** Britain's NESO expanded its Demand Flexibility Service on 9 April 2026 to bidirectional flexibility, zonal procurement and a 0.1 MW eligibility threshold; the page schedules constraint-management procurement from 7 October. APIs, baselines, metering and eligibility make integration and verified delivery valuable. [NESO, 2026 service documents](https://www.neso.energy/industry-information/balancing-services/demand-flexibility-service-dfs). Google reported 1 GW of demand-response capacity incorporated into utility contracts on 19 March 2026. This demonstrates contracts, not a measured simultaneous 1 GW curtailment; Google explicitly acknowledges location and flexibility limits. [Google, 19 March 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/demand-response-data-center-milestone/).

6. **Pipeline reliability is itself an engineering problem.** PJM's 29 May accuracy report compares forecast large loads with operational observations, explicitly flags zones lacking historical large-load data, and suppresses some customer-identifiable data. A connection queue cannot be treated as realised future consumption. Forecasting requires project identity, commitment status, timing and ramp, not simply summing announced MW. [PJM, 29 May 2026](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/load-forecast/2026-load-forecast-accuracy-report.pdf).

7. **Chip infrastructure is producing, not merely announcing.** TSMC's 15 October Q3 results report US$46.72 billion revenue and 4.476 million 12-inch-equivalent wafer shipments. Future fab plans remain conditional; capital intensity does not imply proportional generalist software hiring. Inference serving, compiler/kernel optimisation, networking, scheduling and reliability are more accessible software routes than semiconductor device research, but demand deeper systems skills. [TSMC Q3 presentation, 15 October 2026](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/qr/phase4_reports/2026-10/6996be551d904f126c5521bd349b85be71cea0d6/3Q26%20Presentation%20%28E%29_WoG.pdf).

8. **Water is a local constraint hidden by global averages.** Berkeley Lab estimated 66 billion litres of direct US data-centre water consumption in 2023; electricity generation adds a separate indirect footprint. Its projections depend on cooling, locations, hardware and utilisation. Site water consumption differs from withdrawal and cannot be inferred from generic per-prompt claims. [Berkeley Lab, 19 December 2024, pp.55–58](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/lbnl-2024-united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report_1.pdf).

9. **Adaptation need is not a software budget.** UNEP estimates developing-country adaptation needs of US$310–365 billion annually by 2035 versus US$26 billion international public flows in 2023. Climate-risk software, flood/heat modelling, infrastructure monitoring and water optimisation have real needs, but a funding gap is not an addressable SaaS market. Buyers must have a budget and a decision that the product improves. [UNEP, 29 October 2025](https://www.unep.org/en/publications/report/adaptation-gap-report-2025).

10. **Nuclear progress and fusion progress have different meanings.** NRC approved TerraPower's Kemmerer construction permit on 4 March 2026; that is not an operating licence or electricity production. [NRC, 4 March 2026](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/news-releases/2026/nrc-issues-first-commercial-reactor-construction-approval-10-years-terrapower-wyoming). LLNL explicitly states fusion ignition has not yielded more energy than the facility consumes; a power plant requires continuous net gain and high repetition. Research software and controls jobs can grow before commercial power, but abundant fusion electricity by 2031 is an unsuitable base-case career assumption. [LLNL, current energy-security explanation](https://lmf.llnl.gov/science/energy-security).

## Three less-obvious opportunities, with tests that could disprove them

**A. Build the evidence layer between sensors, models and accountable operators.** Asset identity, telemetry quality, imagery inspection, maintenance triage, lineage and replayable validation are plausible nearer-term roles for an AI/robotics engineer. Begin with supervised maintenance/planning workflows, where adoption is already higher. Counterevidence: integration may remain bespoke consulting rather than scalable product revenue. Falsifier: buyers cannot supply accessible telemetry or fund deployments, or incumbents package the integration adequately. Test with a real operator dataset and measurable avoided inspection/rework, not a chatbot demonstration.

**B. Optimise usable compute capacity under power limits, rather than sell carbon dashboards.** Deadline-aware scheduling, queue admission, model routing, power telemetry and coordination with batteries/flexible connections can let the same electrical connection produce more accepted work. This joins distributed systems with constrained optimisation. Counterevidence: interactive workloads, high GPU utilisation, training checkpoint costs and limited geographic substitutability reduce flexibility; only part of demand can move. Falsifier: measured savings/connection benefit cannot exceed SLA penalties and integration cost under representative traces. Measure completed useful work per MW and cost per accepted outcome, not tokens alone. Improving joules per token does not determine total consumption: task volume and task complexity can rise faster. Do not extrapolate one year's efficiency trend for five years.

**C. Treat project-pipeline credibility and physical-risk decisions as data products.** Reconcile sites, permits, utility commitments, construction evidence, ramp assumptions, weather exposure and asset vulnerabilities. This could support capacity planning or adaptation procurement. Counterevidence: commercially sensitive data and fragmented jurisdictions impede access, and a risk score without an actionable decision has little value. Falsifier: it cannot outperform a simple staged-commitment baseline, cannot be independently audited, or no buyer will change a funded planning/maintenance action because of it.

## What the work could look like in 2031

Career inference: fewer isolated model demos; more engineers combining time-series/GIS/imagery data, deterministic constraints, optimisation, operational security and user-facing decision tools. The reachable roles are energy data/ML engineer, infrastructure solutions engineer, inference performance engineer, storage/EV integration engineer, reliability/OT-security engineer and climate-risk data engineer. Core grid protection, nuclear safety and device physics need additional specialist grounding. Strong portfolio work uses public grid/weather data, uncertainty bounds, backtesting and failure simulation, then earns access to real telemetry through a domain partner. Capital announcements cannot establish how many vacancies will exist, and these sources do not quantify that.
