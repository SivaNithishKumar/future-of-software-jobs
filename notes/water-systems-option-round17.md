# Water-system software: validate the sensing/model before classifying a leak

Owner: robotics_physical. Checked 20 October 2026. Draft for independent review; not self-accepted. This is a narrow extension to `climate-water-round3.md` and the existing `energy-public-round6` records, not another water-sector survey. Existing reports, catalogues and trees are unchanged.

The defensible developer exercise is a **sensor/model-validity and ambiguity gate** ahead of leak triage. It connects time-series engineering, units, graph models, numerical failure handling and operator-facing evidence. A high anomaly score alone cannot establish a leak, its location, or money saved. This is a conditional option through 2031, with no claim about job growth, vacancy prevalence, personal readiness or a new commercial moat.

Only public source files, small configuration text and primary pages were read. No network model, SCADA workbook, image, model weights or data corpus was downloaded. No upstream code, simulator, MATLAB toolkit or package was run. The executed work consists of an independently authored standard-library arithmetic/source-check script. Its results and the 21 retained source texts accompany this note.

## The precise gap and counterevidence

The earlier report already covers EPANET/WNTR, BattLeDIM, utility repair workflows and a generic leak-investigation project. This pass adds four source-level questions: what observations identify a cause; which hydraulic/leak semantics a chosen engine implements; whether an apparent successful run covers the promised horizon; and what the benchmark's cost score actually charges.

A deliberately simple counterexample makes the first question falsifiable. A source has total head 50 m and the demand junction elevation is 10 m. Choose a toy pipe with headloss `100000 Q²`, with Q in m³/s. At demand 0.010 m³/s, inlet flow is 0.010 and pressure head is 30 m. A 0.001 m³/s leak gives inlet flow 0.011 and pressure head 27.9 m. An otherwise identical network with legitimate demand 0.011 and no leak gives **exactly those same two observations**. A pressure-only bias of −2.1 m also reproduces the pressure change, though a trusted independent flow sensor can separate that particular bias. These are existence counterexamples, not a prevalence estimate or a claim every real leak is unidentifiable. Demand bounds, additional measurements or independently confirmed events can resolve particular cases.

This is not a new objection to all model-based detection. [Vrachimis, Eliades and Polycarpou, 8 March 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/dwes-11-19-2018), report bounded-state analysis on actual Cyprus transport-network measurements. Their modified case has nine measured demand nodes, known topology and assumed uncertainty bounds; the detection example adds an artificial leak and assumes extra sensors. The real-data section finds an apparent imbalance compatible with uncertainty. This supports using bounded consistency checks while limiting claims about confirmed field-leak detection, sparse customer-demand observability or utility savings. The final paper is CC-BY-3.0; linked data rights were not inspected.

[Vrachimis, Timotheou, Eliades and Polycarpou, May 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conengprac.2021.104755), describe interval model invalidation for leak detection/localisation. The publisher abstract explicitly treats sparse hydraulic inversion as underdetermined and combines prior bounds with pressure/flow evidence. Only primary abstract/metadata were accessible here; no full-method reproduction, code grant or theorem verification is claimed. A proposed interval baseline is existing prior art, not a novelty claim.

### Hydraulic engines have different contracts

Pinned [`documentation/hydraulics.rst`](https://github.com/USEPA/WNTR/blob/11eec7d5bf0a9367ffe04d75707918f0c0a890a1/documentation/hydraulics.rst), lines 15–18, 52–54, 121–128, 270–298 and 300–323, makes these differences explicit:

- EpanetSimulator defaults to EPANET 2.2.0; its older 2.00.12 option has different demand support. WNTRSimulator supports demand-driven and pressure-dependent demand but does not solve water-quality transport. It only uses Hazen–Williams headloss.
- WNTRSimulator can override pressure-demand parameters per junction. EpanetSimulator's relevant parameters are global. Requested demand and delivered flow are different quantities under low-pressure PDD conditions.
- WNTR's separate leak representation and EPANET emitters are different interfaces. Emitter coefficients in the model are ignored by WNTRSimulator. WNTR's documented leak exponent is fixed at 0.5; gauge pressure head, area and discharge assumptions matter.

Therefore a wrapper called “EPANET compatible” is insufficient provenance for a residual baseline. The task must fix engine, headloss, demand mode, pressure convention and leak representation. The quadratic toy above is **not** a Hazen–Williams numerical reference for WNTR. An extended-period hydraulic model is also not an acoustic, water-hammer, treatment-process or wastewater-drainage simulator.

### Nonconvergence is a first-class output

[`wntr/sim/epanet.py`](https://github.com/USEPA/WNTR/blob/11eec7d5bf0a9367ffe04d75707918f0c0a890a1/wntr/sim/epanet.py), lines 68 and 97–101, and [`core.py`](https://github.com/USEPA/WNTR/blob/11eec7d5bf0a9367ffe04d75707918f0c0a890a1/wntr/sim/core.py), lines 1191–1209 and 1287–1323, document optional raising versus warnings and partial output. [`results.py`](https://github.com/USEPA/WNTR/blob/11eec7d5bf0a9367ffe04d75707918f0c0a890a1/wntr/sim/results.py), lines 7–9, encodes error as zero. Do not interpret a truthy result object or a falsey integer error code as a complete successful horizon. Native warning/exception safeguards already exist; the concern is downstream ingestion preserving them, not an allegation that WNTR silently certifies success. Even a non-error result needs expected-time/sensor coverage checked.

The historical [generator](https://github.com/KIOS-Research/BattLeDIM/blob/ea81f544debb21587e09c32801ce605f8305720a/Dataset%20Generator/dataset_generator.py), lines 196–202, passes `mode=` into WNTRSimulator, then checks an empty pressure table and result-object truthiness. Current WNTR constructor accepts only the network, with demand mode set in options (`core.py`, lines 606–610). This is a static compatibility mismatch, **not a failed historical benchmark result or an executed exception**. No old environment was reconstructed.

### BattLeDIM is a deliberate same-network benchmark

The [organisers](https://battledim.ucy.ac.cy/) describe hypothetical L-Town and generated SCADA based on a real Cyprus network, with 2018 historical data and 2019 evaluation. Its paper is Vrachimis et al., *Battle of the Leakage Detection and Isolation Methods*, J. Water Resources Planning and Management 148(12), 04022068 (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)WR.1943-5452.0001601). Metadata and protocol/source were inspected; the full benchmark paper's appendix was not audited here.

Independent counting of the pinned [historical](https://github.com/KIOS-Research/BattLeDIM/blob/ea81f544debb21587e09c32801ce605f8305720a/Dataset%20Generator/dataset_configuration_historical.yalm) and [evaluation](https://github.com/KIOS-Research/BattLeDIM/blob/ea81f544debb21587e09c32801ce605f8305720a/Dataset%20Generator/dataset_configuration_evaluation.yalm) configurations finds 14 and 23 leak rows, identical 33 pressure / 3 flow / 1 level / 82 AMR lists, and four common pipe IDs continuing across the year boundary. These are **repository generator-config facts**, not independent verification of every published workbook or `.mat` label. The design measures future-time performance on familiar topology, which is legitimate. It does not test unseen-utility transfer, and public test labels now require a separate untouched portfolio test if methods are tuned on them.

The generator converts leak/inlet flow to m³/h but AMR demand to L/h (`dataset_generator.py`, lines 216–220, 248–252, 265–283). One nearby comment labels both as m³/h; the arithmetic and output sheet names differ by 1000. Verify the selected file's actual schema rather than inherit that comment. It also rounds several outputs; these source operations do not prove all noise/clock semantics of the released data.

The native [scoring parameters and dispatcher](https://github.com/KIOS-Research/BattLeDIM/blob/ea81f544debb21587e09c32801ce605f8305720a/Scoring%20Algorithm/Scoring_Algorithm.m), lines 33–35 and 87–126, sort detections chronologically, choose an eligible nearby leak, ignore already recognised repeated detections with zero score, and charge false detections €500. The [scoring function](https://github.com/KIOS-Research/BattLeDIM/blob/ea81f544debb21587e09c32801ce605f8305720a/Scoring%20Algorithm/functions/scoring_function.m), lines 34–67, uses a pipe-centre network-length distance, a 300 m cutoff, remaining five-minute leak volume × €0.80/m³, and a distance-scaled crew proxy. Exact-pipe distance zero gives zero crew proxy. This is useful benchmark design, not observed repair labour, all repeated dispatches, detection-to-repair delay or marginal utility cost. The owned six-sample illustration yields −€245.20 at distance 150 m; it is not a benchmark result.

## One bounded no-new-hardware project

This project is an alternative to the earlier larger leak-prioritisation project, **not another simultaneous commitment**. Planning allowances are 30–40 focused hours in 30 days and 80–120 total in 90 days; available user time/hardware and a domain reviewer are unknown. Begin with a 6–8 hour launch gate. No installs or downloads are authorised by this report.

**Launch gate:** reproduce the accompanying owned counterexample, unit conversion, bounded balance and deliberately partial-record rejection. Specify features available at decision time, pressure head versus total head, m³/s versus m³/h versus L/h, instantaneous versus interval-averaged samples, and an “unresolved cause” label. Have one actual feature/label/clock fixture before scheduling the larger experiment. The executed script provides arithmetic/source checks only; no time-series detector is implemented.

**30-day proposal:** use an owned single-pipe quadratic system, six named event families (healthy, legitimate demand change, leak, pressure bias, stuck/missing reading, resistance change). Freeze exactly three regimes: training `D=0.010, R=100000`; validation `D=0.008, R=120000`; test `D=0.012, R=80000`. Each regime contains all six families and one 24-hour trace sampled every five minutes, for **18 traces / 5,184 rows**. Event onset is hour 8 in training, hour 10 in validation, hour 12 in test; step events persist for six hours. Include the separate exact leak/demand observational pair as a mandatory ambiguity fixture. These are regime/event-time holdouts on one authored topology, not new-pipe, new-network or real-sensor generalisation. Sensor noise distributions and additional weather/pump effects are outside this first manifest.

Compare an explicit mass/energy consistency envelope and a persistence/EWMA change flag; do not require a classifier when the oracle is ambiguous. Fit/calibrate only on training/validation. A valid gate should reject malformed clocks/units and incomplete horizons, report a change without falsely proving its cause, and show when a trusted extra demand observation resolves the ambiguity. The closed-form equation and independent balance—not a second run of the same simulator—are the oracle. All thresholds, aggregation and any excluded rows must be recorded.

Report all 18 traces, event episodes and time coverage, false leak declarations per sensor-day, delay at five-minute resolution, missed changes, abstentions and invalid/partial-input counts. Set a hard acceptance rule of **zero confident leak-cause declarations on the exact ambiguity pair**, zero accepted partial/unit-invalid fixtures and no hidden test retuning. Do not compute confidence intervals from thousands of correlated rows or invent a commercial false-alarm tolerance. Keep the authored traces/outputs below 5 MB and any later CPU stage below 60 seconds per trace; runtime is a proposed cap, not measured here. Stop if those gates cannot be met within the launch allocation.

**90-day conditional extension:** only after explicit installation/dependency approval, pin the actual WNTR build and loaded EPANET engine, then create a tiny owned network rather than start with a full benchmark archive. Check native flow balance and the chosen headloss law; compare DD requested flow with PDD delivery under declared pressure settings. Match leak representations before interpreting cross-engine differences. Use exception/warning plus full time/sensor coverage checks. Reserve a newly specified topology and demand/parameter regime before claiming that transfer; no such second-network split currently exists.

BattLeDIM replay is optional and separately gated on the selected-file licence, bounded byte delivery, one real schema/label fixture and a compatible source environment. Keep its native scorer beside explicit event/coverage/false-investigation metrics. Original 2019 labels, if consulted, make that portion development/evaluation history rather than a fresh untouched test. A model-comparison extension cannot establish real leak confirmation, repair savings or a safe control action. A real read-only utility study additionally needs authorised telemetry, calibrated topology/valves, instrument specifications and independently confirmed investigations/repairs, including negative investigations; no partner is secured.

**Kill or narrow the claim** if sensor/demand assumptions make the cause unidentifiable; a gain requires unavailable true demand or future repair labels; incomplete/nonconvergent runs disappear from the denominator; units, averaging or demand/leak semantics explain the result; selected data rights/build compatibility remain unresolved; or installed tools already meet the user's decision need. Do not expand into treatment quality, sewer spill control or live valve/pump operation to rescue this small experiment.
