# Learning, credentials, visibility and durable returns: a causal audit

Primary methods checked 20 October 2026; publication cutoff remains 19 October. This is preparation research for the reader, whose current skill evidence, education, tenure, time, work rights and financial constraints remain unknown. Five full primary documents were inspected. Earlier reports and role snapshots remain frozen.

The evidence supports diagnosing the bottleneck before choosing a course, credential or contribution. Instruction can improve measured skill; credible signals can change screening; placement services can improve an individual's position; some training-and-support packages raise earnings for years. Those results identify different interventions. None estimates the return to buying a particular course for an employed Indian AI engineer, or a guaranteed route to leadership by 2031.

## The chain and its missing bridges

**Access → participation → learning → retained transfer → credible evidence → screening/access → sustained useful work → earnings net of total cost.**

A failure at one link should change the intervention. More instruction cannot directly fix an immigration restriction. A better profile cannot substitute for a missing foundational skill. A skill increase can coexist with temporarily lower employment, and an earnings increase need not mean more people employed. A successful participant can also move ahead of someone else rather than expand the number of opportunities.

| Intervention | The comparison needed | What to measure separately |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction | Comparable people offered different access to learning | Uptake, completion, independent performance, delayed transfer |
| Credential or visibility | Comparable competence with different credible information shown | Attention, screening, actual starts, reporting behavior |
| Placement/network access | Comparable candidates offered different search/matching support | Search behavior, placement, retention, untreated competitors |
| A complete career program | Comparable eligible applicants offered the package | Total earnings, hours, employment, costs and persistence |
| Personal exploration | A predefined unfamiliar problem and an independent acceptance test | What was learned, assistance, failure recovery, interest and feasibility |

The final row is a decision aid. A small personal before/after exercise is not a causal estimate of labor-market returns.

## 1. Credential visibility: a real intervention, with narrower outcomes than the headline

[Athey and Palikot, *The Value of Non-Traditional Credentials in the Labor Market*, canonical arXiv v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00247v2), registered 5 November 2025, cover 6 November. Completers were randomized in monthly, domain/country/education strata to a sharing nudge plus simpler LinkedIn integration. Nonvisitors remain assigned treatment. Table 3 uses **36,946** profiles; “new job” includes internal promotions and relies on profile reports, not payroll or earnings. The paper's approximate full population is 880,000; table-specific samples differ. About 90% of initially supplied profiles remained accessible; recovery by arm needs clarification.

| Table 3 outcome | Effect, percentage points | SE, percentage points |
|---|---:|---:|
| Credential shared | 2.887 | 0.400 |
| New position, ≥1 month | 1.020 | 0.452 |
| In-scope position, ≥1 month | 1.198 | 0.397 |
| New position, ≥4 months | 0.620 | 0.329 |

The longer-window reporting check is positive but its any-position estimate does not exclude zero at 5% under normal arithmetic. Profile-length balance does not measure all search behavior. The text contradicts itself about whether URL provision preceded assignment. Learned heterogeneity and IV estimates require additional assumptions; LLM resume ratings are simulated evaluations, not recruiter responses. These qualifications preserve useful evidence for visibility without identifying training returns. Methods: printed pp. 13–28.

The [original AEA registration](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/9438), first public **3 August 2022**, predates the intervention. It lists sharing, new jobs, promotions, internships and pay increases; it planned a survey and a 10,000-profile subsample. The published design expanded its batches/sample and emphasizes different available endpoints. Registration does not automatically make every later machine-learning subgroup or resume-scoring analysis prespecified. The public history/analysis-plan reconciliation remains incomplete. “Registered” should not become a blanket validation of every reported result.

**Independent interpretation.** The most defensible causal object is the effect of offering this visibility package among the analyzed, already-qualified learners, subject to selection and measurement assumptions. The effect of actually sharing needs exclusion and compliance assumptions: encouragement might also affect confidence or job-search effort. A stronger first stage does not prove exclusion. A positive result does not establish that the credential contains new information about underlying competence, that employers read it, or that a newly reported position represents a transition out of unemployment. In particular, a low rate of new positions is not a high unemployment rate.

For the reader, this supports making existing legitimate evidence easy to inspect. It supplies no reason to purchase another course merely to create a badge. Whether a technical case study, maintained contribution or credential produces better access for a particular target role is still an empirical question.

## 2. Actual course access: learning gains, participation friction and mixed work outcomes

[Aramburu, Goicoechea and Mobarak, World Bank WP9721, June 2021](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/670761624977598623/pdf/Coding-Bootcamps-for-Female-Digital-Employment-Evidence-from-an-RCT-in-Argentina-and-Colombia.pdf). **803 eligible women** in Argentina/Colombia entered a sequential scholarship lottery; **402** received offers. Tuition subsidies were 65%/80%; **283** accepted, and **241 of 285** total enrollees graduated. About one-third of controls took alternative courses. Follow-up: **724**, response **93.3% treatment / 87.0% control**.

Adjusted offer effects: skill test **+1.119/10** (SE .182), coding employment **+6.7pp** (SE 3.1), overall employment **−7.2pp** (SE 2.9); no clear earnings gain. The four-minute, eight-question/provider-designed quiz was administered **May–June 2020**; employment recalled **February**, 2–5 months after completion. Those clocks differ.

Table 11's coding-employment multiplicity-adjusted **p=.061**. Table 10 has Lee-bound endpoints whose intervals include zero, despite stronger narrative wording. Its test-score scale is 0–8 versus 0–10 in the main table; do not merge those estimates without reconciliation. Skills evidence is stronger than every employment claim. Completion-defined IV requires exclusion despite partial attendance and substitution. Methods pp. 7–18, robustness pp. 24–25; Tables 5/7 read, Tables 10/11 also rendered.

**Independent interpretation.** This is an offer of a subsidized, selected package against available alternatives. It does not isolate a pure knowledge channel from financial support, curriculum, peers, signaling or job-search preparation. Comparing actual graduates with controls who chose other courses conditions on behavior after assignment; it cannot by itself establish that a paid course is better than a free one. Differential attrition can remain consequential even when observed baseline variables look balanced. Bounds depend on assumptions, and statistical significance at a 10% threshold should not be described as certainty at 5%.

The practical lesson is to inspect completion feasibility and independent transfer alongside the headline placement rate. A denominator of graduates can conceal substantial attrition from initial offers or applicants. Assessing a program requires the full funnel and the counterfactual options, including self-study and courses people would take anyway.

## 3. Long-run earnings: a package can help, and its effect can change

[Yusim et al., MDRC, *Effects of Sector-Focused Training After 10 Years*, September 2025](https://www.mdrc.org/sites/default/files/WorkAdvance_10-Year_Report_MDRC.pdf). WorkAdvance randomized **2,564** screened, low-income unemployed/low-wage adults to services or community alternatives across four providers. The package included sector training, placement and advancement support. NDNH outcomes cover UI jobs, excluding contract/self-employment; supplemental IRS data cannot supply all missing earnings amounts.

Year-10 confirmatory earnings: St. Nicks **+$8,580**, **p=.005**, N=479; its **+7pp** above $45,000 has **p=.091**, a weaker threshold. IT provider Per Scholas: **+$1,971, p=.489**, N=690, after earlier gains. Exploratory pooled earnings **+$2,089, p=.074**; any-work **−0.6pp, p=.741**. Year 10 spans 2021–23 by cohort. These are site-specific offer effects, not random assignment across career fields.

Cumulative earnings were added after observing results, require cross-source scaling/interim imputation, and have no significance tests. They cannot replace prespecified endpoints. Tables retain all randomized participants; arm-specific linkage diagnostics were not located in this long-term report. Neither wage rates, hours nor current sectors are directly observed. Methods pp. 3–9; Tables 2/3/6; Appendix B. [Official report page](https://www.mdrc.org/work/publications/effects-sector-focused-training-after-10-years).

**Independent interpretation.** Persistence needs observation, rather than extrapolation from a promising first year. Earnings can rise through hours, weeks, job quality or wage rates; an unchanged employment rate does not identify which mechanism changed. A package effect cannot reveal whether a certificate, teacher, employer relationship or post-placement support produced it. Site differences mix populations, implementation and labor markets. They do not establish that one domain is better for the reader, or that AI caused fading effects.

A course buyer should therefore ask what continues after graduation: feedback, a credible route to work, a way to handle failures, and opportunities to acquire the next skill. These are mechanisms worth testing, not components whose individual returns this trial identifies.

## 4. Recognition and hard filters can matter even with identical written experience

[Deming et al., AER 106(3), March 2016](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20141757); detailed methods inspected in the [accepted manuscript](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/91512/1/Yuchtman_The-value-of-postsecondary.pdf). **10,492 fictitious resumes**, five US metropolitan markets, business/health vacancies, April–November 2014. Credentials varied within vacancies while comparable experience/templates were assigned randomly; errors cluster by vacancy.

Accepted-manuscript Table 5: online for-profit BA **−2.13pp callbacks**, SE **.58pp**, against a 9.1% nonselective-public baseline, N=4,100. Credential differences vary by job requirements and external licensing signals. Callback is personalized contact, not necessarily an interview, offer, wage or productivity. Employer expectations are measured; actual instruction/competence is held outside the treatment. US institution categories and 2014 screening do not transfer automatically to modern Indian AI roles. [Published replication pointer](https://doi.org/10.3886/E113033V1); package not downloaded or executed.

**Independent interpretation.** Evidence against a universal “credentials are worthless” claim is compatible with evidence against indiscriminate credential buying. Recognition is specific to the evaluator and the role. A formal qualification can remove an explicit gate while another badge supplies little incremental information. An accepted manuscript's exact coefficient should also not be silently presented as a freshly replicated final-journal result.

For current preparation, distinguish a hard gate from a preference, a reliable external assessment from a completion badge, and a credible technical demonstration from a claim of expertise. The existing 15-role audit is illustrative and frozen; it can inform those distinctions but cannot estimate market-wide credential returns.

## 5. Placement gains and total opportunity are different outcomes

[Crépon et al., NBER18597, December 2012](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18597/w18597.pdf), later [QJE 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjt001). First, **235** French local markets were assigned offer saturation 0/25/50/75/100%; then eligible young, educated job seekers were randomized within markets. This identifies direct comparisons and local spillovers under partial-interference assumptions.

For followed cohorts, **29,636** selected for survey, **21,431** recovered at eight months; principal unemployed-at-assignment analysis **11,806**, based on retrospective status. Overall take-up **35%**, higher among unemployed candidates. Employment came from follow-up surveys rather than linked tax payroll; errors cluster by local area. Direct short-run gains were partly offset by disadvantages for untreated competitors and faded. Sparse implementation at early/late cohorts motivated exclusions; financial-crisis timing and labor-market boundaries matter. Study methods pp. 12–18 and externality analysis should accompany the aggregate interpretation.

**Independent interpretation.** The experiment is placement assistance, not coding instruction. It demonstrates why individual advantage cannot simply be multiplied into national job creation. It does not show that every certificate or original contribution displaces someone, nor that more productive workers never expand demand. Employer demand, new tasks, firm entry and output must be measured to distinguish those mechanisms.

## What the combined evidence changes

The useful preparation target is a package of **real capability, credible evidence and attainable access**. These are complements with distinct failure modes. Weak independent performance calls for better learning and feedback. Strong performance hidden behind unclear documentation calls for better presentation. A hard qualification or location filter calls for a deliberate access decision. Strong credentials followed by poor retention calls for a different learning design. Neither an attractive course nor a polished portfolio proves all three components.

Original work and formal study also solve different problems. A well-scoped reproduction can demonstrate careful execution. A documented counterexample or maintained fix can demonstrate judgment. An original research contribution additionally requires a prior-art distinction, a credible baseline, a clean test and independent scrutiny. A degree may offer structured depth, supervision and eligibility. A short credential may provide a useful assessment or a recognized screening signal. None should be labelled “original research,” “expertise” or “leadership” merely because it took time to complete.

| Diagnosed bottleneck | Revealing low-cost test | What could justify a larger commitment | Revision trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing knowledge | Explain a mechanism, then solve an unseen failure with normal documentation | Structured instruction and feedback address a demonstrated gap | Tests improve only on rehearsed examples |
| Weak independent judgment | Audit an intentionally wrong solution and defend the correction | Supervised practice with a stronger oracle | Fluent explanations fail runnable checks |
| Unclear evidence | Make one permitted artifact reproducible and its limits explicit | A contribution or assessment adds independently inspectable evidence | More presentation yields no stronger technical proof |
| Qualification filter | Map a specific target's required versus preferred credentials | Formal study removes an actual gate at acceptable total cost | Requirement is merely assumed, or equivalent experience is accepted |
| Weak access | Establish actual location, work-right and entry-channel conditions | A program supplies a reachable route and credible support | Eligibility or employer connections remain vague |
| No sustained benefit | Track accepted work, failures and operating burden over time | Further depth improves a measured constraint | Initial gains disappear or depend on permanent rescue |

“Low cost” here means bounded use of an existing approved environment; actual money/time costs are unknown. This is not an invitation to enroll, purchase, install, contact employers or publish private work.

## A 30-day exploration that can change the decision

**Days 1–3: choose one testable gap.** Select one branch problem accessible with existing resources: a backend recovery failure, an ML experiment with leakage risk, or a robotics log/timing problem. Define the unfamiliar task, expected outcome, error cost and acceptance oracle before studying it. Inventory what is already independently demonstrated. Choose one main project rather than attempting all domains simultaneously.

**Days 4–14: learn and reproduce.** Use primary documentation and one bounded learning resource. Reproduce a known reference behavior and a real counterexample. Log time spent learning, implementing, checking and repairing; record assistance. Keep the expected results separate from the implementation. A generated implementation grading its own tests is weak evidence.

**Days 15–21: test transfer.** Change the data, failure mode or environment without changing the underlying concept. Later explain and repair an unfamiliar case with ordinary reference material, while withholding a generated answer to the target exercise. Separately test assisted operating skill. A delay of a week is a practical proposal, not a scientifically optimal schedule established by these studies.

**Days 22–30: make the evidence inspectable.** Produce a compact case study: problem, baseline, independent oracle, actual results, adverse cases, assistance and remaining limits. Use synthetic/public material with appropriate rights; private employer work needs permission. Check fit against a few already identified role requirements, separating unknowns. No publication, application or outreach is performed by this research.

The decision at day 30 is whether the chosen investment improved independent transfer, remained feasible and exposed an interesting problem. A badge earned or an attractive demo alone does not pass that gate. Lack of progress can justify a better instructor or narrower prerequisite; it does not establish lack of ability.

## A 90-day extension toward credible depth

For days 31–60, extend the same system through new conditions, regressions and recovery. Keep the original baseline and untouched cases. If there is a genuine defect or missing capability in a maintained public project, prepare a narrow local contribution with reproducible evidence. Acceptance, publication or collaboration remains a separate outcome. Use authorized real users or hardware only where access and operating conditions are established.

For days 61–90, test whether another person could understand and operate the artifact from its instructions, subject to separately authorized review. Track maintenance burden, accepted outcomes and failures. Explain at least one tempting approach that the evidence rejected. A research extension needs a defensible novelty claim; an engineering extension can be valuable without novelty if it reliably removes a real constraint.

Then choose between deeper self-study, supervised instruction, a formal qualification, a maintained contribution or a different branch. Compare the total commitment: tuition, time, foregone alternatives, hardware, feedback and access. No study here supplies a numerical break-even calculation for the reader. Leadership requires sustained judgment, useful adoption and supporting other people over time; it is not a ninety-day certificate outcome.

## Falsifiers and unresolved evidence

- If apparent learning disappears on delayed/unseen tasks, revise the method before accumulating more credentials.
- If competence is demonstrated but presentation is opaque, improve the evidence before assuming another course is needed.
- If the target route's hard eligibility cannot be met, redirect the entry path or explicitly price the qualification/relocation decision.
- If a program's placement claim omits applicants, noncompleters, comparison outcomes or costs, its marketing cannot establish incremental returns.
- If original work has no prior-art distinction, retain it as reproduction or engineering evidence rather than inventing research novelty.
- If actual work improves but access does not, investigate signaling, targeting and institutional constraints; do not infer the learning had no value.
- If early gains fade or support burden dominates, investigate adaptation and ongoing supervision; do not extrapolate a short test to five years.

The important remaining research would link randomized or otherwise credible training access to delayed independent skill, verified starts, earnings, costs and untreated competitors. A factorial design separating instruction, assessment, visibility and employer access would be stronger than a single package evaluation for identifying mechanisms. Personal exploration can identify the next sensible experiment while that larger evidence remains incomplete.
