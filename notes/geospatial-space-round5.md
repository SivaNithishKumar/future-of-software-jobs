# Geospatial, Earth observation and selected space-software paths

Research cut-off: 19 October 2026. This pass covers optical/SAR/time-series analysis, geolocation, data fusion, labels, operational data delivery, and selected onboard/simulation roles. It extends the institution-only space scan and field-robotics report without repeating their evidence. Public documents, papers, repositories and model/data cards were inspected. No datasets or weights were downloaded, software installed, paid compute run, private API accessed, systems controlled or people contacted. All proposed projects remain unexecuted. Previously reviewed reports are unchanged.

## Finding and 2031 inference

The credible developer opportunity is to turn measurements into **a reproducible, timely and reviewable decision product**: a crop/land-management screening result, flood extent with uncertainty and acquisition time, or a reliable image-processing pipeline. There are already operating buyers. Training a novel Earth foundation model is one route, with substantial compute and research barriers; it is not a prerequisite for useful work.

By 2031, a plausible geospatial software role combines Python/SQL/data engineering with coordinate systems, observation dates, sensor physics, spatial validation and domain-specific error costs. Inference: cheaper general representations will reduce the value of a generic image-classification wrapper while making good labels, integration, availability guarantees and honest exceptions more valuable. This is not proof of rising total headcount or a standalone startup market. Existing GIS vendors, public services, satellite operators and domain incumbents can absorb the opportunity.

Two distinct entry lanes should remain separate. Ground-based data/platform work can build on an experienced developer's existing skills; onboard and flight software requires embedded/runtime expertise and access to target hardware and mission validation. An offline notebook is evidence for neither flight qualification nor economic impact.

## Technical distinctions that prevent misleading proofs

**Observation cadence is different from useful information latency.** An optical revisit can be cloudy or arrive after a decision window; a monthly composite can conceal the date of a change. Record acquisition time, product availability, processing completion and decision deadline separately. Use observation masks and time since the last valid measurement. Missing pixels are not a negative observation. Annual embeddings can be useful for retrospective mapping while being unavailable for an earlier in-year decision.

**Resolution is not synonymous with accuracy.** Sentinel-2 bands have native 10, 20 and 60 m sampling; interpolation to a 10 m grid does not create new 10 m spectral detail. Resampling also changes edge labels. Small parcels, narrow flood corridors and mixed pixels need an error budget appropriate to the task. Pixel accuracy dominated by background says little about missed flooded roads or split smallholder fields.

**Preprocessing can create a false environmental change.** The [official harmonized Sentinel-2 catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S2_SR_HARMONIZED) documents a 1,000-DN shift from processing baseline 04.00 after 25 January 2022; harmonization reconciles it. It also records changes in QA60 cloud-mask availability/construction. Use reflectance scales, band names, processing baseline and mask provenance explicitly. The [Sentinel-1 processing guide](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/sentinel1) distinguishes orbit/noise/calibration/terrain corrections and notes Earth Engine does not apply radiometric terrain flattening. A terrain-corrected product and an RTC product need not have equivalent radiometry. Linear backscatter and dB are different inputs; speckle, slope, shadow and incidence angle can confound a water detector.

**Spatially random tests are often the wrong test.** Nearby chips can share source imagery, land parcels, weather, annotation conventions and the same event. Overlapping crops, temporal revisits and neighboring fields should not straddle a purported independent split. Hold out whole geographic groups and acquisition periods, with a buffer determined from imagery footprints and the task's correlation scale. Report the original benchmark separately from a more stringent experiment. Unsupervised global pretraining on a test region is not automatically supervised label leakage, but it prevents a claim that the model has never observed that region's imagery.

**Ground truth is an instrument and sampling process.** Satellite-derived labels, field surveys, analyst polygons and administrative parcel records answer different questions. A government crop map is not automatically independent ground truth for another classifier trained from the same survey points. A parametric hazard trigger is different from measured property damage. Matching geolocation without matching date can label a harvested field as its earlier crop. Calibration should be checked on the intended population, not only on a convenient annotated subset.

### A fresh benchmark worth using carefully

[GEOID-Flood, arXiv:2608.02315v1, 3 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02315v1), has an instructive result: TerraMind-L binary water IoU 0.884 versus Swin-T 0.873 at roughly 323M versus 32M parameters, while the best single-modality flood-class IoU is only 0.484. Water detection and transient-flood detection are different outcomes. The paper groups touching/overlapping AoIs for splitting, but its permanent-water reference is model-derived from flood-year AlphaEarth annual embeddings; pre-2017 events use 2017. This creates retrospective information and reference-dependence concerns, particularly for real-time claims or evaluating AlphaEarth against its own derived labels. These are audit limitations, not proof the paper's supervised comparisons are invalid.

The [released code](https://github.com/links-ads/geoid-flood) and [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/links-ads/geoid-flood) improve actionability: MIT code, CC BY 4.0 compilation/labels, with separate WorldDEM-30 terms. Python 3.11/3.12 and TerraTorch 1.1 are specified. The full selection is roughly 584–586 GB, S1-GRD plus labels about 205 GB, held-out S1-RTC evaluation about 20 GB. A small `sample/` contains training AoI `EMSR712-10` and test AoI `EMSR712-3` from the same activation. That fixture is useful for wiring; it does not prove event generalization and motivates auditing activation IDs rather than trusting the README's broader event-independence wording. The DEM's 30 m observations are resampled onto the 10 m grid.

Older datasets remain useful controls. [Sen1Floods11 v1.1 documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudtostreet/Sen1Floods11/master/docs/README.md) distinguishes 446 manually labeled chips from 4,385 weakly labeled chips, including Otsu-derived targets. A model judged mainly against its threshold-generated labels can reproduce the labeler rather than surpass it. Its [main repository](https://github.com/cloudtostreet/Sen1Floods11) states open access but did not supply an explicit dataset redistribution license in this audit; a third-party CC-BY tag is insufficient. [Kuro Siwo](https://github.com/Orion-AI-Lab/KuroSiwo) states CC BY for data and MIT for code, whereas its [2024 supplement](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/43612b0662cb6a4986edf859fd6ebafe-Supplemental-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf) described MIT for dataset/benchmark. Confirm version-specific terms before redistribution.

## Access, pipelines and actual data cost

[AlphaEarth's current GCS documentation](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/aef_on_gcs_readme), updated 7 July 2026, offers annual 2017–2025 embeddings as COGs under CC BY 4.0, with provider-paid bucket access at that date. These are 64-channel, signed-int8, normalized vectors; -128 is masked. The prescribed nonlinear dequantization is signed-square scaling, and averaging raw quantized values does not produce the correct overview. Use the supplied spatial index to select an area rather than fetching global files. This is a public data product, not release of the encoder's trainable weights. A dataset license is separate from the terms/costs of an execution platform.

[NISAR's S-band public release, 24 July 2026](https://www.isro.gov.in/NISARS_Band_SAR_Data_Products_Release.html), is a current, concrete expansion of Indian SAR access. Products from cycle 25 onward use baseline v1.4.0 / CRID P00500, with historical reprocessing planned in phases. Bhoonidhi uses registered download/programmatic access; the announcement lists complex, covariance, interferometric and offset products. Do not treat all these levels as interchangeable ready-to-model rasters or assume all historic acquisitions are already uniformly reprocessed. The [mission page](https://www.isro.gov.in/Mission_GSLVF16_NISAR_Home.html) states a free/open data policy; exact product terms, credentials, footprint and storage must still be verified before a project. A 12-day repeat is not a 12-day guarantee of delivered analytic insight.

The practical pipeline is catalog/search → immutable asset manifest → bounded spatial read → calibrated/masked aligned arrays → model/baseline → uncertainty and coverage → versioned GIS output. Preserve CRS, transform, nodata, acquisition/processing dates, resolution, units, license, checksum and source lineage. Use geographically appropriate area calculations instead of treating longitude/latitude degrees as equal-size metres. Chunked/windowed processing avoids loading every band/date into RAM. Count network and decompression time, retries, cache use and review time as well as inference. No particular cloud-account entitlement or zero-cost compute is assumed here.

## Onboard software: a real route with a stricter evidence boundary

NASA's [7 May 2026 Prithvi-in-orbit article](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/ai-foundation-model-in-orbit/) describes demonstrations across two platforms. Its linked, currently listed [Du et al. preprint, arXiv:2512.01181v1, 1 December 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01181v1), is narrower: Kanyini validation relied on engineering hardware; IMAGIN-e executed an uploaded test dataset under orbital conditions because its imager had a power fault. The paper identifies live-data demonstrations as future work and promises code after publication. Treat this as orbital runtime/numerical feasibility, not verified live two-satellite flood monitoring. Ground hardware equivalence, sensor adaptation, end-to-end operational utility and economic delivery are separate claims.

A second primary account, [Szywala et al., arXiv:2610.11735v1, 8 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11735v1), makes the distinction explicit. Its PhiSat-2 marine pipeline functioned in the mission environment, but initial simulated-data models mismatched real L1 observations. Retraining used 16 scenes and testing eight; ambiguous areas were excluded from anomaly scores, and the real set had no oil-spill samples. Stages were evaluated independently with perfect upstream labels. Its high revised stage scores are therefore not an end-to-end, all-pollutant operational success rate. The paper's estimated five-minute scene processing is not real-time event detection latency.

For a software-first learning route, [F Prime](https://github.com/nasa/fprime) is an Apache-2.0 component-based flight/embedded framework with test/simulation support; [FPP](https://nasa.github.io/fpp/fpp-users-guide.html) generates part of an implementation from typed models, with project-specific C++ still required. [Basilisk 2.4.0 documentation](https://hanspeterschaub.info/basilisk/) describes an ISC-licensed spacecraft simulation framework supporting macOS, Linux and Windows. Reading those artifacts verifies availability, not local buildability. An offline telemetry/replay/test harness can demonstrate software discipline; it cannot establish radiation behavior, thermal limits, mission assurance or safe operation of real spacecraft.

## Proposed 30/90-day proofs

Choose one project first. All targets below are proposed decision rules, not reported results or industry standards. Before any download or installation: confirm source rights and attribution; inspect one real feature/label record; pin code and model/data revisions; check disk/RAM/OS; declare an offline-only, no-paid-compute budget. Current branch links are discovery references, not immutable builds. Attempts to retrieve some source/dataset revision APIs were inaccessible, so exact execution pins remain a launch gate rather than invented hashes.

### A. Retrospective land/field monitoring with independent observation labels

**Buyer hypothesis:** an analyst needs a prioritized, evidence-backed review queue, with reliable confidence and source dates. First aim for land-cover/crop screening, not automatic regulatory judgment or credit decisions.

**Days 1–30:** use the [2024 RPA GOP catalog](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/bff7e0d5-e813-45ad-bb0e-7321dc042d72/rpa-crop-map-of-england-features-and-events-ground-observation-points-2024), which explicitly states OGL v3.0. Inspect one record's crop/event code, date, CRS and survey meaning before defining labels. Compare a majority/season prior, spectral indices plus logistic regression/random forest, and a small classifier on cached AlphaEarth features. This is retrospective mapping: annual features may contain observations later than an individual survey. Do not claim in-season prediction. Use spatial groups and separate validation from test; do not train on the CROME map and then call its source GOP independent validation.

**Days 31–90:** add an untouched geographic zone and a later observation year only after verifying that release's terms and label equivalence. The [2025 producer catalog](https://dsp.environment.data.gov.uk/dataset/5ab8d86d-1e14-4630-9fd0-e1a459420409) reports more than 12,000 locations in 32 zones, mowing/harvest/tilling observations and substantial repeat-location overlap from 2024. It is a strong lead, but the full page timed out and the 2025-specific license was not confirmed here. Group repeat locations/parcels across all years and audit whether a test crop appears in training before selecting an unseen-region claim. If moving to in-season detection, replace full-year features with strictly prior observations and evaluate the loss in accuracy/coverage.

**Proof metrics:** per-class precision/recall, macro-F1, date/zone holdout performance, calibration/Brier score, selective risk versus accepted coverage, missed high-priority changes, imagery age, processing time, bytes read and analyst review minutes per accepted case. Proposed target: at a declared false-alert budget, improve high-priority recall by at least 10 percentage points over the cheap spectral baseline on untouched spatial groups, or achieve comparable performance with at least 50% lower measured processing/label effort. Show interval estimates grouped by zone, not millions of pseudo-independent pixels.

**Kill criteria:** no usable date/label fixture; unresolved rights; improvement disappears after repeat-location grouping; same-year annual features are essential to an allegedly early warning result; or a simple index/season rule matches the review utility. A screening score cannot demonstrate farm-level yield accuracy, compliance or reduced default losses. Needed fundamentals: CRS/raster-vector operations, sampling bias, time-series features, calibrated classification, SQL/PostGIS, windowed rasters and reproducible data lineage.

### B. Flood mapping with real event and time boundaries

**Buyer hypothesis:** an emergency/asset analyst needs a useful flood estimate within a decision window, with missing coverage and suspected failures visible. No emergency service is to be operated through this project.

**Days 1–30:** use GEOID's small released sample strictly to verify file formats and pipeline wiring. Validate one pre/post VV/VH pair, its linear-to-dB conversion, label values, nodata and CRS; inspect whether a “10 m DEM” is resampled. Build Otsu/change-threshold and permanent-water-prior baselines before a neural model. Measure binary water and flooded water separately. Never treat derived `permwater`/`label` layers as legal model inputs. Record the sample's same-activation train/test relationship visibly.

**Days 31–90:** select a small manifest of independent full activations, with no overlap between train, validation and test and at least one untouched later-period set. Audit sensor/source acquisition dates and CEMS delineation times. Keep the official benchmark result separate from the stricter activation-grouped experiment. Compare threshold/change logic, a compact supervised UNet/Swin baseline, and a tiny/frozen TerraMind configuration with the same decoder and sensor inputs. Test GRD versus RTC and SAR-only versus added pre-event optical information separately. Add an independent or prior-year-only permanent-water reference and report how conclusions change; this directly tests whether a retrospective/model-derived reference drove the result. Inspect a blinded sample of disagreements rather than automatically declaring every reference mismatch a model failure.

**Proof metrics:** flood-class IoU/F1, omission/commission area, performance by event/slope/cloud/urban setting, abstention coverage, event-level calibration, output age, p95 end-to-end runtime, peak memory, storage, bytes read and review time. Proposed target: at a fixed false-flooded-area budget, reduce missed flood area by at least 20% versus the strongest cheap baseline across held-out events while staying inside an explicitly measured laptop resource budget. Report failures and event-level uncertainty; no gain on binary-water IoU alone passes.

**Kill criteria:** the independent subset cannot fit the approved resource budget; event metadata cannot enforce a clean split; a released checkpoint or license cannot be pinned; the claimed benefit vanishes with a prior-year/reference-independent test; or a small baseline provides the same accepted map sooner. Public-data replication cannot prove disaster-response benefit, calibrated river depth, live mission performance or insurance payouts. It also cannot establish generalization to Indian monsoon conditions unless those are independently represented and held out.

### Optional space-software extension after a ground proof

An offline F Prime or Basilisk example could generate/replay synthetic telemetry, inject dropped/late observations, enforce queue/time budgets and preserve typed event provenance. Compare unbounded processing to a bounded, observable pipeline; verify deterministic replay and the documented handling of stale data. By day 90, a realistic deliverable is an auditable software-in-the-loop harness and measured resource budget. A Mac result does not predict ARM/accelerator latency or spacecraft power/thermal behavior. Target hardware, hardware-in-loop tests and mission-specific assurance are prerequisites before claiming onboard suitability. No hardware purchase, flight deployment or control operation is authorized or needed for the initial proof.

## What would falsify the opportunity thesis?

Evidence against the broad entrepreneur story would be buyers accepting existing public maps/GIS tooling at negligible switching cost; foundation features plus a simple head absorbing the needed analysis; high review/label costs canceling compute savings; public procurement consolidating into a few primes; or domain/geographic credentials blocking access to the actual decision workflow. Evidence for a narrower engineering career is already stronger: operator data maintenance, scientific-code productionization, onboard/ground numerical parity, reliability, metadata and application feasibility appear in current primary role descriptions. The defensible first move is a small, independently evaluated decision workflow attached to one real domain, with the expensive space or model-research commitment earned by its results.
