# Energy round 2: funded work, attainable roles, research entry paths

Researcher: energy_infrastructure. Evidence checked 19 October 2026. Scope: energy-specific technical/budget evidence, India/UK entry paths, vendor competition, public tools and institutional channels. This is a purposive evidence sample, not a representative employment survey. Root owns the combined directory and labor worker owns the general jobs matrix. No installations, applications, outreach, purchases, live controls, private systems or external writes were performed.

## Finding

The most accessible entry is **ordinary production software inside an energy business**, then domain depth acquired alongside analysts and engineers. It is not necessary to become a power-protection engineer first. Conversely, an AI/backend portfolio does not immediately qualify someone to commission substations or develop safety-critical power controllers. The job evidence makes this distinction unusually clear.

The startup opportunity is also narrower than “AI for energy.” Siemens already sells congestion/flexibility software, NVIDIA already schedules around power constraints, and equipment vendors already sell predictive asset services. A credible new entrant needs a specific integration, cross-vendor workflow, validation gap or commercial decision that an existing buyer cannot adequately solve with its installed platform.

## 2. Funding and buyer workflow: what is actually established

**A funded research project, with a bounded outcome.** National Grid's DELIVER digital-twin restoration project is marked complete. Its 2025/26 narrative reports **£591k total expenditure, including £513k external**, while the summary field says £586k; retain that discrepancy rather than silently treating both as the same audited number. The project built RTDS/RSCAD test systems and demonstrated restoration/adaptive-protection functions. It did not establish routine production deployment across the grid. A £146m projected benefit is conditional cost-benefit analysis, not realised savings. Arup is the named collaborator. [ENA project record, close-down report listed 16 July 2026](https://smarter.energynetworks.org/projects/nia2_nget0033/).

**Specific procurement need, without a contract award.** National Grid's April 2026 RFI asks for a Network Master Model Manager that governs, validates and distributes trusted electrical-network models across engineering/operational tools. The notice explicitly makes further procurement optional, so an RFI is not funded recurring revenue. It nevertheless identifies a concrete workflow: ownership, versioning, validation and interoperability of network models. [Official Sell2Wales notice, 29 April 2026](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/search/show/search_view.aspx?ID=APR612908).

**An operator reports deploying a platform, but the result is operator-claimed.** National Grid says its Atos-built Triton planning tool launched on 29 January 2026, with a claimed 70% reduction in time to analyse reinforcement options. This is useful evidence of a buyer and incumbent delivery partner; it is not independently measured proof that a new entrant can reproduce the benefit. [National Grid release](https://www.nationalgrid.com/national-grid-unveils-award-winning-new-digital-twin-and-data-visualisation-tool-triton-accelerate).

**There is industrial R&D funding, but aggregate allocations must not become product budgets.** Berkeley Lab's 12 March 2026 release identifies REFLEX load-flexibility demonstrations and a Data Center Cooling Collaborative among 16 selected DOE projects in a **$155m aggregate initiative**. That figure is not a grant to one startup or a budget for the software opportunity described here. The stated work includes testing protocols, digital-twin software, business-case tools and commercialisation pathways. [Berkeley Lab announcement](https://eta.lbl.gov/news/improving-us-industrial-competitiveness-and-productivity).

For a buyer-led pilot, identify the named workflow owner before proposing a model: energy-data team lead (quality and publication latency), asset engineer (inspection/maintenance prioritisation), battery optimiser (constraint compliance and dispatch decisions), or data-centre platform/facilities lead (workload deadlines versus site electrical limits). Budget owners, operational users and data owners may differ. Publication or a research grant alone does not prove willingness to buy a separate platform.

## 3. Native vendors: the competitive baseline

- **Siemens Gridscale X Flexibility Manager** already combines congestion prediction, asset flexibility identification and DSO integrations. Its up-to percentages are vendor claims. The competitive implication is that a generic congestion dashboard is not sufficient differentiation. [Official product page, undated, checked 19 October 2026](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/gridscale-x/flexibility-manager/).
- **NVIDIA Mission Control / Power Reservation Steering** already treats power as a schedulable Slurm resource. Its architecture coordinates workload placement, power headroom and operational constraints. Therefore, “write a power-aware GPU scheduler” is neither a greenfield product thesis nor proof of a moat. A possible gap is auditable integration across heterogeneous hardware, utility commitments and customer SLAs; that gap needs customer evidence. [Official PRS administration documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/mission-control/docs/systems-administration-guide/latest/prs/introduction.html); [dated technical architecture, 1 April 2026](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerate-token-production-in-ai-factories-using-unified-services-and-real-time-ai/).
- **Hitachi's HMAX Energy / asset-performance services** combine monitoring, predictive lifecycle management and services with installed equipment. Its Chile deployment announcement illustrates incumbent asset/data access. The new entrant must establish what it can do across assets or workflows that the service agreement does not already include. [Hitachi release, 14 August 2026](https://www.hitachienergy.com/in/en/news-and-events/press-releases/2026/08/hitachi-energy-boosts-generadora-metropolitana-s-efficiency-in-chile-through-its-ai-powered-suite-of-services-and-solutions).

Commercialisation implication, explicitly an inference: the first 90 days should validate access, workflow and incremental value. A full utility procurement or commissioning cycle should not be promised inside 90 days. Employment with an incumbent/integrator can be a more immediate route to learning the actual operating environment than founding a platform that requires its data and trust.

## 4. Open technical entry points, papers and replication limits

### Data-centre demand response

**Paper:** Acun et al., *Investigating Power Consumption Flexibility of AI Data Centers for Demand Response Participation*, ACM E-Energy, 22–25 June 2026. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.bu.edu/peaclab/files/2026/03/FlexDC_Sim_ACM_E_Energy26.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3744255.3798112).

Its 18–55% flexibility result is simulated using hardware profiles, not measured grid-scale curtailment. Profiles use four P100 GPUs; inference is batch-style. The paper defines QoS degradation as `(sojourn - minimum runtime) / minimum runtime`; thresholds of 3–5 correspond to total sojourn of 4–6 times the unconstrained minimum before being classified as a threshold violation. Those assumptions cannot establish equal flexibility for tight interactive latency. Replicating profiles physically needs suitable GPUs and administrative power-cap access; laptop simulation does not.

**Code:** [PEACLab FlexDC-Sim](https://github.com/peaclab/flexdc-sim), BSD-3-Clause; paper CC-BY-4.0. README specifies Python 3.10, input configurations and bundled signal/profile data. Code inspection only; no install or execution performed. A CPU laptop can be used for simulation after an approved environment setup. A useful contribution is a reproducible trace/SLO benchmark and calibration report, rather than claiming savings on modern hardware from old profiles.

### Storage and energy-system optimisation

**Framework:** [PyPSA official documentation](https://docs.pypsa.org/latest/), MIT code; maintained by TU Berlin's Department of Digital Transformation in Energy Systems. The canonical paper is Brown, Hörsch and Schlachtberger, *PyPSA: Python for Power System Analysis*, 2018, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5334/jors.188). The framework supports storage and constrained dispatch; it is not a certification for operating live assets.

The [rolling-horizon example](https://docs.pypsa.org/stable/examples/rolling-horizon/) explicitly compares limited foresight with perfect foresight, making it a useful antidote to inflated backtests. The example does not include forecast errors, which must be added for a realistic experiment. [Release 1.4.0, 9 October 2026](https://docs.pypsa.org/stable/release-notes/) fixes some unit-commitment/rolling-horizon and ramp-limit behaviours. Pin the version and rerun validation rather than assuming old example results will remain identical. No software was installed.

### Public market and system data

**Elexon Insights:** [official API developer portal](https://developer.data.elexon.co.uk/) says APIs are public and require no API key; historical publication times allow recovery of superseded forecasts. This enables a causally correct backtest using only information available when a decision was made. Follow the linked data/API terms and attribution requirements. The old BMRS documentation has different key requirements; use the current Insights portal.

**NESO:** [official API guidance](https://www.neso.energy/data-portal/api-guidance) documents CKAN/Datastore endpoints and recommends no more than one CKAN request per second and two Datastore requests per minute. Cache metadata and retrieve only changed resources. [Demand Flexibility Service documents](https://www.neso.energy/industry-information/balancing-services/demand-flexibility-service-dfs) provide the market/baseline/API side. Public aggregate data does not provide customer-level telemetry or permission to dispatch an asset.

### Cooling and thermal models

**Berkeley Lab Modelica Buildings Library:** [project](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/), [license](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/license.html), a modified BSD-3-Clause form with an additional enhancements paragraph. Uses physical thermal/control models rather than an unconstrained predictor. Replication needs a compatible Modelica environment; that was not installed. For a software engineer, thermal simulation tooling and data integration are nearer entry points than designing cooling hardware. Live validation still needs a testbed or facility partner.

### Forecast uncertainty matters for career reasoning

The *United States Data Center Energy Usage Report: 2025 Update* was published **18 June 2026**, despite its title. It estimates US 2030 demand at **649 TWh**, with compounded bounds **521–843 TWh**, and relies on shipment/power assumptions. It should not be harmonised silently with IEA's global central case. [DOE/OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3374245); [DOE executive text](https://www.energy.gov/documents/united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report-2025-update). This uncertainty favours transferable systems/validation skills over a career dependent on one demand number.

## 5. Concrete 30/90-day experiments and kill criteria

These are proposed future experiments, not implementations performed in this research. Choose one branch; doing all three simultaneously dilutes the signal. CPU laptop work is sufficient for the initial stages. New dependency installs, cloud rental, instruments or physical assets require separate approval.

### A. Fastest lateral route: trustworthy energy data/backend

**30 days:** Use a small Elexon/NESO public-data window to build a typed ingestion service, schema/version tracking, publication-time-aware historical replay, missing-data handling and an API that returns data with provenance. Add a narrow read-only analyst tool that always supplies source timestamps and units. Test settlement-period/DST edge cases, revised data, duplicated records and stale forecasts. Compare against a plain SQL query and manual analyst extraction. No model training or specialist hardware required.

**Prerequisites:** Python/SQL, API design, time-series validation, UTC versus settlement time, units, idempotency and observability. Learn the selected dataset's actual market meaning before adding AI. Entry roles: backend/data/platform engineer at Modo/Kraken/tem or enterprise-software vendor teams.

**90 days:** Reproduce one actual analyst or data-engineering workflow with a domain reviewer: e.g. retrieve the latest forecast that existed at a historical decision time and explain subsequent revisions. Add quality alerts and reproducible evidence for every transformation. A future public bug/documentation contribution can provide stronger evidence than a generic dashboard; no contribution was posted here.

**Go/kill:** Continue if a reviewer can use it to complete a recurring decision/report faster without losing traceability. Kill the standalone-product thesis if the incumbent platform already performs the task adequately, the only value is a nicer chart, or no permitted data can support the workflow. A useful employment portfolio can remain even if the startup thesis fails.

### B. Optimisation route: honest battery-dispatch experiment

**30 days:** Build a small constrained dispatch study using public data and an openly documented model. Explicitly specify power/energy limits, charge/discharge efficiency, state-of-charge reserve, terminal energy value and cycling/degradation proxy. Compare fixed schedule, simple threshold policy, rolling forecast and perfect-foresight upper bound. Out-of-sample evaluation must use historical forecast versions, with costs and uncertainty assumptions exposed. Wholesale prices are not automatically an owner's realised tariff or trading revenue.

**Prerequisites:** Linear algebra, probability, constrained optimisation, numerical tolerances and basic storage physics; Python modelling. A small CPU model is enough. No battery, inverter, smart meter or energy-market credentials needed for simulation; real dispatch and market participation require an authorised asset/operator path.

**90 days:** Validate against a battery analyst/optimiser's workflow specification, reproduce forecast-error and degradation sensitivity, and package a decision/report rather than a revenue claim. Investigate entry work at an optimiser, storage-data provider, DER software team or incumbent vendor. The required additional skill is domain constraints and backtest discipline, not necessarily another deep neural network.

**Go/kill:** Kill any claimed edge that vanishes after forecast leakage, fees, reserve obligations, cycling costs or a stronger simple baseline. Do not commercialise a simulated result without asset-level tariff/constraint validation. Continue as a learning portfolio if it demonstrates good engineering and correctly exposes why a naive strategy fails.

### C. Systems/research route: data-centre flexibility under real SLOs

**30 days:** Inspect and, after approved setup, reproduce one FlexDC-Sim scenario with its provided fixtures. Add a latency-sensitive workload with explicit arrival bursts and service deadlines. Sweep utilisation, idle power, allowed slowdown and inaccurate power profiles. Include the simplest static cap/FIFO policy as baseline. Clearly label simulations; no claimed modern-GPU efficiency measurements without hardware.

**Prerequisites:** Queues, tail latency, scheduling, profiling, probability, distributed failures, power versus energy and constrained optimisation. Existing laptop for simulation. Physical profiling needs a supported GPU, permission to set power limits and suitable telemetry; a cloud tenancy may not expose those permissions. No rental was started.

**90 days:** Produce an independently reproducible limitation/extension report, then seek a permitted trace or testbed via an employer/research collaboration. Compare the desired workflow against NVIDIA Mission Control before proposing a product. A likely contribution is calibration, heterogeneity or utility/SLA integration, not another general scheduler.

**Go/kill:** Kill the commercial thesis if gains require unrealistic 4–6x interactive slowdown, if metrics exclude missed work or if native vendor software already meets the customer's need. Continue only when measured useful work, deadline compliance and power constraints improve together under representative data. Research employment/contribution remains possible even if product economics fail.
