# Field robotics: the opportunity after the demo

Research cut-off: 19 October 2026. Owned scope: agriculture, civil/construction inspection, underground mining, aerial and marine inspection; degraded localization, sensing, edge execution, communications and accepted work. This is a distinct extension of the first three robotics reports. It does not repeat their industrial installation counts, company filings or VLA benchmarks. Public sources were inspected; no software was installed, datasets downloaded, experiments executed, hardware operated or people contacted. Previous reviewed reports remain unchanged.

## Finding and 2031 inference

Field robotics offers a credible route for a software engineer through **reliable measurement, mission software and delivery of accepted inspection data**. The evidence is weaker for a broad, remotely accessible market in general robot intelligence. A robot can create value by moving an inspector away from danger or avoiding a railway closure while still requiring trained operators, calibration, transport and maintenance. Removing people from the hazardous location is different from removing paid human work.

The probable 2031 job combines some familiar software engineering with physical constraints: preserve sensor provenance and coordinate frames; detect when localization has become untrustworthy; keep useful data during intermittent communications; show an operator exactly what was and was not inspected; and integrate the result into asset maintenance. This is inference from the operating systems and research below, not a quantified hiring forecast. It does not establish that a small startup can sell a standalone generic “robot reliability API”; OEM integration, data rights, procurement, site access and service responsibility can capture the value.

Three defensible, less obvious lessons are: (1) choosing an easier measurement problem can beat a more general perception model; (2) degradation detection and truthful coverage can matter more than lower average localization error; and (3) the denominator should be accepted work after revisits and support, rather than autonomous minutes or frames per second.

Avoid two recency traps. Network Rail's [19 October 2026 East Coast announcement](https://www.networkrailmediacentre.co.uk/news/drone-technology-takes-flight-to-strengthen-east-coasts-response-to-severe-weather-on-the-railway) describes exercises and exploration of weather-response uses: the earlier operating savings cannot be transferred to this new route. For operator economics, define the output before pricing the robot. Examples are a specified length of pipe with reviewable imagery, identified defects and location confidence; an accepted offshore inspection scope; or a hectare meeting crop-damage and weed-control tolerances. A useful accounting identity is:

`cost per accepted unit = (equipment + mobilization + on-site staff + remote staff + data review + maintenance + communications + weather/idle capacity + rework) / accepted units`

Use equipment cost amortized over defensible utilization, not purchase price divided by 24/7 theoretical capacity. Separately estimate avoided line closures, vessel exposure or chemical inputs; these may dominate wage savings. None of the sources above publishes every term. Therefore this report does **not** calculate a normalized cost per useful hour or claim a validated startup margin. Decisive buyer evidence would be repeat paid scopes, rejected/revisited work, support hours and the conventional service's matched cost. Operator-authored rail evidence is stronger than a supplier's attractive time-saving percentage, but it still lacks a complete causal cost audit.

## The technical frontier is conditional reliability

**A better model is sometimes the wrong first investment.** FarmDroid's seed-position strategy turns part of crop/weed discrimination into a known spatial relationship. This restricts compatible planting and operating conditions, but the restriction is commercially useful. A vision-based competitor has to improve the whole outcome—weed removal, crop damage, uptime and operating cost—rather than merely win image segmentation. Conversely, an RTK-dependent design can lose its advantage under multipath, correction-link loss, positioning mistakes, unrecorded plants or an incompatible crop. This is a falsifiable tradeoff, not a blanket endorsement of classical systems.

**GNSS denial creates different failure modes.** In a long, visually repetitive pipe or tunnel, translation along the corridor can be poorly observable even if a map looks clean. Darkness compromises cameras; airborne dust or smoke can produce misleading exteroceptive returns; changing scenes make a previously correct map stale. More sensors help only when their timing, extrinsics, independence and failure behavior are handled. A confident fusion of correlated errors can be worse than an honest degraded-state signal. These are engineering implications; their frequency in a particular mine or sewer must be measured on that site's data.

CMU's [SubT-MRS release](https://superodometry.com/datasets) is particularly useful because it names actual failure conditions and supplies calibration and separately distributed ground truth. Examples include `Long_Corridor`, `BlockLiDAR`, `BlockVisual`, `SmokeRoom`, `OutdoorNight` and `FlashLight`; the modalities differ between sequences. Do not claim a common sensor configuration or compare algorithms on unavailable inputs. The paper is [Zhao et al., CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Zhao_SubT-MRS_Dataset_Pushing_SLAM_Towards_All-weather_Environments_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf), with [arXiv:2307.07607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07607). The public page links ROS bags, extracted files and dataset issues; an explicit dataset license was not located in this audit. Access does not establish unrestricted commercial reuse.

There is meaningful learned-method counterevidence. [SUPER ODOMETRY 2.0, arXiv:2608.25427v1, 26 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25427v1) describes feature, state-direction and engine adaptation plus learned inertial odometry, trained on over 100 hours from heterogeneous platforms. Authors report 200 km and 800 operating hours across aerial, wheeled and legged systems. The arXiv record lists a related Science Robotics DOI [10.1126/scirobotics.adv1818](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.adv1818) and journal year 2025; the 2026 arXiv upload must not be mislabeled as the original publication date. Learned motion priors may extend a useful fallback beyond hand-selected heuristics, but finite test hours do not establish indefinite IMU-only localization or all-site reliability.

Crucially, the public [SuperOdom repository](https://github.com/superxslam/SuperOdom) describes itself as a slim LiDAR/IMU implementation, rather than proof that every 2.0 paper component is released. It includes alignment-risk/degeneracy detection and ROS 2 Humble support, with GTSAM, Ceres, PCL and Eigen dependencies. Its stated license is GPL-3.0-only with an alternative commercial license; GPL permits commercial use subject to its terms. Pinning and verifying the actual implemented module is part of replication. Do not reproduce the README's privileged-container/permission commands blindly or infer successful execution from reading them.

**Do not choose radar solely from an all-weather slogan.** [Burnett et al., arXiv:2203.10174v3, 8 June 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10174v3) compared radar, LiDAR and cross-modal localization on Boreas. Their LiDAR pipeline had the best localization accuracy even in the tested snowstorm; radar was competitive with a smaller map. Updated calibration and ground truth changed the reported results between paper versions. This is direct counterevidence to “radar always replaces LiDAR in bad weather,” not evidence that LiDAR survives every precipitation or contamination regime. The valuable engineering skill is identifying the tested regime and failure envelope.

**Construction priors can matter as much as model scale.** The [Hilti–Trimble–Oxford 2026 release](https://github.com/Hilti-Research/hilti-trimble-slam-challenge-2026) combines 360 visual/inertial data with floor-plan localization tasks on active construction sites. It supplies OpenVINS and Stella-VSLAM baseline instructions and explicit calibration/ground-truth conventions. Its paper is [arXiv:2607.06464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06464), with RA-L acceptance reported by the maintainers. A floor plan can be a useful prior but is not automatically a current occupancy map. The [dataset and benchmark license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hilti-Research/hilti-trimble-slam-challenge-2026/main/LICENSE) is **CC BY-NC-SA 3.0**. This is a research portfolio route, not an automatically reusable commercial training corpus.

**Safety filters are conditional systems.** [Sankaranarayanan et al., arXiv:2605.15782v1, 15 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15782v1) reports a body-frame, LiDAR-driven composite control-barrier-function filter in underground quadruped inspections, including dynamic obstacles and abrupt localization anomalies. The record says “under review.” Local sensing can reduce reliance on a globally correct map, but the guarantee still depends on perception, dynamics, latency and feasible control assumptions. A point-cloud replay cannot certify collision avoidance, and an attractive trajectory is not evidence of a safe recovery policy.

## Two credible 30/90-day experiments

All work below is proposed. No commands, downloads, model training or physical tests have been performed. Before choosing either route, create one actual record/label fixture, verify its rights, check disk/RAM/OS compatibility and freeze a minimal reproducible environment. Any needed installs require the user's explicit approval. Replaying historical data measures offline error/detection: it does not establish closed-loop recovery, collision avoidance or paid-labor savings.

### A. Degraded localization with an honest availability signal

**Buyer hypothesis:** an inspection or mapping team needs to distinguish a trustworthy trajectory from a plausible but wrong one before accepting a report. The first demonstrator should output a georeferenced trajectory, validity intervals and a reviewable explanation of missing coverage. It should not command a robot.

**Days 1–30:** choose Boreas for clearly stated rights, or SubT-MRS only after resolving its dataset terms. Start with two or three complete sessions, one nominal tuning session and one untouched adverse session. First validate timestamps, extrinsics, frame direction and float precision against a small known pose fixture. Reproduce one classical LiDAR/IMU or visual/inertial baseline appropriate to the inputs; a constant-velocity/dead-reckoning baseline establishes whether the extra system adds value. Compare a fixed estimator with a simple residual/innovation-gating version. Only then consider a released degeneracy-aware method. Keep GNSS/ground truth exclusively in scoring if the algorithm is described as GNSS degraded. Report whole-session failures and withheld intervals rather than quietly restarting on difficult segments.

**Days 31–90:** freeze at least one site/session/weather holdout and add sensor dropout, timestamp delay and calibration perturbations as separately labeled synthetic tests. Compare fixed fusion, robust gating and an available learned/adaptive method with equal sensors, map access and compute. Require at least two untouched real adverse sessions before claiming more than a one-session finding. Estimate uncertainty calibration from held-out residuals; compare to a no-confidence and fixed-threshold baseline. A small mission-data review UI can show pose validity, last trustworthy observation and uninspected segments without inventing a new motion controller.

**Metrics and prespecified proof target:** ATE/RPE with alignment convention; distance/time before an error threshold; valid coverage; false alarm duration; undetected-error distance; false relocalizations; p50/p95/p99 processing latency and peak memory on named hardware. Pick the error threshold from a concrete inspection acceptance example, not post hoc. As a proposed engineering target, reduce undetected-error distance by at least 30% on held-out adverse sessions while sacrificing no more than 10 percentage points of valid coverage relative to the fixed baseline; report uncertainty across sessions. A classifier's AUC alone fails this criterion. This target is a portfolio decision rule, not an industry requirement or a safety claim.

**Kill criteria:** baseline cannot be reproduced after a bounded week; sensor calibration/rights are unavailable; improvement disappears when sessions are held out; uncertainty relies on future frames unavailable online; or added computation routinely misses the declared replay deadline. Stop a learned extension if a simple gate matches it on the selected risk/coverage tradeoff. Physical validation later needs suitable sensor-equipped equipment, site permissions and independently checked reference measurements; road replay cannot validate smoke, water or underground emergency use.

**Fundamentals:** SE(3), calibration/time synchronization, EKF/factor graphs and observability, robust optimization, numerical precision, ROS bag/data formats, Linux/C++ profiling and experimental design. An experienced software engineer can build the replay/measurement layer before acquiring research-level estimator expertise.

### B. Crop perception that admits when it cannot identify a plant

**Buyer hypothesis:** a weeding or scouting team needs a useful weed/crop decision at a tolerable crop-damage risk on a new growth stage, not the highest mean pixel score on neighboring image patches.

**Days 1–30:** use PhenoBench v1.1.0, verify its official checksum and inspect one image, mask and plant-instance record against the devkit. Resolve the partial-class naming discrepancy before scoring. Preserve the official benchmark; additionally build a date-held-out experiment from filename metadata and use the persistent plant IDs to keep repeat observations on one side of a split. Start with an excess-green/HSV plus morphology baseline, one compact segmentation model and an official stronger baseline. Record the actual available compute before choosing model size; do not assume training is practical on a Mac or that converting a checkpoint preserves scores.

**Days 31–90:** calibrate an abstention threshold on a designated validation date; freeze it before unseen acquisition dates. Compare model-only confidence, simple color/size heuristics and a calibrated model under natural date/growth shift. Add lighting/compression corruption separately from natural shift. Report whether foreground accuracy changes because backgrounds, crops or weeds differ. Create a review queue that preserves source image, date, plant ID, model/version and abstention reason. An external crop dataset can test stronger shift only after label semantics, image geometry and rights are reconciled; do not pool mismatched crop definitions.

**Metrics and prespecified proof target:** per-class precision/recall and IoU, plant-level crop-as-weed error, missed-weed rate, selective risk versus accepted coverage, calibration, p95/p99 latency, peak memory. At a fixed crop-as-weed error budget, target at least 20% fewer missed weeds than the simple baseline on date holdouts; include image/date-level confidence intervals. A pixel error is not a crop destroyed: no claim of damage avoided or chemical saved is allowed without the mechanism's geometry, timing and field trial. The operational risk budget must eventually come from a buyer/agronomist; a chosen portfolio threshold is only a hypothesis.

**Kill criteria:** neighboring patches/repeat plants leak across splits; a held-out date cannot be constructed; result depends on tuning after test inspection; abstention discards nearly all useful work; a cheap geometric/seed-map strategy meets the same use case more economically; or deployment hardware cannot meet the declared budget. By day 90 the useful artifact is a reproducible evaluation and review workflow, not an “autonomous weeding robot.” Physical proof would require crop/site access, calibrated camera-to-tool geometry, agronomic supervision and controlled equipment trials, none authorized here.

## What would change the career conclusion?

The opportunity strengthens if operators publish falling intervention/support effort per accepted scope, repeat procurement across new sites and sustained margins after service costs; if open estimator releases replicate their research robustness on held-out environments; and if UK/India employers hire software/data engineers into these workflows without requiring prior equipment ownership or inaccessible credentials. It weakens if OEM bundles and RTK/floor-plan priors solve most target tasks, failure detection has no useful risk/coverage point, contracts remain bespoke services, or on-site support grows as quickly as fleets. Large model improvements could reduce custom perception and estimator work while increasing the need to measure actual site performance; neither outcome guarantees more total developer jobs.

The strongest entry bet is therefore a narrow proof of **accepted measurement under a declared failure budget**, attached to an existing operator workflow. The evidence does not yet support purchasing hardware, assuming a remote-only field-robotics career, or founding a company around a broad autonomy claim.
