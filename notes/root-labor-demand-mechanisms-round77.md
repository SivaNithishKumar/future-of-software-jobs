## Primary result and identification boundary

Acemoglu and Restrepo, *Journal of Economic Perspectives*33(2),2019,pp3–30, model displacement, productivity and labor-favoring new tasks separately. Their outcome is wage bill; employment and wages need not move together. New tasks favor labor **when labor has comparative advantage**, rather than because every newly named activity needs people. The model assumes immediate cost-minimizing task adoption. Its historical decomposition uses residual industry labor-share changes after a calibrated substitution adjustment. Separating displacement from reinstatement adds an assumption that an industry does not undertake both simultaneously. The1987–2017 analysis covers61US private industries; self-employment income is excluded. The authors explicitly allow changing bargaining/monopsony and product markups to confound interpretation. Their positive task/proxy correlations support the framework but are not randomized developer training evidence. Appendix proofs/data/robustness were not independently reproduced. ## Coordinator inference, not an estimate from the paper

The future tree must keep three questions apart: will software produce more useful output; how much human work will that output require; and who captures the money? A productivity result can help the first while reducing the second, leaving the third unresolved. Better agents can also substitute for the evaluation, integration and domain tasks proposed as preparation. Calling those tasks new does not prove a lasting human comparative advantage.

A minimal accounting illustration makes the missing link visible. Fix the same acceptance/quality boundary and observation period. Let Q be accepted output units and h be **all** human hours per accepted unit, including failed attempts, review and support. Total human hours H=Q×h. These are invented dimensionless ratios, not measured AI effects:

| Q after/before | h after/before | H after/before |
|---:|---:|---:|
|1.00|0.80|0.80|
|1.25|0.80|1.00|
|1.50|0.80|1.20|

The same20% reduction in human hours per accepted unit is compatible with less, unchanged or more total human work. Turning hours into headcount would additionally require comparable paid hours per worker. Turning them into wages, take-home income or accessible jobs needs further observations. An unfunded backlog is not observed paid output expansion. This is an accounting counterexample to a direct speedup→jobs inference, not a demand elasticity estimate or replacement for the paper's equilibrium model. It neither proves demand will rebound nor rules it out.

For each serious branch, the useful signposts are therefore specific: a competent native baseline at the same acceptance boundary; actual funded output expansion rather than announced interest; the human burden and new work across an operating period; whether that work remains human-favored under better automation; and a current reachable role or receiving owner. Missing measurements stay missing. Aggregate US industrial history does not supply India eligibility,2031 job quantities or the reader's training return.
