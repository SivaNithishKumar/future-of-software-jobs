# Adversarial 2031 scenarios: can the preparation strategy survive its own success?

Round 9, written 20 October 2026. This reviews the existing corpus and provisional claim register, rather than repeating the native-platform, protocol or directory audits. No new broad web search, implementation, installation, private access, recruitment, outreach or external mutation. Earlier reports, catalogs and claims remain unchanged. Findings below are proposed synthesis changes and conditional reasoning, not accepted forecasts.

## Result

The corpus supports learning useful technical capabilities, but does not establish that **broad systems plus domain ownership is the highest-return career strategy for this individual**. That strategy is especially vulnerable to a world where its projects succeed: agents and platforms learn to integrate, evaluate, diagnose and repair; one experienced person supervises more accepted work; customers retain formal accountability without retaining the previous staffing ratio. Skill usefulness can survive while the number of paid seats falls.

The strongest contrary case is already supported in pieces, rather than merely imaginable. Native tools assist identity administration and incident investigation; research systems synthesize policies; agents complete substantial implementation and optimization tasks; incumbents possess distribution, operating context and budgets. None proves whole-job automation or inevitable consolidation. Together they rule out treating the remaining hard tasks as an enduring human labor reserve.

The preparation that survives this review is narrower: **one accessible depth path, competent use and independent checking of automation, and short experiments that can terminate an expensive specialization**. This is an option-preserving decision rule, not proof of future job security, premium wages, entrepreneurship or prominence. The report's new contribution is the scenario/investment comparison and revision rules; the earlier [adversarial baseline](<adversarial-baseline.md>) already identified the unsupported bridges and should not be presented as newly discovered here.

## Four outcomes the tree must never merge

| Outcome | Evidence that would support it | Evidence in this corpus that does not suffice |
|---|---|---|
| **Useful skill** | A person makes a better decision, diagnoses an unfamiliar failure or completes a permitted task under a sound oracle. | Having read papers, used an SDK or produced an assisted artifact. |
| **Paid role count** | Defined jobs/hours actually funded, filled and retained across a population and period, including transfers and exits. | More deployments, work volume, risk, open-source activity, training or a selected vacancy. |
| **Wage scarcity** | Compensation/offer changes associated with hard-to-substitute capability after accounting for employer, location, seniority and access; ideally a credible causal comparison. | A frontier company's exceptional salary, an AI-adopter premium or a shortage survey alongside unfunded vacancies. |
| **Venture moat** | Repeated customer preference and retention at attractive margins despite competent substitutes, distribution costs and incumbent response. | Technical difficulty, a software budget, a successful benchmark, regulation or an integration a vendor can bundle. |

“Domain ownership” can refer to knowledge, decision rights, customer trust, formal responsibility, on-call duty or commercial control. These are different assets. Domain facts can become documentation/model context; decision rights can be delegated; an institution can bear liability; a small group can retain approval while agents perform execution. Neither liability nor tacit knowledge supplies a demonstrated permanent human moat.

## The strongest contrary case, stated without a human fallback assumption

Suppose capable agents improve at the exact activities the recommended projects teach: reading organization context, deriving a state machine, proposing specifications, generating adversarial fixtures, comparing implementations, fixing tests, diagnosing incidents and maintaining migrations. Suppose native platforms can reuse identity, telemetry, contracts and customer distribution. The platform pays a large fixed cost to learn these workflows once; each additional customer/deployment uses less human integration effort. Domain specialists supply initial targets and periodic exception review, then supervise more volume. Procurement and liability remain, but attach to the established vendor or a smaller internal team.

In this world, proving that integration is valuable supports the platform's product more directly than a large labor market for integrators. A worker's domain knowledge can help train/configure the system and then become a less scarce input. High performers can earn well, but more output and fewer entry seats coexist. Independent vendors compete against a bundled feature with installed permissions and trusted distribution. An excellent local benchmark may be easy for another agent to reproduce, weakening it as a hiring signal or product barrier.

This is a **conditional causal chain**, not an observed end-to-end 2031 outcome. Its first components have evidence above; the extent of cross-domain transfer, total operating cost, organizational adoption and labor response remain open. A legitimate objection is that a provider may still struggle with novel contexts and severe tail failures. The required follow-up is to measure those residuals and the staffing they actually require, rather than rename them “judgment” and presume they remain human.

### Accountability compression: a constructed counterexample

Let annual accepted workflow volume be `Q`, human variable effort per accepted outcome `h`, and annual fixed human design/approval/operating effort `F`. Total human hours are `T = Q × h + F`; dividing by working hours gives an approximate labor requirement, not observed hiring.

Illustration only: `Q=100`, `h=20`, `F=200` gives 2,200 hours. After automation, `Q=300`, `h=2`, `F=50` gives 650 hours. Output triples while human effort falls. The numbers are deliberately hypothetical; their purpose is to disprove the logical inference “more work + continuing accountability → more paid people.” The opposite outcome is possible if volume, new tasks or service expectations expand enough.

In a simplified model where lower unit labor cost fully reduces price, output demand elasticity governs how much volume offsets reduced labor per outcome. Real businesses also have compute, capital, distribution, risk and procurement costs; pass-through and demand are unknown. This corpus does not estimate those elasticities for an Indian developer, a robotics vendor or a UK employer. Consequently it cannot identify future role counts from technical success alone.

## Three scenarios to stress-test investments

These are conditional archetypes, not mutually exclusive worlds, assigned probabilities or extrapolated doubling curves. Different sectors can occupy different columns at the same date.

| Dimension | S1 — slower adoption and uneven capability | S2 — rapid consolidation around native platforms | S3 — broad autonomy with widely available tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Context, procurement, data access, integration and acceptance slow deployment; assistants still lower some task costs. | Platforms/large employers automate more of the whole loop and amortize context/control costs; a smaller specialist group governs larger volume. | Strong tools become broadly usable for implementation, evaluation and some research; many entrants can assemble products cheaply. |
| Existing anchor | Mixed productivity/adoption outcomes; actual access/credential filters; operator projects with bespoke integration. | Native policy/admin/investigation tools, established finance/energy suites, selected frontier work reorganization. | Long-task implementations, measurable optimization and narrow accepted automation; open artifacts lower some access costs. |
| What is not yet observed | A stable 2031 deployment ceiling or permanent refusal to automate. | A representative causal fall in integration/SRE/evaluation seats attributable to native bundling. | Cheap reliable completion of heterogeneous full jobs with representative maintenance, human standards and costs. |
| Likely engineering allocation, inference | Practical backend/data/migration/support inside operating businesses; selected domain integration. | Platform internals, high-value domain exceptions, deployment/contract integration and larger spans of responsibility. | Rapid experimentation and product variation; difficult acceptance, resource or physical constraints where they actually remain. |
| Main personal risk | Spending years on a frontier specialty customers cannot adopt or employer channels cannot access. | Becoming good at a feature vendors bundle, or entering a specialty after its junior ladder shrinks. | Treating reproducible technical output as scarce when competitors produce it too; demand/distribution, not implementation, limits income. |
| Business risk | Slow procurement and bespoke services consume runway; important need has no funded buyer. | Installed context, permissions, indemnity/support and distribution dominate incremental model quality. | Easier creation increases supply and competition; scarce customer attention and complementary assets may capture value. |
| Sensible response, conditional | Improve an accessible existing workflow; acquire domain knowledge through real work; limit speculative retraining. | Be fluent with native tools and prove a residual contribution or enter the platform/operator organization. | Use strong tools aggressively, shorten experiments, test willingness to adopt/pay and preserve ability to reject a bad result. No “human-only” task is assumed. |

**Why this is more than cautious storytelling:** the scenarios disagree about which costly investment pays off. A custom identity gateway is vulnerable in S2; an implementation-only product is vulnerable in S3; an expensive hardware/research pivot without access is vulnerable in S1. The same foundation can remain educational across all three while no scenario guarantees it becomes well-paid work.

## Is the strategy flattering existing interests?

There are three risks of circular reasoning in this corpus.

First, the same residual is repeatedly selected: every field has data, integration, evaluation and recovery, and these match the developer's stated interests. That is a reason the skills transfer; it is not evidence the intersection has the best expected earnings or strongest personal fit. Second, technically legible open artifacts are easier to research than customer acquisition, sales, management or employer screening. The visible opportunity set is therefore partly selected by researcher access. Third, an engineer can enjoy building a harness while avoiding the more decisive question: whether anyone needs the output enough to employ or buy it.

The opposite bias also matters. Recommending “ordinary backend” merely because it sounds less fashionable is not supported by a direct comparative career trial. Specialization can create access and depth, but can also lock a person into a shrinking vendor/tool or inaccessible geography. The correct comparison includes current earning capacity, learning speed, real prerequisites, desired work style and opportunity cost. None has been independently assessed for this user; historical projects and expressed interests are not a verified CV.

At least four genuinely competing routes should remain visible: deepen a current backend/data/product specialty; pursue low-level systems/performance depth; enter a domain operator/vendor through existing software work; or invest in research credentials and a scrutinized research record. Entrepreneurship adds a fifth route centered on customer access and distribution. These should not all be disguised as “become an AI generalist.” A credential can be worthwhile when it removes a verified hard filter; collecting generic badges without such a bridge has no demonstrated return here.

## Observable triggers for revising the strategy

These are proposed decision rules, not statistical thresholds validated by the literature and not scheduled monitoring tasks. Root can attach them to the final tree. Every trigger should preserve model/platform/source revisions and include unsuccessful cases.

| Trigger | What to observe | Direction of update |
|---|---|---|
| **Integration absorbs into native tooling** | Across two distinct permitted workflows, a configured native or agent-only baseline meets the same effects/maintenance oracle with lower total setup/review/support burden. | Reduce investment in a custom horizontal layer; retain general state/identity knowledge or move toward the platform/operator context. Two workflows are a diagnostic, not population proof. |
| **Accountability compresses** | Accepted workload grows, severe failures remain controlled, and human approval/repair/on-call hours per workload fall; direct staffing/role-scope evidence shows broader spans. | Weaken “more operational responsibility → more seats.” Assess whether the accessible ladder shrinks even as senior output rises. |
| **Automation quality transfers** | Fresh held-out sources, changed requirements and maintenance no longer undo measured agent gains at fixed all-in budget. | Shorten manual implementation specializations; strengthen automation operating skill. Do not infer whole-job p95 from one suite. |
| **Native automation remains brittle** | Repeated consequential exceptions survive current configuration and improved context, and independent operators still fund resolution rather than tolerate/avoid the task. | Strengthen the narrow domain/integration employment hypothesis. Technical residual without budget is insufficient. |
| **Research automation becomes economically convincing** | Independent revalidation and accepted changes, including failed experiments/compute/human review, show lower cost than a comparable human/assisted baseline on several tasks. | Do not reserve experiment design or performance work as human-only; adapt research training toward actual residual constraints. |
| **Demand expands enough to offset automation** | Comparable firms/sectors show accepted output, paid starts/hours and compensation growing after adoption, with acquisition/transfer and counterfactual issues addressed. | Strengthen paid-role opportunity; still separate firm market-share gains from economy-wide expansion and individual eligibility. |
| **Access is the real bottleneck** | Actual target roles require unavailable work rights, enrollment, field history or credentials; public projects do not change screening. | Redirect toward an adjacent eligible route or explicitly price the credential/relocation path. Do not spend another quarter polishing an ineligible signal. |
| **Learning does not transfer** | After a delay, unfamiliar diagnosis or explanation remains poor despite polished assisted artifacts. | Change practice/teaching, narrow scope and test retention; no inference of permanent atrophy or one universally best learning style. |
| **An exceptional frontier route opens** | A real eligible employer/program supplies needed access/resources and a bounded trial demonstrates fit better than the current path. | Increase frontier investment; selective exploration should be revisable rather than permanently conservative. |

### A practical commitment gate before a costly pivot

Use one 30–50-hour cycle as a planning assumption, with an initial 4–6-hour rights/runtime/oracle preflight. Select one accessible task and one comparator route, not all projects. Record assistance and conduct an unfamiliar transfer check. Preserve a local result that can be inspected by another engineer when one is independently available. At the next decision, compare demonstrated competence, a credible access path and foregone work/study value.

This does not validate 30 days as an optimal learning interval. It constrains downside while evidence is thin. If a target requires years of controls/training/production ownership, the exercise should reveal that distance rather than promise to traverse it. A five-year plan can be ambitious while keeping the initial commitment small and reversible.

## Missing evidence that still prevents ranking careers confidently

1. A representative task-share and labor-demand link for current agents: accepted output, prices, human hours, hiring, wages and new-firm entry together. No benchmark score supplies this bridge.
2. Prospective native/agent-only/human-assisted comparisons of integration, evaluations, incident handling and maintenance at matched correctness/severity and full cost. Current evidence covers selected slices.
3. Evidence about spans of control, junior learning ladders and accountable seats after automation, including displaced or non-hired workers. Senior productivity is not enough.
4. Comparative returns to these learning paths for employed India/UK developers, with actual prerequisites, costs, transfer and eligibility. Certificate visibility studies do not identify course enrollment returns.
5. A verified personal baseline: independent systems/ML/math skill, actual available hours, degree/work-right constraints, resources and work preferences. No private account or employer access is inferred or requested here.
6. Recurring customer demand and distribution economics for residual products after native response. Funded programs and product revenue establish existing work, not the new entrant's margin.
7. Updated raw capability-data reconciliation. The referenced `agent-capability-data-round7.md` was not present during this pass; this review uses the saved round6 audit and does not invent reconstructed horizons. An unavailable local note is not evidence public raw data do not exist.

The evidence supports preparing to do valuable work with changing tools. It does not support guaranteeing that the worker retains the same share of value, that today's residuals remain labor-intensive, or that any field guarantees leadership by 2031. Leadership evidence would be sustained accepted contributions, other people using and maintaining the work, and better technical/organizational decisions under real constraints.
