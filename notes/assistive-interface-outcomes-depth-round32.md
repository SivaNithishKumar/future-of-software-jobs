The methodological implication is narrower than the headline. Shared completion, user satisfaction, measured correctness and independence must stay separate. A selected trace cannot supply a denominator for all failures or retries. Human action time cannot supply unmeasured observation/monitoring time. A perceived speed item cannot replace an instrumented manual comparator. A nonsignificant returning-versus-new comparison cannot establish population equivalence or make the mixed, earlier-rating comparison randomized. The unchanged executor strengthens a module comparison but cannot remove order, self-selection, changed-site or exposure effects by itself.

The twelve audit rows preserve unresolved selection, disability/AT coverage, in-task help, correctness adjudication, exclusion/retry counts, rating aggregation, privacy/consent and long-term outcomes. In particular, installation help is not evidence of hidden in-task rescue. Missing consent text is not evidence that consent was absent. No native-AT-only comparator was established, so no replacement claim is justified. The paper's intervention-intensity denominator differs between Equation 3 and Table 3's caption; reuse requires clarification rather than silently choosing a formula. Predicting a user's intervention is also different from deciding whether intervention is necessary or the outcome is right.

Only a reporting/oracle refinement is proposed for **a11y-r15.task-editor**. Preserve its eight states, twenty transitions, twenty-four planned development cases and eight planned withheld variants. Add explicit offered/started/correct/wrong/abort/blocked/not-run statuses; all-attempt/retry accounting; actor/setup/help provenance; exact saved-state and focus/error checks; and separate active, waiting, monitoring, recovery and review time if later measured. An optional chosen trace may be a convenience view and must not replace the ledger. Synthetic replay creates neither a participant nor a satisfaction score.
