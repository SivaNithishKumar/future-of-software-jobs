# Public-service, legal and administrative software: attainable developer paths, round 5

Research cutoff and access date: **19 October 2026**. Scope: document ingestion/OCR, retrieval, citation checking, case workflow and decision support; funded requirements and evaluation quality. This is a bounded primary-source audit, not a census of employers or legal advice. It supplements the earlier Copilot survey and Lex institution map without repeating them. No private cases, accounts, outreach, applications, installations, software execution or model benchmarks were performed.

## The career conclusion

For an existing AI/backend engineer, the accessible work is converting messy documents into reviewable records, keeping versions and permissions correct, integrating case systems, measuring errors and operating reliable services. The evidence supports these engineering tasks today; it does not establish a five-year forecast of job numbers. Becoming the person who determines whether a legal argument applies to a real client is a different path, often requiring professional qualifications and practice experience.

**Inference for 2026–31:** expect more demand for engineers who can combine ordinary distributed systems with domain-specific error measurement. A general document chatbot faces competition from existing productivity suites and specialist legal platforms. A defensible entry project must identify an expensive, recurring failure in a particular workflow and show that it improves the whole reviewed task. Neither a retrieval leaderboard score nor a well-formatted answer establishes that.

## Public-service outcomes: preserve the denominator and comparison

**Consult is an actual used workflow, with narrow measured outcomes.** The 16 October 2025 government release reports sorting over 50,000 Independent Water Commission responses, two hours of computation, £240 processing cost and 22 expert checking hours; theme agreement F1 was 0.79/0.82 against two reviewer groups. The same release explicitly stopped further development of Redbox because secure LLM tools integrated into existing software deals had become available. That is concrete platform-bundling displacement, not just a hypothetical risk. [Official release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ground-breaking-use-of-ai-saves-taxpayers-money-and-delivers-greater-government-efficiency)

The Commission's methodology separates structured questionnaire responses, long-form submissions and campaigns. Policy experts analysed long-form submissions individually; campaign material was handled according to compatibility with the original questions. Therefore the headline is not evidence that AI replaced all substantive analysis or read 50,000 equally complex independent essays. Agreement with one of two human groups is also a different event from agreement with a fixed gold label. [Commission methodology, July 2025, pp. 4–7](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/687d4ecaa5561a5a7e726bd1/Independent_Water_Commission_-_Summary_of_Responses_to_the_Call_for_Evidence.pdf)

**Caddy supplies encouraging randomized workflow evidence, with limited outcome measurement.** The government's archived 13 March 2025 account describes invisible random assignment of help requests to Caddy or supervisors, over 1,000 support interactions over three months, and adviser surveys after calls. That supports studying an adviser-assistance workflow. Adviser confidence and self-reported resolution must not be relabelled independently adjudicated advice correctness or long-term client outcomes. The accessible primary account did not provide a complete preregistration, attrition table or cost model in this pass. [Archived official evaluation account](https://tnaqa.mirrorweb.com/ukgwa/20260902044136mp_/https%3A/ai.gov.uk/blogs/transforming-civic-engagement-with-caddy/)

**A caution about negative headlines:** Canada's Auditor General tested Charlie with only **six** tax questions in 2025; two answers were accurate. That is a small diagnostic test, not a representative estimate of millions of interactions. The current CRA page describes a GenAI chatbot, so old Charlie results cannot establish the current system's accuracy. [Auditor General report, October 2025, paras. 35–36](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/oag-bvg/audit-reports/documents/parl_oag_202510_01_e.pdf), [current CRA service description](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/contact-information/cra-chatbot.html).

The common inference is about measurement, not that government AI always works or fails. Measure processing, checking, correction, queue delay and downstream resolution separately. Human sign-off is a workflow requirement whose effectiveness and labor cost must be tested.

## Methods: what must be correct before the final answer is useful

| Layer | Strong simple comparator | Additional AI capability worth testing | Failure that a generic accuracy score hides |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ingestion and OCR | Original XML/HTML or embedded PDF text; fixed-form parser; conventional OCR | Layout/table extraction, noisy scan interpretation, document classification | One wrong date, negation, currency or table association; an invented but fluent transcription |
| Retrieval | Exact citation/identifier lookup; field filters; keyword/BM25 | Hybrid retrieval and reranking for varied wording | Relevant exception omitted; wrong document version; relevant source inaccessible to this user |
| Citation verification | Canonical identifier resolution and exact quoted-span matching | Claim-to-evidence entailment candidate scoring | A real citation supporting a different proposition, jurisdiction or time period |
| Rules and application | Explicit rule table/state machine where the rules are actually determinate | Missing-fact extraction, ambiguity flags, reviewer assistance | Source-grounded prose applies the wrong rule, misses an exception or assumes a missing fact |
| Case workflow | Queues, validated forms, deadlines, audit trails and role permissions | Suggested routing, summaries and draft checklists | Duplicate action, missed event, unauthorized disclosure, stale recommendation or reviewer overload |

This is an engineering decomposition, not a claim that every legal interpretation has a unique computable answer. In genuine legal application the qualified reviewer may need competing interpretations, authoritative updates and case facts which public documents cannot supply.

### Retrieval and groundedness benchmarks

**LegalBench-RAG** is explicitly an information-retrieval benchmark. It provides character-span gold evidence for complex contract questions, permitting deterministic precision/recall. Its code is MIT; its README separately requires agreement with ContractNLI, CUAD, MAUD and PrivacyQA usage policies. It also says LLMs participate in generation, so regenerating the corpus will not exactly reproduce the distributed benchmark. Freeze corpus bytes, offsets and revision; do not call a score a legal-reasoning or real-case safety result. Nicholas Pipitone and Ghita Houir Alami's original paper was submitted 19 August 2024. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10343), [current official code/readme](https://github.com/zeroentropy-ai/legalbenchrag).

The stronger independent commercial-tool study is Magesh et al., **accepted 14 March 2025**, with queries actually executed **March–May 2024**. It preregistered 202 queries and distinguishes correctness from misgrounding: actual cited sources can be misinterpreted or inapplicable. Its 17–33% historical rates must **not** be advertised as 2026 rates for current Westlaw/Lexis products. It did not evaluate the entire universe of drafting and review tasks; vendor access and intensive manual judging limited sample size. Half the dataset was reserved against memorization. Use the protocol and distinctions, not a timeless vendor ranking. [Published paper and measurement appendix](https://dho.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/Legal_RAG_Hallucinations.pdf)

**Source audit excluded from quantitative conclusions:** Das, Abualhaija and Bianculli's 14 August 2026 legal-RAG preprint reanalyses 317 existing benchmark outputs plus 142 expert questions. Only three of the original questions are jurisdiction/time-specific; code/materials are promised upon publication. More seriously, its definition makes strict hallucinations a subset of all hallucinations, but Table 1 sometimes reports strict rates greater than total rates—e.g. CIVIL BM25+GPT-5 answer-level 16.6% strict versus 8.3% total. This could be a table/label error, but remains unresolved in the inspected version. Exclude headline rates until clarified. [Version inspected](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14210v1)

**CitizenQuery-UK**, arXiv record submitted 3 February 2026, offers 22,066 synthetic citizen question/answer pairs from GOV.UK. Three annotators inspected the same stratified 100-pair sample twice; the evaluation uses automated atomic-fact judging and separately samples 100 pipeline outputs for human alignment. It is useful coverage scaffolding, not 22,066 independently expert-verified answers or evidence from a deployed service. The paper acknowledges answer freshness decays as policies change. Its HTML displays an August date despite the v1 February record, so preserve the version and retrieval date rather than inventing a unified publication date. Dataset/code licences and a stable project repository were not independently verified here. [Paper and methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04064v1)

### OCR and structured document engineering

Docling offers a useful contribution route: unified document representation, layout/reading order and table parsing, with MIT application code. Model licences remain separate. Its existence means a portfolio should test data contracts, failure localization or review ergonomics rather than rebuild a generic PDF converter. The project originated in IBM Research Zurich and is hosted by LF AI & Data. [Current code and licence statement](https://github.com/docling-project/docling), [technical paper, January 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17887).

OmniDocBench supplies module-level and end-to-end parsing evaluation; the current dataset README records annotation corrections and a 296-page hard addition on 9 April 2026. **Pin the version:** current 1,651-page data and older 1,355-page benchmark reports are not interchangeable. Repository code is Apache-2.0; a distinct unambiguous dataset licence was not located in the inspected card, so do not infer data rights from code rights. [Code](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench), [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/opendatalab/OmniDocBench/raw/main/README.md).

The May 2026 PureDocBench preprint proposes source-traceable HTML/CSS-generated pages with clean, digitally degraded and physically degraded variants, and challenges old annotation/contamination assumptions. Treat its reported comparative results as authors' benchmark evidence requiring reproduction, not an independent procurement recommendation. The method is valuable for independently known text/structure and matched distortions; synthetic rendering still cannot establish performance on unknown council archives. [Paper, 8 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07492)

## Permissions, state and access are product requirements

TNA's current Find Case Law guidance permits many reuses under the Open Justice Licence, but explicitly requires a separate free application for **computational analysis**. Its public API docs include bulk programmatic search to identify/extract/enrich contents in that category. Browsing access is not blanket permission to build a RAG corpus. No licence application was made. [Current application guidance](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/apply-for-a-licence), [public API conditions](https://nationalarchives.github.io/ds-find-caselaw-docs/public).

The TNA April 2025 FOI response describes notifications to licence holders when courts require judgments to be removed, plus feeds/APIs for corrected and newly published versions. That implies engineering for deletion and corrections across source storage, indexes, caches and generated artifacts; a one-time scrape cannot remain authoritative. [Official FOI response](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/freedom-of-information/requests/cas-252683/).

The legislation API is separately catalogued under OGL and exposes time-specific XML/RDF/HTML. That is an accessible research input, with attribution and source-specific exceptions still to record. [Official data catalogue](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/a2416481-271a-42b2-ace8-fc247dd251be/legislation-api). For any prototype maintain document licence, original URI, exact bytes/hash, publication/revision date, retrieved date, effective interval, supersession relation and permitted purpose. Retrieval should select by authorization and decision-time state **before** supplying text to a generator. Absence from a partial collection cannot establish absence of applicable law.

Legora's 11 September 2026 engineering account makes the infrastructure work concrete: project-scoped keyword/vector search, dormant matters, approved third parties, isolated tenants and customer-managed keys. It describes a migration from Elasticsearch through PostgreSQL/vector search to namespace-oriented Turbopuffer. These are the company's reported operating choices, not independent proof of product correctness or a universal architecture. [Engineering account by Erik Carlsson](https://legora.com/engineering/scaling-search-for-legal-ai)

Its separate evals account by Eirik Drage Steen describes versioned runtimes plus reproducible legal matters containing documents, permissions, databases and blob state, repeated trials, isolated environments and durable execution. This illustrates attainable systems/evals work. LLM judges and agent-driven optimization are used internally; their presence does not make the evaluation oracle independent. [11 September 2026 technical account](https://legora.com/engineering/building-scalable-evals-infrastructure-for-legal-agents)

## Three defensible opportunity hypotheses, with ways to disprove them

1. **Version-aware document quality is more defensible than generic chat.** Build auditable extraction/correction that survives source revisions and can explain every field to a reviewer. TNA's publication tools and correction feeds make this a real systems problem. Counterevidence: upstream XML and mature parsers can eliminate much of it; incumbents can bundle it. Falsify the business hypothesis if representative buyer documents are already structured, quality review takes negligible time, or the incumbent meets the same criteria within the existing contract.
2. **Evaluation infrastructure is reachable, but its oracle is the scarce asset.** Reproducible case state, permission tests, cost accounting and error taxonomies suit an AI/backend engineer. Counterevidence: vendors already build internal systems and may not buy standalone eval tooling; obtaining representative confidential matters and qualified adjudication can cost more than software. Falsify if only model-judged synthetic benchmarks are available, errors cannot be independently categorized or a domain reviewer cannot identify a decision the score changes.
3. **Administrative integration can beat higher model intelligence.** Validated queues, consistent records, controlled suggestions and correction workflows may remove the expensive bottleneck. Havering's retained custom system and Redbox's discontinuation support integration and distribution as constraints. Counterevidence: bespoke services can have weak margins, long procurement cycles and high support costs. Falsify if savings disappear after reviewer/migration/support time, no budget owner exists, or accessing the essential workflow requires an unavailable prime-vendor agreement.

These are hypotheses about attainable work, not investment advice or promises of a company opportunity.

## A safe 30-day proof: procurement-document amendment and evidence checker

**Output:** a local review tool that extracts a small record and shows exactly which document version supports it. Use invented procurement notices and amendments first; optionally add a small licensed public-notice sample restricted to organizational/procurement fields. Do not use private submissions, personal case histories, citizen eligibility or legal conclusions. A procurement example is chosen because field correctness and supersession can be specified independently.

**Days 1–7: define the oracle before a model sees anything.** Author a formal schema and independently written expected records for 40 notice families: identifier versus OCID, notice type, estimated versus awarded value, currency/VAT basis, supplier organization, deadline, status and evidence span. Create explicit amendments, cancellation, missing amount, conflicting documents and publication-after-cutoff cases. Keep a human-readable source rendering and structured truth separately. Have a second reviewer verify a stratified subset against the specification without seeing model outputs; report disagreements. Without a second reviewer, label it a single-author synthetic correctness exercise.

**Days 8–14: implement the hard baseline.** Parse machine-readable originals and templates with deterministic code; use keyword/field search for evidence. A state machine chooses the valid revision at a specified decision time. Distinguish a notice's publication timestamp, contract estimate and actual award. Do not use a later amendment while pretending to reproduce an earlier decision. Maintain a source manifest and immutable oracle. Start with Python standard library/SQLite or the developer's existing approved stack; new packages, OCR weights or APIs require installation/cost approval later.

**Days 15–21: test whether an AI increment helps.** Only after the baseline, compare optional model extraction on held-out layouts, prose amendments, degraded synthetic scans, tables and false premises. Reject unsupported fields rather than inventing them. Preserve page/box or character offsets and output validation. A model may suggest a change; deterministic workflow rules and a reviewer authorize acceptance. The prototype never files a procurement response or takes external action.

**Days 22–30: evaluate by source family, not random pages.** Hold out entire notice templates/buyer families and later amendments; keep duplicate content and clean/degraded variants in the same split. Compare field exact-match, catastrophic monetary/date/status errors, evidence precision/recall, missing-field recall, abstention/coverage, review minutes and cost per accepted record. Evaluate whether reviewers catch intentionally injected errors, not just whether they like the answer. A time-split must preserve publication/revision availability. Semantic similarity is not an acceptable oracle for a wrong currency or deadline.

**Predeclared kill criteria:** do not pursue the AI feature if deterministic parsing wins the same task at lower total cost, if any accepted output crosses synthetic tenant permissions, if critical mistakes remain concealed from the review UI, or if review-plus-correction time erases the gain. Report confidence intervals by independent document family; zero errors in a small sample does not certify a rare-error rate. No accuracy threshold authorizes live legal or public-service decisions.

## A 90-day extension and buyer validation

Extend only if the 30-day artifact exposes a recurring unresolved task. Add a held-out public-source collection with explicit permission and a later publication period, independent manual field annotations, cancellation/deletion/reindex tests and a synthetic multi-tenant permission matrix. Compare the final reviewed record against the original rules and bytes. Keep synthetic policy amendments explicitly fictional so readers cannot mistake them for current rules.

The plausible buyer is a procurement/case-operations lead, records/data-quality team or incumbent implementation team. The workflow questions are concrete: Who owns the accepted record? What currently costs time? Which errors change action? What system receives the record? Who corrects it, and how are amendments propagated? What procurement/framework or subcontract route can actually pay for it? Public procurement and process documents can establish an initial workflow map; they cannot confirm willingness to pay. Any later interviews or shadowing require separate authorized contact and synthetic/public demonstration material. None happened here.

For evaluation, use a fixed non-AI UI and AI-assisted UI with counterbalanced synthetic cases, independent blind adjudication, time accounting and delayed error checks. Repeated cases from the same person/source are correlated; report that instead of treating every field as a fresh independent trial. A small portfolio test cannot substantiate a population productivity claim. Include support, monitoring, extraction/OCR/API charges and reviewer costs in the same denominator:

`net workflow value = baseline reviewed cost − (AI processing + review + correction + integration/support allocation)`.

For a legally meaningful future experiment, a qualified domain reviewer must specify the application oracle and disputes before evaluation. If no such reviewer or legally usable data can be obtained, keep the claim at document engineering. Kill the commercial path if the domain owner cannot identify a recurring budgeted decision, if native functionality closes the gap, or if onboarding/access effort exceeds credible savings.

**Prerequisites:** Python/TypeScript, SQL, queues and idempotency, authorization, audit logs; basic IR and precision/recall; probability, sampling and uncertainty; document coordinates and schema validation. An ordinary laptop is sufficient for the synthetic corpus, parser, retrieval and review UI. GPU/model/API spending is optional and unapproved; model/weight licences must be checked separately. A law qualification is not needed to build this software demonstration, but the demonstration does not confer legal competence, civil-service eligibility, vetting clearance or a UK right to work.

## Boundaries and remaining uncertainty

The inspected evidence does not identify a representative India/UK legal-tech vacancy count, forecast occupational displacement, establish current vendor hallucination rates, or prove a startup procurement channel. Primary pages and employer indexes can be stale; board presence, job-body access and eligibility are separately labelled. Several Find a Tender and ai.gov.uk pages were blocked, so the report uses readable official notices, published PDFs and an explicitly identified government archive where available. Aggregator figures and unaudited vendor growth claims were excluded from conclusions. The proposed proofs are plans, not executed results.
