# Agricultural decision data: was the in-season information actually available?

Bounded source audit and proposed option, 20 October 2026; `/root/bio_science`. Existing sector, climate/water, geospatial and field-robotics notes already cover irrigation simulation, crop mapping and weed perception. This pass adds one narrower problem: **an as-issued evidence contract for a subnational crop-yield shortfall review queue**. It is an analyst-screening software exercise, not a farm-level yield model or an instruction to irrigate, fertilise, spray, trade or extend credit.

Primary papers, selected public code, data cards, professional profiles and official career pages were read. No crop table, raster, model, package, repository checkout or device data was acquired; no upstream code, crop model, forecast, native workflow, farm operation or account was run. Saved exact source texts are receipts, not an installed checkout. Six own standard-library date/set/status checks ran on invented examples, alongside plan/source-integrity checks. ## The specific gap, with native safeguards preserved

[CY-Bench, Kallenberg et al., 11 June 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-18-3997-2026), is an important positive baseline. Its task is maize/wheat yield **per administrative region and season**, not an individual farm. The paper uses lead-time truncation and whole-year holdouts, explains random-split leakage and explicitly acknowledges rolling-forward validation as a better operational approximation when sufficient data exist. It also describes static masks/calendars, missing management factors, complete-sample selection and uncertain reported-yield quality. Initial model results are illustrative; a separate follow-up comparison is outside this audit.

The checked code at **`4c2c58b70b0405b9a26d94ac2aeaa9712f044b2c`**, 22 May 2026, provides average-yield, trend, ridge, random-forest and neural baselines. [`run_benchmark.py:160–191`](https://github.com/WUR-AI/AgML-CY-Bench/blob/4c2c58b70b0405b9a26d94ac2aeaa9712f044b2c/cybench/runs/run_benchmark.py#L160-L191) deliberately trains on all other years for each held-out year. [`alignment.py:45–96,199–212`](https://github.com/WUR-AI/AgML-CY-Bench/blob/4c2c58b70b0405b9a26d94ac2aeaa9712f044b2c/cybench/datasets/alignment.py#L45-L96) computes crop-season cutoffs and filters observation dates. These are real protections. A changed forward-only/availability experiment answers another question; it must be reported alongside the official LOYO protocol, not presented as its reproduction or a discovered bug.

The native [`TrendModel:118–164`](https://github.com/WUR-AI/AgML-CY-Bench/blob/4c2c58b70b0405b9a26d94ac2aeaa9712f044b2c/cybench/models/trend_models.py#L118-L164) prefers earlier years when it can estimate a trend, then has explicit later-year/mean fallbacks. Do not claim every native trend prediction uses future labels. For a prospective experiment, restrict **all** fitting, preprocessing, calibration and label revisions to what was available by the issue time, including baseline fallbacks.

The unclosed operational question is whether observation dates, crop masks, retrospective statistics and training labels can be linked to their actual release/revision/receipt times. A date before harvest alone cannot answer that question. The selected downloader demonstration filters FPAR files by their nominal filename date; the inspected path does not establish historical delivery or consolidation-stage provenance. Exact archived CY-Bench input files were not accessible, so **this audit does not establish that CY-Bench actually used an unavailable CF product**.

## Why the FPAR version is consequential

[Seguini et al., 13 January 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-18-309-2026), distinguishes past-only NRT filtering from retrospective smoothing. It stores C0–C4 and a final CF estimate, with CF produced 90 days after C0. Data before July 2023 are explicitly NRT **hindcasts**, and historical long-term statistics support the filtering. Quality/status layers already describe sparse observations and processing. Its filtering evaluation compares stages with CF as a reference, not independently measured field yield; direct validation against ground observations is outside that paper's scope.

The product therefore already contains useful revision and quality concepts. A proposed adapter should consume those rather than invent a missing quality system. It still needs an actual manifest tying the selected stage, nominal period, production/release time, revision and local receipt to each decision. A hindcast can be useful scientific evidence without proving that the data were delivered to a particular analyst then. CF may be a legitimate later reference; it becomes inappropriate only when a claimed earlier decision uses it before availability. That distinction must be checked, not assumed.

The [pinned FPAR data card](https://github.com/WUR-AI/AgML-CY-Bench/blob/4c2c58b70b0405b9a26d94ac2aeaa9712f044b2c/data_preparation/global_fpar_500m/README.md) explicitly documents consolidation and CF delay. Some operational notes in that card are dated 2023/2024; they are not a fresh 2026 delivery guarantee. The [JRC dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.2905/1aac79d8-0d68-4f1c-a40f-b6e362264e50) and release directory were inaccessible to this reader. Current stage/receipt history remains a launch gate, not a claim that the producer lacks it.

`maize_IN` is configured in the checked code. The [India card](https://github.com/WUR-AI/AgML-CY-Bench/blob/4c2c58b70b0405b9a26d94ac2aeaa9712f044b2c/data_preparation/crop_statistics_IN/README.md) describes ICRISAT district statistics for 1990–2017, 2015 district boundaries, yield in kg/ha and a CC-BY-4.0 source claim. Its publication date is unspecified; the original portal timed out. Exact release bytes, upstream terms, boundary changes and conversion to the benchmark's target units must be verified before reuse. This is historical district data, not a current-season field truth set.

Hold out entire seasons and spatial groups appropriate to the intended deployment. A forward-season test for known districts is different from transfer to unseen districts; neither establishes new-farm performance. Shared weather grids, neighbouring regions, repeated parcels, changing boundaries and seasonal aggregation can create dependence. A district label cannot be assigned as each field's independent yield label. If field identities/management/outcomes are absent, field-level validation remains unavailable. Published statistical yield is a measurement process, not a perfect oracle for a private farm.

## One equipment-light 30/90-day proof, conditional and unrun

Project `agriculture25.project.as-issued-shortfall` keeps one crop, one declared region-season unit and one review decision. The first artifact is a metadata/reference ledger using an existing approved CPU/text stack. No new board, sensor, imagery or model is required for that bounded mechanism. Actual hours, skill, reviewer, operating data and approved runtime are unknown.

**First 30–50 focused hours**, an unvalidated planning allowance: 8–12 to bind crop/calendar/unit/reference/rights semantics; 8–12 to write owned date/revision/status examples; 8–14 to build the versioned eligibility/decision ledger; 6–12 for independent oracle/prior-art review. This is one selected probe, not an additional geospatial/irrigation curriculum. Eight exposed seeds cover late CF versus eligible C0; missing receipt time; an earlier-season yield label released after the forecast; a later correction to an older label; LOYO versus forward-only labels; a boundary mismatch; aggregate-to-field substitution; and missing/abstained decisions. They are invented, not cropped from real farmers.

For example, a fictional C0 is issued 12 July and its fictional CF on 10 October, 90 days later. At a 15 July decision, a nominal 5 July date cannot make the CF eligible. These dates are owned illustration inputs, not observed JRC latency. Require declared producer release **and** local receipt where the claim needs actual operational availability. Missing evidence stays `AVAILABILITY_UNVERIFIED`; an assumed lag is a labelled sensitivity scenario, not a recovered historical fact.

Before any fresh assessment, an independent setter must prepare expected eligibility, revision, unit/group and decision-status tables. Use a separately authored as-of query/reference path and manually reviewed counterexamples. The learner and actor must not see final keys. Proposed development/fresh packs are 20 configurations each, grouped by season, spatial identity and availability policy; no hidden pack exists now. Author-seen seeds are regression practice. Seed a genuinely unavailable CF, late label, incompatible unit and wrong field inference so the oracle rejects those errors; retain eligible examples so reject-all fails useful coverage. A model judge or the ledger's own success flag is not the oracle.

**Next conditional additional 90–150 hours** uses the production path's existing 10–20/40–60/20–35/20–35 blocks, with setup, oracle and support work inside. First decide whether exact rights, an approved compatible native environment and a genuinely small permitted table/manifest can be secured; do not default to the multi-gigabyte release. Then compare the unchanged native LOYO result/protocol with a separately labelled forward/as-available variant on the same chosen unit. Average/persistence/trend and ridge are strong baselines; expensive neural models are optional. Add spatial transfer only if it is the actual question and sample size supports it. Record all missing/late opportunities and one regime where the more complex approach loses. End with independent replay and a continue/narrow/remove decision. Without lawful small data, independent keys or native access, retain the source/fixture proof and mark those arms `NOT_RUN`.

Report eligibility/status agreement; forbidden future/revised inputs; offered versus usable forecast coverage; grouped yield error and bias; uncertainty/interval coverage by lead time and stratum where estimates are justified; false/late reviews, abstentions and full setup/retry/compute/storage/review/support cost. Fit normalization, trend, calibration and thresholds only inside the permitted training history. Repeated rows are not independent seasons, and an interval for mean error is not predictive coverage. Selected tiny fixtures establish no extreme-event rate.

Decision loss is initially a **declared synthetic screening loss** over false alerts, missed shortfalls and review effort. A point forecast is not automatically a calibrated shortfall probability. Review does not prevent a crop loss merely because the model escalates it. Do not turn a retrospective RMSE gain or hypothetical loss table into yield, input savings, farmer income, insurance/default benefit or ROI. Actual decision value needs an authorised analyst/agronomist, real opportunity/capacity/cost information and an appropriate prospective study; a full growing season may exceed 90 calendar days.

Stop or narrow when native metadata/baselines already meet the contract; no actual availability history or independent oracle can be obtained; improvement depends on future-year labels/retrospective products; field claims use aggregate labels; missing seasons/regions are silently removed; rights/setup exceed the selected allowance; or claimed economics need unavailable field authority. Native wins can leave useful documentation, schema, regression and maintenance contributions. A current unmet upstream gap and independent review are needed before calling one original or accepted.
