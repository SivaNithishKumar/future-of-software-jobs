# Serving economics at a fixed quality and latency target

This is a bounded systems-methods contribution, not a GPU purchase recommendation or final career conclusion. The newly audited result is the published [DistServe OSDI24 proceedings paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi24-zhong-yinmin.pdf). It is an older paper absent from the bounded serving-note census, not evidence of the latest model frontier. The question was fixed before retrieval.

Its useful contribution is to treat serving capacity as requests supported at a latency-attainment target per provisioned GPU, rather than raw tokens per second. Separating prefill and decoding permits different allocation and parallelism choices. The companion JSON retains the exact workload, hardware, SLO and result scope. Timing capacity is still a narrower endpoint than useful complete work: it does not establish independently correct answers, zero failed deadlines or business value.

The empirical envelope matters. Dataset text and token lengths are paired with synthetic arrivals, not observed production arrival timestamps. Selected main-system results and simulated placement ablations are separate evidence. The paper’s own small-resource and throughput-focused countercases are positive guidance: a simpler colocated system may be the better choice. A historical speedup cannot establish a universal advantage or today’s native-runtime gap.

There is a strong native countercase. [vLLM’s current official documentation](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/disagg_prefill/) offers experimental disaggregated prefilling, separate latency tuning and connector integration. It explicitly distinguishes that purpose from improving throughput. This is documentation of a feature and a technical contribution interface, not measured parity, production readiness on a particular cluster or an immutable engine build. The [DistServe repository](https://github.com/LLMServe/DistServe) exposes implementation/evaluation routes and an Apache2.0 licence label, while its README requires multiple GPUs and another backend. No licence body, dependency grant, paper-matched revision or contribution-policy body was followed.

## What the timing claim leaves open

A complete comparison needs the arrival clock, not only the time after admission. Keep intended offered requests, queueing, admission, completion, late output, cancellation, timeout, malformed output and unknown state visible. Do not silently move a request outside the denominator after it becomes inconvenient. Run and drain duration, warmup, compile state, cache reuse, load variability and adequate tail-sample coverage also change the claim. These were not fully recovered from the selected paper and landing pages; that is not an accusation that the implementation hid failures.

A per-request average token time can hide individual streaming stalls. Separate phase percentiles do not by themselves establish a joint per-request deadline: two90%phase attainments can have disjoint failures. The tiny arithmetic illustration in the JSON shows only that mathematical distinction, not a discovered error in DistServe. A business end-to-end deadline and an independently accepted answer are additional endpoints. Neither a timing run on code prompts nor the use of the same model family establishes functional code correctness.

Fixed model and precision make a scheduling comparison more informative, but the model/tokenizer revision, prompt/output distribution, sampling, stopping, backend and cache state must still match. Truncated context support must stay in the claim. Native serving stacks and their parallelism/features have changed since the paper; the historical baseline description cannot be carried forward as a current constraint. The selected paper’s placement/simulation comparison helps examine mechanism, without proving that every backend difference or future workload is controlled.

Low measured aggregate transfer share also does not establish negligible network risk everywhere. Placement can exploit an especially fast local link; longer contexts, different topology, stage imbalance and faults change the envelope. Repeated weight copies, idle stage capacity and redundancy must remain inside resource accounting. The paper’s fault/preemption limitations should not be replaced by a production-reliability claim.

## The cost bridge

At an equal hardware cost and fixed workload/acceptance, more capacity per allocated GPU can reduce the GPU-capacity component of cost. Inverse capacity alone is not full cost per accepted customer outcome. A consistent bill needs a leased **or** amortized-owned basis, utilisation/idle reserve, host and network resources, included versus separately charged power/storage, setup, maintenance and operating/review effort. Avoid counting components twice when a rental price already includes them. Keep failures and quality rejection in the useful-output denominator. No such complete bill or buyer-return result was established here.

This distinction is useful preparation because a kernel or scheduler win can disappear at the application boundary. It does not establish a durable specialist moat, headcount growth, a paid budget or a reason to acquire hardware. Mature native automation can be the right answer.
