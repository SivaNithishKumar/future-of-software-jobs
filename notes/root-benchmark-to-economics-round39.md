## Source observations

[GDPval v1, 5 October 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04374v1) evaluates 1,320 tasks across 44 occupations, with 220 public gold tasks. Its GPT-5 cost table reports 90× naive speed and 474× naive cost ratios, versus 1.12× and 1.18× after one review/fallback attempt. Appendix A.6 defines ordinal agreement as `1−|H−A|`, using ratings 0, 0.5 and 1. The reported 65.7% model–human and 70.8% human–human figures exclude twelve tasks and invalid automated outputs. These are not exact classification accuracies. Appendix A.2.1 gives 404 minutes for average human completion, whereas A.4 Table 3 gives 9.49 hours; their reconciliation remains unknown here. The review/fallback calculation is a proxy, and the paper acknowledges missing human-deliverable review and catastrophic-error costs. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04374v1#A2.SS1).

[SWE-Lancer v4, 29 May 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12115v4) draws 1,488 tasks from one Expensify/Upwork setting. Its 764 implementation and 724 proposal-selection tasks share a million-dollar historical payout total; benchmark earnings are score mappings, not observed payments to autonomous agents. Management is agreement with the original choice, supported by an additional human validation campaign. Section 3.6's model/freelancer fallback calculation assumes quick verification, while noting that verification can exceed 24 hours. Section 4 retains repository/platform concentration, limited clarification and possible exposure of public 2023–24 tasks. Infrastructure work is underrepresented. These limits do not erase its more realistic full-stack tasks and professional end-to-end tests. [Methods and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12115v4#S4).

The [official GDPval introduction](https://openai.com/index/gdpval/) separately says the evaluation is one-shot and omits interactive context building and revision. Its selected occupations and constructed assignments do not supply an employment panel. The paper and introduction are not independent replications. ## Our distinct tests and unresolved questions

The owned stdlib program `benchmark-economics-demo-round39.py` performs seven exposed arithmetic checks, using invented assumptions and exact fractions. It does not recalculate either benchmark. Its expected answers and implementation share an author; independent rechecking is separate.

* With a hypothetical 100-minute human task, one-minute model attempt and one-fifth operational acceptance, a one-minute review yields 82 expected minutes. A thirty-minute review yields 111. This is a counterexample to inferring end-to-end savings from cheap inference alone, not an empirical estimate of either benchmark or the reader's work.
* Two different ratings can earn half credit under an ordinal distance measure while never being exactly equal. A percentage must therefore retain its scoring rule, comparator, exclusions and unit. This does not show that expert preference is useless or that an automated grader cannot improve.
* Across hypothetical tasks, averaging each task's fallback time can differ from multiplying pooled mean time by pooled failure probability. Our two-task witness gives 5 versus 50 minutes. That mathematical distinction does not establish that GDPval implemented an incorrect estimator: the actual paired data, code and summary-table reconciliation have not been checked.
* When half of hypothetical tasks always fail and half always succeed, retrying the same procedure three times still succeeds on half. Treating the pooled mean as independent identical retries projects seven-eighths instead. This is a limiting witness, not a measured diagnosis of either study. Repairing context or using a different method could change the distribution.

An output can lose a pairwise preference comparison and still satisfy a legitimate operating contract; a preferred output can also violate a hard requirement. Our research must retain both preference and task-specific acceptance. A hidden benchmark test is stronger than a patch's self-report, but it is not evidence of operating authorization, live payment, maintenance cost or personal eligibility.

## Action within an existing project

Use one already selected workflow or software-reliability artifact rather than starting a new career track. During its first-stage window, record model/tool effort, human reading, diagnosis, repair, verification, setup and waiting time separately. Retain every attempted, failed, abandoned and incomplete case. Keep the same legitimate task contract and competent native/deterministic baseline. If no approved runnable arm or independent expected outcome exists, produce a design or arithmetic result and label it accordingly.

Before the 90-day extension, define hard acceptance requirements separately from blinded preferences, preserve the task-paired denominator and test whether retries repair failures or repeat them. A blinded comparison requires an unexposed independent setter/reviewer; these have not been recruited. Account for reviewer learning and order effects, do not reuse exposed answers as unseen validation, and put limits around rare consequential failures rather than manufacturing a monetized expected-loss estimate.

Continue a custom layer only if it improves the chosen contract under the same measured resources or answers a distinct useful question. Prefer the existing baseline when it meets that contract. This is a proposed measurement discipline within existing hours, not another allocated project, a superiority claim, a job forecast or evidence that evaluation itself remains a scarce human skill.

The career implication is conditional: systems, experimental design and domain-specific acceptance knowledge are useful preparation for doing this work.
