## Findings that are changing the picture

**1. AI changes engineering unevenly.** Company field experiments, mostly conducted in 2022–2024, report developer gains, while a small randomized study of experienced maintainers found a slowdown with early-2025 tools. These are different people, tools and tasks. Neither supports “all developers become dramatically faster” or “AI cannot help experienced engineers.” Neither measures five-year employment. [Company experiments](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535), [maintainer study](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09089v2).

**2. Moving into research is not an automatic escape from automation.** RE-Bench demonstrates strong AI research-engineering results, including a kernel solution beating all nine human attempts on that task. But its seven selected environments, 2024 models and normalized scores do not measure a whole research job. Its human comparison includes 71 attempts by 61 selected people, who could use LLMs. The 32-hour comparison selects among attempts; it is not continuous long-term research. Reported API-versus-human costs exclude the H100 environment. Both the automation success and those boundaries matter. [Exact paper version](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15114v2).

**3. Integration work has real commercial settings, while platforms already automate much of it.** Palantir’s filed results and a saved forward-deployed engineering role show platform commerce and customer-facing engineers coexisting. Its platform already includes workflow, agent and evaluation capabilities. This weakens both “integration is just demos” and “any custom integration layer will be valuable.” Company revenue does not identify AI’s causal contribution or future developer demand. The role also has education, experience, travel and location conditions; it is not a freshly reconfirmed opportunity for you. [Filed results](https://investors.palantir.com/files/2026%20Q2%20PLTR%2010-Q.pdf), [role description](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/5168e8fd-fec1-4fea-b7a1-81bdaea65850).

**4. Robotics transfer is real; deployment portability remains a technical problem.** Open X-Embodiment reports 3,600 physical evaluation trials across six robots. Its highlighted cross-robot skill transfer targets a robot already represented in training, rather than an unseen embodiment. A common seven-dimensional action format can still encode different frames, positions or velocities. Some comparisons lose to per-robot baselines. That creates concrete questions about representation, calibration and evaluation—not a licence to assume a generic robotics startup or guaranteed job boom. [Exact methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08864v9).

**5. “AI engineer” hides very different jobs.** Saved Mistral evaluation-platform requirements emphasize production software and GPU/distributed infrastructure; previous benchmark design is explicitly unnecessary. A Cohere senior evaluation role emphasizes methods, data and research track record. A small portfolio can demonstrate a component, but cannot substitute automatically for years of experience, employer-assessed equivalence or senior research ownership. These indexed requirement snapshots are not verified current vacancies. [Mistral](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/mistral.ai/ff382d26-285e-499d-973f-3d789d995ff9), [Cohere](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/cohere/cb5d588c-5637-423a-968b-bf637ee2caf9/).
